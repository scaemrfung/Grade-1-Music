For the adult in the room

How to teach from this site

Open the week. Each week runs over three 30-minute classes: you open with a devotional song (about 5 minutes), then teach about 20 minutes of music from the “This week in 3 classes” plan, leaving a few minutes for transitions. Project the page so the children can see the songs and their jobs.

Three 30-minute classes a week

  1. Devotional song 5 minutes — yours to lead; not part of the music plan.
  2. Music 20 minutes — Weeks 1–19 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Later months use the same approach as each month is rewritten.
  3. Day 1 prepares. Day 2 practises. Day 3 transfers the idea to a new song, game or instrument, with rotating 10-second checks (about 7 children, Y/N) — stamp the week here. Short week: 2 classes → drop Day 3; 1 class → Day 1 only.
  4. Each class has a “Bring” line: the portable instruments to carry into the homeroom that day. Handing them out and collecting them takes about a minute and is counted inside the 20. Activities from Week 5 fit a carpet circle or standing beside desks; Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (with a short homeroom note).

How to use the Studio

The Practice studio supports your teaching on the classroom display. You lead; the screen helps.

Before class

Open the week’s lesson page on the classroom display. The “Use in class” links in each class tab open the tool already set up for that day’s song. Open it before the children arrive. Nothing plays until you tap, so it is safe during the devotional song.

During the 20 minutes

  1. Warm up (2–3 min). Pitch pipe or the keyboard for the starting note, then So–mi–la echo or Hello, name!
  2. Main song (5–8 min). Singalong (bouncing ball) or Copycat songs, one phrase at a time, with so–mi labels on for hand-sign work.
  3. Skill game (5–8 min). Pick one for the week’s skill:
    • Beat: Steady beat, Find the beat, Heartbeat, or Count the hops.
    • Rhythm: Drum echo, Rhythm puzzle, or Find the rest.
    • High and low: Melody maze, High or low?, or Solfège staircase.
    • Loud and quiet, fast and slow: Dynamics slider or Conductor.
  4. Play instruments (5 min). Hand out the portable instruments, then Song + ostinato (half sing, half play) or Beanbag pass.

Tips

  • You lead and the screen supports. Sing it first, then use the tool to reinforce.
  • Use whole-class response modes (stand or sit, hand signs) so every child answers.
  • Concert weeks: Concert program and Concert manners.
  • Sub days: point the substitute to the “Use in class” links in each Day tab.

If you have the GAMEPLAN book

Keep using it. This site is a companion, not a replacement. GAMEPLAN Grade 1 (Kriske & DeLelles, Kid Sounds) is copyrighted. We follow its public year — pulse, ta/rest, ti-ti, so–mi–la, mallets, form, timbre — and we score the folk songs anyone may write down.

  • Use your charts, rhythm cards, three-line staff disks, and seating template.
  • Use their original songs, picture-book lessons, and Orff arrangements from the book only.
  • The 36 weeks follow the book’s month-by-month order: September voices, pulse and high/low; October sound, silence and timbre; through May compositions, performance and the picture check. Each week has a teal GAMEPLAN note and a “GAMEPLAN book” line (month, week, concept, and a slot for the book’s lesson and page) in every Day tab, so you can open your copy to the matching lesson.
  • GAMEPLAN lessons are about 60 minutes. Ours are about 60 minutes of music a week too, split into three 20-minute music blocks. If you want a book activity, swap it into Day 2 or Day 3.
  • Book lesson numbers and page references for September–January are filled from the GAMEPLAN contents (pp.). Later months get page numbers as each month is rewritten. No book text is reproduced here.

The quiet hand

Hand up = sound off. Practise it the first day. Do not shout over them.

Singing voice

Grade 1 needs a higher starting pitch than an adult speaking voice. Praise a light tone, never the loudest child. Mouthing along still counts in September.

Instruments

Rest position before the first sound. You pass them out. They do not help themselves. Fewer instruments is better than a riot.
