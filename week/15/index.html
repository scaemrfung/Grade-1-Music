Week 15 · Introduction, Phrase & Loud/Quiet · Jan 4–8

High-Low Phrases and Holiday Songs

Teddy Bear becomes a high-low chart, we learn La Piñata with body percussion, build song rhythms with sticks, and read holiday patterns while we sing seasonal songs.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Jan 4–8

Full week: every Grade 1 class gets all 3 classes.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Weeks 1–15 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Later months use the same approach as each month is rewritten. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Day 1 · Prepare

Bring: Hand drum, board or chart for Teddy Bear high/low

GAMEPLAN book, Day 1: p. 44

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi. One short phrase-echo clap pattern (book p. 38 idea).
  3. 5 minTeddy Bear with chart (book p. 44) — Review Teddy Bear (on this site). Show a simple high/low chart for “Teddy bear” (two highs, one low). Sing “high, high, low” in place of those words. Class invents new body pairs (shoulders/knees, waist/toes). Perform sing / think / sing.
  4. 6 minPhrase rainbows — Each Teddy Bear line gets one rainbow arm. How many phrases?
  5. 5 minSame-different-same — Sing, think with actions, sing again.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — High-high-low tip-toe to the door.

Use in class

  • 🐱Copycat songsTeddy Bear chart: high-high-low.
  • 🐦High or low?Phrase rainbows: one rainbow per line.

Day 2 · Practice

Bring: Popsicle sticks (about 12 per pair) or paper strips, Polar Bear book if you have it

GAMEPLAN book, Day 2: p. 45

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi.
  3. 5 minLa Piñata (book p. 45) — Teach from the book (Mexican folk; title and page only). Short talk about the piñata tradition. Echo Spanish text by two-measure phrases using the book’s pronunciation guide in class (not copied here). Add clap/pat body percussion from the book’s idea. Partners may invent a gentle hand game. Homeroom: circle on carpet; keep hand games low.
  4. 6 minPopsicle Stick Game (book p. 45; see also p. 36) — Pairs build rhythms from familiar titles: Teddy bear; Dale dale dale; Dashing through the snow; Up on the housetop. Use one stick = ta, two short sounds on one beat (beam), and a rest. Speak and clap. Homeroom: sticks on desks.
  5. 5 minRead-aloud · Polar Bear, Polar Bear — Use the book guide. One phrase per animal turn.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Clap one stick row once, then walk.

Use in class

  • 👏Body percussionLa Piñata: clap and pat on the pulse.
  • 🔢Rhythm makerPopsicle Stick Game: build a holiday row.

Music time: 20 min · plus 5 min devotional

Day 3 · Transfer

Bring: Holiday pattern chart or board, shakers or jingle bells, optional Jingle Bells instrumental recording

GAMEPLAN book, Day 3: p. 46

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi.
  3. 5 minOh What Fun! (book p. 46) — Show four holiday rhythm patterns from the book chart (title and page only; draw simple ta / two-short / rest rows yourself). Speak with rhythm syllables you already use; review the repeat sign. Point to numbers while the class claps quietly (two-finger clap). Optional: clap along with an instrumental Jingle Bells while you change patterns.
  4. 5 minTraditional holiday songs (book p. 46) + 10-second checks — Build seasonal repertoire by title only (for example Jingle Bells chorus on this site; Up on the Housetop; other secular seasonal songs your school uses). Keep it light and inclusive — offer a non-holiday winter song if a family prefers. Rotating check, group C (about 7 children): while the game runs, give each child a 10-second solo: clap one four-beat holiday row (ta, two-shorts, rest) and freeze. Mark Y or N on the record sheet.
  5. 6 minPattern + phrase checks — Checked children clap one Oh What Fun row and draw one phrase rainbow for Teddy Bear or La Piñata.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Jingle the pulse to the door.

Use in class

  • 🐱Copycat songsHoliday songs: Jingle Bells chorus.
  • 🔢Rhythm makerOh What Fun: clap a pointed pattern.

Story suggestion

Polar Bear, Polar Bear, What Do You Hear? · Bill Martin Jr. (illus. Eric Carle)

Call-and-answer animal sounds are phrase-length turns — the same feeling as one musical sentence, then another.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (The World of Eric Carle on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, after Teddy Bear high/low (about 5 min).

Before reading

  • “Each animal gets one turn — that turn is like one phrase.”
  • Draw one rainbow in the air for a practice phrase.

During reading

  • On each call, draw a rainbow; on each answer, draw another.
  • Keep voices light; freeze between animals.

After reading

Count how many rainbows you drew.

Quick check: “How many animal turns (phrases) did we hear?”

Adapt: Whisper the answers if the room is lively.

Extend: Pairs invent one call and one answer and draw two rainbows.

Activity · Same-different-same

Sing, think with actions, sing again.

Close

High-high-low tip-toe to the door.

Teacher notes

  • GAMEPLAN December Week Three (pp. 44–46). La Piñata and Oh What Fun chart by title and page only; no book notation copied. Teddy Bear and Jingle Bells chorus are on this site.
  • Two sounds on one beat appear in stick rows — feel and clap them; formal ti-ti naming is Week 21.
  • Inclusive repertoire: secular seasonal songs; respect families who opt out of holiday material.
  • Sticks: one small set per pair is enough for a homeroom.

Watch-outs

  • Copying book notation onto the board from memory.
  • Stick fights.
  • Holiday songs that run long and skip the reading game.

Challenge

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Teddy Bear

Traditional action song · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Turn around, touch the ground, show your shoe, that will do — one action per phrase.

Actions stay small so the tune survives.

Jingle Bells (chorus, first phrase)

James Lord Pierpont, 1857 · public domain · do–re–mi–so · 4/4

mi = E (do = C)

Game: Jingle shakers or bells on the pulse while half the class sings.

A winter song, not a religious one. The chorus only.
