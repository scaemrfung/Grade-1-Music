Ti-ti

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Class 1 introduces, Class 2 practises, Class 3 applies and checks. Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Class 1 · Introduce

Bring: No instruments (voices and body percussion)

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 4 minSlow ta, quick ti-ti — Step ta in place beside desks, then two quick toe taps for ti-ti on the same beat speed (finger taps work too).
  3. 6 minRead-aloud · Tanka Tanka Skunk! — Hook for Teach, per the book guide. Clap the drumming words.
  4. 5 minTi-ti shares the beat — One ta is a sandwich, ti-ti is two halves on the same plate.
  5. 5 minDraw two little notes together — Two sticks with a beam, matching week 14’s icon style.

Class 2 · Practise

Bring: Shakers, woodblocks

  2. 3 minSlow ta, quick ti-ti — Warm up.
  3. 6 minPease Porridge Hot — Clap the words. Find the quick pairs and say them as ti-ti. Keep the beat in the feet.
  4. 1 minInstruments out & back — Hand out woodblocks and shakers in rest position; collect them the same way at the end.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Slow ta, quick ti-ti

Step ta. Jog in place ti-ti on the same beat speed — two little steps in the time of one. Hold a chair if jogging is too much; two finger taps instead.

Teach

Ti-ti shares the beat

One ta = one sandwich. Ti-ti = two halves. They take the same plate (the beat).

Draw two little notes together

Two sticks with a beam, or two dots with a line. Stay consistent with week 14’s icon style.

Pease Porridge Hot

Clap the words. Find the quick pairs. Say them as ti-ti. Keep the beat in the feet.

Read mixed lines

ta ta ti-ti ta. Point. If they say ti-ti too slowly, it becomes two tas — fix it kindly with a model.

Story suggestion

Tanka Tanka Skunk! · Steve Webb

Two-syllable names (Tan-ka) vs one-syllable (Skunk) map cleanly to ti-ti and ta.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Mr. Simon Music on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Hook for Teach (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “Say the animal names. Do they have one sound or two sounds on a beat?”
  • Review: one finger = ta, two fingers = ti-ti.

During reading

  • Chant “Tan-ka Tan-ka Skunk” with the beat on laps. Tanka is ti-ti and Skunk is ta. Rest on the next beat: ti-ti ti-ti ta sh.
  • For each animal page, say the name and show one or two fingers: skunk, fox, yak, ox = ta; tiger, cheetah, llama, zebra, panda = ti-ti.
  • Longer names like kangaroo and caterpillar: just enjoy them. No need to notate.
  • Play the Tanka chant on a drum and the Skunk beat on a shaker.

After reading

Build a bar: fill four beat boxes with animal names from the book (e.g., “tiger, skunk, zebra, fox”). Clap and read them as ta and ti-ti.

Quick check: Teacher says “skunk” or “tiger.” Students hold up one finger (ta) or two (ti-ti).

Adapt: Show animal pictures with dots underneath (one dot or two). Wigglers can step ta and jog ti-ti.

Extend: Children invent their own four-beat animal parade and play it on drums.

Activity · Build a bar

Four beat boxes on paper. Fill each with ta or ti-ti. Clap. Trade papers.

Close

Ear quiz: teacher claps ta or ti-ti. Students hold one finger or two. Stamp if most are right.

Materials

  • Board: ta vs ti-ti
  • Chants with pairs of shorts (Pease Porridge Hot)

Teacher notes

  • The error will be ti-ti as two full beats. Use the sandwich plate picture again.
  • No rest yet unless they ask. “Empty box” is next week.

Watch-outs

  • Writing ti-ti as two tas in two boxes.

Challenge

Make a pattern that uses both ta and ti-ti and teach it to your row.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Pease Porridge Hot

English nursery rhyme · public domain · rhythm chant (ta and rest) · 2/4

Spoken. Add so–mi later if you wish.

Game: Partner clapping on the tas; open hands on the rest.

The rest after hot and cold is the whole point. Do not skip it.

Acka Backa

Traditional playground rhyme · public domain · so–mi chant · 2/4

Spoken or so–mi

Game: Passing a beanbag on the beat. The child on “boo” is next leader — kindly, no out-shame.

Pulse first. Rhythm of the words later.
