Melody Goes Up and Down

A melody is a line. It can go up, down, or stay. We draw the line as we sing.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Finger paint the air

Teacher sings a slow so–mi–so. Students draw it in the air with one finger. Then a line that only goes up. Then only down.

Teach

Contour means the shape

We do not need note names to see the hill. Draw three maps on the board: hill, slide down, flat road.

Map a known song

See-Saw is a little up-down. Draw it together. Rain, Rain too.

Same or different shape

Sing two patterns. Thumbs up if the maps would look the same.

Stay means repeats

Ta ta on the same pitch is a flat line. That is still a melody.

Story suggestion

Little Melba and Her Big Trombone · Katheryn Russell-Brown (illus. Frank Morrison)

Melba’s trombone melodies rise, fall, and soar—melodic contour in a musician’s story.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Miami Children’s Museum on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Hook for Teach (about 7 min). The book is long, so read up to Melba’s radio solo and save the rest for another day.

Before reading

  • “Listen for the sound words. Does each one go up, down, or stay the same?”
  • Finger ready to draw in the air.

During reading

  • On the sound-word page (the plink of a guitar, the hum of a bass, the thrum of a drum, the ping-pang of a piano, the tremble of a horn), say each word with a voice shape and trace it in the air.
  • When Melba gets her trombone, slide your arm like the trombone slide while your voice slides up and down. That smooth line is contour.
  • Her first try sounds like a howling dog: a wobbly line. After practice, a simple tune: a smooth hill. Draw both on the board.
  • Ask: “Did that go up, down, or stay?”

After reading

Draw the tune: students listen three times to a short pattern and draw one contour line. Check with a neighbour.

Quick check: Hold up hill, slide, and flat-road cards. “Which map matches what I sang?”

Adapt: English learners can use arm moves only. Children who need movement can walk a contour line on the floor.

Extend: Play 20 seconds of a Melba Liston recording. Trace the trombone’s line in the air.

Activity · Draw the tune

Students listen three times to a short pattern and draw one contour line. Share with a neighbour: did your hills match?

Close

Hold up two student maps. Class sings them as best they can. Stamp if drawings show direction.

Materials

  • Paper for contour maps
  • A known so–mi song
  • Yarn optional

Teacher notes

  • Accept wobbly lines. Direction is the outcome, not art.
  • Do not require staff paper.

Watch-outs

  • Asking them to write note names on the staff today.

Challenge

Sing a friend’s map without being told the words.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

See-Saw

Traditional · public domain (also sung as Saw, Saw, Saw Away) · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Partners hold hands and bend on so, stretch on mi — or the reverse, as long as the class agrees.

A small knee bend on each so keeps the beat honest.

Rain, Rain, Go Away

English nursery rhyme · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Finger rain on the beat. Freeze on the rests.

The classic so–mi reading song. Sign so (higher) and mi (lower) while you sing.

Practice studio

So–mi–la piano

G is so, E is mi, A is la. Tap to hear. Grade 1 lives on the first three.
