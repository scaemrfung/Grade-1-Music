This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Story suggestion

The Jazz Fly · Matthew Gollub (illus. Karen Hanke)

Scat and drum patterns invite echoing and building short rhythmic (four-beat) ideas.

Buy on Amazon · Listen: author’s full audio (Matthew Gollub (author) on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, in the idea slot (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “The fly is lost and asks animals for directions. Listen for the sounds they answer with.”
  • Put four beat boxes on the board.

During reading

  • Turn each animal sound into a four-beat row with ta and rest: hog “oink, oink” (ta ta), dog “ruff” (ta), then sh (rest).
  • Write one row, e.g. “oink, oink, ruff, sh” = ta ta ta rest. Read it, clap it, then play it on a drum.
  • When the Queen Bee asks for a new beat, stop and ask: “How will the fly make one?”
  • When the fly mixes the sounds, the class reads two rows back to back.

After reading

Read two animal rows back to back.

Quick check: “How many beats in a row?”

Adapt: Provide animal picture cards with the rhythm printed underneath. English learners can say the animal sounds even without the words.

Extend: Layer two patterns at once, half the class on each, like the fly’s band.

Activity · Card change

The teacher swaps one card; the class reads the new row.

Close

Line up.

  • Rows are only ta and rest this week; ti-ti comes next month.

Watch-outs

  • Speeding up in relays.

Challenge

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Pease Porridge Hot

English nursery rhyme · public domain · rhythm chant (ta and rest) · 2/4

Spoken. Add so–mi later if you wish.

Game: Partner clapping on the tas; open hands on the rest.

The rest after hot and cold is the whole point. Do not skip it.

Engine, Engine Number Nine

Traditional playground chant · public domain · so–mi (or spoken on the beat) · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk the beat like a train. Freeze on the rest.

Teach it spoken on the pulse first, then sung so–mi. Chicago can become a local town.
