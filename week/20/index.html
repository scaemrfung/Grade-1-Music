This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Weeks 1–19 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Later months use the same approach as each month is rewritten. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Day 1 · Prepare

Bring: Grade One rhythm cards or board rows (ta, ti-ti, rest), Tanka Tanka Skunk! if you have it

GAMEPLAN book, Day 1: p. 59

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi. One short ta/ti-ti clap echo.
  3. 5 minWhich Rhythm Do You Hear? (book p. 59) — Post five four-beat cards with ta, ti-ti, and rest (title and page only — draw your own). Class speaks and claps each card. You clap one; students show the card number with fingers. Homeroom: cards under the document camera.
  4. 6 minAsk the class — “Which card had ti-ti? Which card had a rest?”
  5. 5 minRead-aloud · Tanka Tanka Skunk! — Use the book guide. Ta and ti-ti names.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Speak one rhythm card once, then walk.

Teach

Oliver Twist · Cumberland Square · Melodrama (book pp. 60–61)

Oliver Twist by title and page only — speak with pulse actions, add melody, sing/think/sing, show three-part form with shape cards (same shape for repeated parts). Cumberland Square with sticks: Part One touches body parts; Part Two taps sticks (Ti-ti ta feel). Melodrama: villain / damsel / hero voices with accordion-folded paper napkins. Homeroom: sticks beside desks; Melodrama seated.

Story suggestion

Tanka Tanka Skunk! · Steve Webb

One-sound and two-sound names map cleanly to ta and ti-ti — the same hear-and-name work as Which Rhythm Do You Hear?

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Mr. Simon Music on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 1, after Which Rhythm Do You Hear? (about 5 min).

Before reading

  • “Some names have one sound on a beat. Some have two — that is ti-ti.”
  • Show one finger for ta, two for ti-ti.

During reading

  • Chant “Tan-ka Tan-ka Skunk” with the beat on laps: ti-ti ti-ti ta.
  • For each animal, show one or two fingers.

After reading

Sort one animal name as ta or ti-ti.

Quick check: “Was that name one sound or two?”

Adapt: Picture cards with one or two dots underneath.

Extend: Build a four-beat animal parade on the board.

Activity · Read-aloud · Tanka Tanka Skunk!

Use the book guide. Ta and ti-ti names.

Close

Speak one rhythm card once, then walk.

Teacher notes

  • GAMEPLAN February Week One (pp. 59–61). Which Rhythm Do You Hear?, Notating SM, Oliver Twist, Cumberland Square, and Melodrama by title and page only — draw your own cards and staff. Engine, Engine is on this site. Ti-ti is in the book this week — include it.

  • Homeroom kit: rhythm cards or board rows, sticks, paper napkins; shape cards if you have them.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group D (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Skipping rests on the cards.
  • Copying book notation from memory onto the board.
  • Melodrama voices that turn into shouts.

Challenge

Lead one Melodrama line with a kind loud or quiet voice.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Pease Porridge Hot

English nursery rhyme · public domain · rhythm chant (ta and rest) · 2/4

Spoken. Add so–mi later if you wish.

Game: Partner clapping on the tas; open hands on the rest.

The rest after hot and cold is the whole point. Do not skip it.

Engine, Engine Number Nine

Traditional playground chant · public domain · so–mi (or spoken on the beat) · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk the beat like a train. Freeze on the rest.

Teach it spoken on the pulse first, then sung so–mi. Chicago can become a local town.
