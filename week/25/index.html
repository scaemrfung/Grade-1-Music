Body Percussion

Clap, pat, stamp, snap — four sounds we already own. Transfer the beat and simple patterns onto the body.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Class 1 introduces, Class 2 practises, Class 3 applies and checks. Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Class 1 · Introduce

Bring: No instruments (voices and body percussion)

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 4 minCopy four — Clap clap pat pat; class copies. Two-finger palm tap for anyone who can’t snap.
  3. 5 minName the four — Clap, pat, stamp (gentle), snap or tap. We should still hear the song.
  4. 4 minSafety — Stamps are quiet toe taps in the classroom, not jumps. Snaps are not at someone’s ear.
  5. 7 minRead-aloud · Clap Your Hands — Brain break standing beside desks, per the book guide.

Music time: 20 min · plus 5 min devotional

Class 2 · Practise

Bring: No instruments (voices and body percussion)

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 3 minCopy four — Warm up.
  3. 6 minBeat on the body — Sing Hello or Rain, Rain; beat in pats, then quiet toe taps. Try Teddy Bear.
  4. 6 minPattern on the body — ti-ti ta rest as clap-clap pat open.
  5. 5 minCopy four (extended) — Students lead four-beat body patterns for the class.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Copy four

Clap clap pat pat. Class copies. Snap if they can; many Grade 1s cannot snap — two-finger tap on the palm is a snap substitute.

Teach

Name the four

Clap, pat (laps), stamp (gentle), snap or tap. Volume rule: we should still hear the song.

Beat on the body

Sing Hello or Rain, Rain. Beat in pats. Then beat in quiet stamps.

Pattern on the body

ti-ti ta rest on clap-clap pat open. This links unit 3 to instruments next week.

Safety

Stamps are not jumps. Snaps are not at someone’s ear.

Story suggestion

Clap Your Hands · Lorinda Bryan Cauley

Clap, stomp, and move cues map directly to clap–pat–stamp–snap body percussion.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Brightly Storytime on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Warm-up extension or brain break (about 5 min). Clear a little space.

Before reading

  • “Our four body sounds: clap, pat, stamp, snap (or finger tap). Listen for which one the book asks for.”
  • “We should still hear the words over our sounds.”

During reading

  • Read the rhyming commands on a steady beat. Do each one four times: clap your hands, stomp your feet (gently), slap your knee (pat), wiggle your fingers (snap or tap).
  • After each rhyming pair, play a four-beat body ostinato: clap–pat–clap–rest.
  • On “quiet as a mouse, now don’t say a word,” freeze for a silent beat.
  • Skip or change the lines that don’t fit a music room (blow a kiss instead of a kiss, no somersault: spin once slowly).

After reading

Body ostinato: a small group repeats clap–pat–clap–rest while the class sings Rain, Rain. Swap groups.

Quick check: “Name our four body sounds. Which one was the rest?”

Adapt: Offer seated versions of every move. English learners can copy your actions without the words.

Extend: Children write a four-beat body pattern with icons (hand, lap, foot) and the class performs it.

Activity · Body ostinato

A small group repeats clap-pat-clap-rest while the class sings. Swap groups.

Close

Freeze in a body-percussion statue. Stamp if volume stayed kind.

Materials

  • Open space
  • A known song
  • Quiet floor for stamps

Teacher notes

  • Substitute snap from day one so no one feels broken for not snapping.
  • Carpet classes: stamp becomes a toe tap.

Watch-outs

  • A complicated cup game. Too soon.

Challenge

Invent a four-beat body pattern and teach your row.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Teddy Bear

Traditional action song · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Turn around, touch the ground, show your shoe, that will do — one action per phrase.

Actions stay small so the tune survives.

Pease Porridge Hot

English nursery rhyme · public domain · rhythm chant (ta and rest) · 2/4

Spoken. Add so–mi later if you wish.

Game: Partner clapping on the tas; open hands on the rest.

The rest after hot and cold is the whole point. Do not skip it.
