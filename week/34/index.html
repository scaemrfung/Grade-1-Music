Your jobs today

Warm-up · Bad audience / good audience

Teacher models wiggling and talking. Students freeze it. Then model still and watching. Name the difference. Keep it funny, not shaming.

Teach

Audience rules

Voices off. Bodies on the chair. Eyes on the players. Clap when the piece is done, not in the rest in the middle. No phones — say it if families will attend.

Performer rules

Wait for the start. If you mess up, keep going. Smile at the end.

Rehearse both jobs

Half class performs piece 1, half is audience. Swap.

The clap

Two or three seconds of clapping, then rest. Not a stomp-clap chant unless you choose that as a class.

Story suggestion

Never Play Music Right Next to the Zoo · John Lithgow (illus. Leeza Hernandez)

Concert-going story with the punchline: pay attention when an orchestra plays.

Buy on Amazon · Watch John Lithgow read it (Storyline Online on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Hook at the start of Teach (about 7 min). Lithgow’s reading on Storyline Online fits well.

Before reading

  • “The boy in this story is at a concert. Listen for what goes wrong for him as an audience member.”
  • Model a good listener: still body, eyes on the players.

During reading

  • The chorus returns: “Never play music right next to the zoo.” The class says it with you each time.
  • When the animals take over (monkeys on fiddle, bison on bass, the yak on sax, the hippo on tuba), pretend-play each instrument for four beats. Then freeze on your quiet hand.
  • Notice that the audience stays calm and keeps watching. Ask: “What does a good audience do when something surprising happens?”
  • End on rule number two: don’t fall asleep when an orchestra plays.

After reading

Rehearse both jobs: half the class performs a concert piece, half is the audience. Swap. Practise a two-second clap at the end.

Quick check: “Name one audience rule and one performer rule.”

Adapt: Show a picture of “audience” (chairs, eyes on the stage). Children who need movement can play the animal parts in the retelling.

Extend: Draw your favourite animal musician and write (or tell) what instrument it plays.

Activity · Dress rehearsal without costumes

Full order. Real start and stop. Teacher only conducts. No extra teaching talk if you can help it.

Close

One compliment from audience to performers, one from performers to audience. Stamp if both jobs appeared.

Materials

  • A short audio or a teacher solo
  • Practice chairs as “audience”

Teacher notes

  • If families come next week, send the family letter today.
  • Plan where coats and siblings go. Grade 1 concerts fail in the hallway, not on the songs.

Watch-outs

  • A lecture on etiquette with no practice. Do the jobs.

Challenge

Be the person who starts the clap when the conductor’s hands drop.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Adult key: start comfortably. Do not force the high notes — hum if needed.
