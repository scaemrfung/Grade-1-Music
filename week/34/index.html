This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. From Week 9 on, each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Weeks 1–8 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Story suggestion

Never Play Music Right Next to the Zoo · John Lithgow (illus. Leeza Hernandez)

Concert-going story with the punchline: pay attention when an orchestra plays.

Buy on Amazon · Watch John Lithgow read it (Storyline Online on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 1, in the idea slot (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “The boy in this story is at a concert. Listen for what goes wrong for him as an audience member.”
  • Model a good listener: still body, eyes on the players.

During reading

  • The chorus returns: “Never play music right next to the zoo.” The class says it with you each time.
  • When the animals take over (monkeys on fiddle, bison on bass, the yak on sax, the hippo on tuba), pretend-play each instrument for four beats. Then freeze on your quiet hand.
  • Notice that the audience stays calm and keeps watching. Ask: “What does a good audience do when something surprising happens?”
  • End on rule number two: don’t fall asleep when an orchestra plays.

After reading

Audience practice: calm eyes, still hands, a real clap.

Quick check: “What does a good audience do when something surprising happens?”

Adapt: Show a picture of “audience” (chairs, eyes on the stage). Children who need movement can play the animal parts in the retelling.

Extend: Draw your favourite animal musician and write (or tell) what instrument it plays.
