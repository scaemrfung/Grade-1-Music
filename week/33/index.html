This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Class 1 introduces, Class 2 practises, Class 3 applies and checks. Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Class 1 · Introduce

Bring: No instruments (voices and body percussion)

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 4 minMemory lane — Hum four known songs; hands up for ones they feel ready to share.
  3. 5 minRead-aloud · Pete the Cat: I Love My White Shoes — Per the book guide. Treat it as one candidate song.
  4. 5 minWhat makes a good share song — We know it, we can start and stop, we still like it.
  5. 6 minVote with kindness — Choose the songs this class can do well together.

Class 2 · Practise

Bring: Hand drum, woodblocks

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 3 minMemory lane — Sing the chosen songs once.
  3. 1 minInstruments out & back — Hand out woodblocks in rest position; collect them the same way at the end.
  4. 6 minThe played piece — Choose one: body percussion ostinato, found-sound pattern, or a student four-beat pattern on woodblocks with your hand drum.
  5. 5 minWrite the order — Song, played piece, song. Put it on the board.
  6. 5 minPractise the standing spots — Walking between pieces is the concert.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Memory lane

Hum four known songs. Students raise hands for the ones they feel ready to share.

Teach

What makes a good share song

We know the words. We can start and stop. We still like it. Not the newest, hardest thing.

Vote with kindness

We may not pick everyone’s first choice. We pick what this class can do well together.

The played piece

Body percussion ostinato, a found-sound pattern, or a student four-beat echoed by all. One only.

Write the order

Song, played piece, song. Practise walking to standing spots between them. That transition is the concert.

Story suggestion

Pete the Cat: I Love My White Shoes · Eric Litwin (illus. James Dean)

Class favorite song is a natural choice when selecting repertoire to share at a concert.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read & sing-along (HarperKids on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Warm-up or Memory lane (about 5 min). Use it as one candidate song for the concert.

Before reading

  • “A good share song: we know it, we can start and stop, and we still like it. Is Pete’s song one?”
  • Hand in (start) and hand out (stop).

During reading

  • Sing through it once with a student conductor starting and stopping each colour verse.
  • Practise a clean ending: after the last verse (wet shoes), everyone stops on the conductor’s closed hand and freezes.
  • Try the transition: sit for the story parts, stand for Pete’s song. Transitions are part of the concert.
  • Vote kindly: thumbs up if you feel ready to share it.

After reading

Draft run: put the chosen order on the board (song, played piece, song) and time one full run.

Quick check: “Why is this song a good choice (or not) for our concert?”

Adapt: A child who is nervous about singing can hold the colour cards on stage.

Extend: Add a class-made verse to perform as a surprise ending.

Activity · Draft run

Stand, piece 1, sit or stay, piece 2, piece 3, bow (a small nod). Time it. Aim under five minutes.

Close

Write the program on the board and leave it up. Stamp if the order survived one full run.

Materials

Challenge

Be the announcer: say the name of piece 1 in a talking voice.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Rain, Rain, Go Away

English nursery rhyme · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Finger rain on the beat. Freeze on the rests.

The classic so–mi reading song. Sign so (higher) and mi (lower) while you sing.

Bounce High, Bounce Low

Traditional singing game · public domain · so–mi–la · 2/4

so = G, la = A, mi = E

Game: Soft ball or beanbag. Underhand only. Swap in a class name for Shiloh.

La is the new neighbour above so. Sign it higher, sing it lightly.

Pease Porridge Hot

English nursery rhyme · public domain · rhythm chant (ta and rest) · 2/4

Spoken. Add so–mi later if you wish.

Game: Partner clapping on the tas; open hands on the rest.

The rest after hot and cold is the whole point. Do not skip it.
