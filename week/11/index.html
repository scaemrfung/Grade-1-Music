Fast and Slow

Music can go fast or slow. That speed is called tempo. The pulse can change speed but it still stays steady.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Nov 16–20

Full week: every Grade 1 class gets all 3 classes.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. From Week 9 on, each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Weeks 1–8 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Story suggestion

We're Going on a Bear Hunt · Michael Rosen (illus. Helen Oxenbury)

Classic chanted/sung adventure with actions—song played as a class game.

Buy on Amazon · Watch Michael Rosen perform it (Walker Books on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, in the idea slot (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “Listen for the part that comes back again and again. That’s our chorus.”
  • “In a singing game we keep our voices light, even when it gets exciting.”

During reading

  • Chant the chorus (“We’re going on a bear hunt…”) with a pat-clap steady beat. Everyone joins.
  • “We can’t go over it. We can’t go under it. Oh no! We’ve got to go through it!” Hands go over, then under, on the beat.
  • Give a small group a turn for each sound word: swishy swashy grass (rub palms), splash splosh river (pat laps), squelch squerch mud (gentle stamps), stumble trip forest, hoooo wooooo snowstorm (voices slide high and low), tiptoe cave (whisper).
  • Run home fast, then freeze under the covers. Talk about how the tempo sped up.

After reading

Run home fast, then slow down to a sleepy walk. Talk about tempo.

Quick check: “When did the story go fast? Slow?”

Adapt: Hold up picture cards (grass, river, mud, forest, snow, cave) so everyone knows what comes next. Children who need to move can lead the actions.

Extend: Add a new obstacle, invent its sound word, and pick a classroom instrument for it.

Teacher notes

  • Tempo is speed; do not mix with loud/quiet.
  • Keep fast safe: fast tiptoe, not running.
  • Train games: walk in a line, hands on shoulders.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group C (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Fast becoming loud.
  • Running in trains.

Challenge

Be the engine and choose the tempo.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Little Red Caboose

American folk song · public domain · mi–so–la · 2/4

so = G

Game: Make a train and chug on the pulse; the engine chooses fast or slow.

Use it for tempo: slow uphill, faster downhill.

Engine, Engine Number Nine

Traditional playground chant · public domain · so–mi (or spoken on the beat) · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk the beat like a train. Freeze on the rest.

Teach it spoken on the pulse first, then sung so–mi. Chicago can become a local town.
