This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Class 1 introduces, Class 2 practises, Class 3 applies and checks. Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Copy my hands, no voice

Silent sign strings of four. Students copy. Then add voice.

Teach

Hands are the music

You are not guessing. You are reading. Eyes on hands, then sound.

Four-beat reading

so so mi so. so la so mi. For a rest, hands stay still and open (a closed fist is the sign for do). Keep it slow.

Student conductor

A child signs a pattern they know. Class sings. Help them stand where everyone can see.

From signs to a song

Sign the first phrase of Rain, Rain. They should recognize it. Magic moment — name it.

Story suggestion

Do Re Mi: If You Can Read Music, Thank Guido D'Arezzo · Susan L. Roth

Picture-book story of solfa/notation that pairs with reading Curwen hand signs.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Mrs. Scharpenberg on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Hook for Teach (about 7 min). Summarise the middle pages. Read the start and the end.

Before reading

  • “Long ago, no one could write music down. How do you think children learned songs?” (By copying, like our echoes.)
  • “Today our hands will be the music.”

During reading

  • When the choirmaster sings and the children repeat, stop and do a quick so–mi echo together.
  • When Guido holds up his hand and says to pretend his fingers are lines, everyone holds up a hand. Notes go up in order from low to high.
  • Sing mi, so, la moving your hand upward. Point out that our hand signs are another way to read music without paper.
  • When the choir finally sings a song they’ve never heard just by reading it, cheer. That is what you’ll do today with signs.

After reading

Sign theatre: student conductors sign so–mi–la patterns and the class sings them. Sneak in the first phrase of Rain, Rain for them to discover.

Quick check: Eyes closed: teacher sings so–mi–la–so. Students sign what they heard.

Adapt: Pair a quieter child with a partner to sign together. English learners can follow the hand shapes without the words.

Extend: Show a picture of a five-line staff and count the lines and spaces on a hand.

Activity · Sign theatre

Three student conductors. Class sings each pattern twice. Teacher sneaks in a known song phrase to be discovered.

Close

All eyes closed. Teacher sings so–mi–la–so. Students sign what they heard. Stamp if signs match.

Materials

  • Clear sightlines
  • No other props

Teacher notes

  • Stand on a chair only if it is safe and allowed. Otherwise a stool or just the front.
  • Show a rest with still, open hands, not a fist: a closed fist is the sign for do.

Watch-outs

  • Patterns longer than four. Reading falls apart.

Challenge

Sign a whole phrase of Bounce High. Can the class name it?

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Bounce High, Bounce Low

Traditional singing game · public domain · so–mi–la · 2/4

so = G, la = A, mi = E

Game: Soft ball or beanbag. Underhand only. Swap in a class name for Shiloh.

La is the new neighbour above so. Sign it higher, sing it lightly.

Rain, Rain, Go Away

English nursery rhyme · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Finger rain on the beat. Freeze on the rests.

The classic so–mi reading song. Sign so (higher) and mi (lower) while you sing.

Practice studio

So–mi–la piano

G is so, E is mi, A is la. Tap to hear. Grade 1 lives on the first three.
