Label La, Mallets and Miss Mary Mack

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Weeks 1–19 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Later months use the same approach as each month is rewritten. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Hello Song, so–mi

Greet on so–mi. Reach once for a high la siren (soft).

Teach

Phrase echoes · Bounce High · Labeling La (book p. 68)

Short phrase-length body-percussion echoes (book p. 38 idea). Bounce High, Bounce Low on this site — bounce on the pulse. Label La: name the pitch above so, show the drooping-hand Curwen sign, echo the book’s call-and-response sequence (title and page only), then combine so–mi–la in four-beat patterns with signs.

Do As I’m Doing (book p. 69)

Title and page only. Sing while marching; copy nonlocomotor moves; then lines behind barred instruments copy the teacher’s light mallet ideas (high/low, tremolo, glissando). Rotate the front player. Homeroom: one instrument; pencil mallets on desks for waiting students.

Which Instrument Do You Hear? · Miss Mary Mack (book p. 70)

Hide six unpitched instruments; one child plays while another points to the matching icon. Miss Mary Mack by title and page only — echo verses; clap/out pulse ostinato; partner claps when ready. Homeroom: instruments behind a screen or under a cloth.

How to use this book

When: Day 3, before Which Instrument Do You Hear? (about 5 min).

Before reading

  • “Each page adds a new sound. Listen — don’t guess yet.”
  • Show listen ears.

During reading

  • Freeze and point to where the new instrument “sits” in the room when you hear a new timbre word.
  • Keep voices off during the listening.

After reading

Name one classroom unpitched sound you know.

Quick check: “Was that wood, metal, or skin?”

Adapt: Two instrument choices only.

Extend: A child plays one hidden sound; the class points to the icon.
