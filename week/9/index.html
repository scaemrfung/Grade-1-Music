Your jobs today

Warm-up · Simon beat

“Beat in your hands.” “Beat in your feet.” “Beat on your shoulders.” Change every eight beats. Freeze on “rest.”

Teach

The beat can move around the body

Same even heart, new place. This is body percussion without fancy names yet.

Shoulders and heads are gentle

No hitting. Two fingers. Model the volume you want.

Sing on top

See-Saw or Engine, Engine. Teacher points to hands or feet. Class keeps the song going while the beat location changes.

Freeze is a rest for the body

Connect to next unit’s silence, but do not name ta yet.

Story suggestion

Noisy Nora · Rosemary Wells

Nora’s noisy hands/feet then sudden silence support body beat plus freeze/control.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Love2Read with Miss Ellis on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: After Teach, before Cue-card beat (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “Nora makes noise to get noticed. Listen for her noises and for the moment everything goes quiet.”
  • “Show me where the beat is: hands? feet?”

During reading

  • After each noisy line (she bangs the window, slams the door, drops the marbles, knocks over the lamp, flies the kite down the stairs), the class plays four beats of body percussion. Call out where: hands, feet, shoulders (two fingers).
  • On “Quiet!” and “Hush!” everyone freezes. Teacher reads the family’s lines. Students only do the freeze.
  • When Nora says she’s leaving and there isn’t a sound, hold a long, still freeze.
  • At the end, when she comes back with a crash, make one big clap and freeze.

After reading

Play Cue-card beat with a “NORA!” card. When it’s held up, freeze.

Quick check: “Where was the beat when I said feet? What did we do on ‘Hush’?”

Adapt: Show pictures or icons for HANDS, FEET, and FREEZE. Let a child who needs to move hold the cards.

Extend: Add small percussion for Nora’s noises (drum for the door, shaker for the marbles). Everything freezes on “Hush.”

Activity · Cue-card beat

A student helper holds HANDS or FEET. Teacher still controls freeze. Rotate the helper.

Close

Name a child who changed on the first try. Stamp if the song survived the switches.

Materials

  • Known song
  • Cue cards: hands / feet / freeze
  • Drum

Teacher notes

  • Helpers who struggle with beat can still hold the card — that is a real job.
  • If noise spikes, sit and do only hands.

Watch-outs

  • Too many body parts. Hands, feet, freeze is enough.

Challenge

Switch every four beats instead of eight. Still even?

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Teddy Bear

Traditional action song · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Turn around, touch the ground, show your shoe, that will do — one action per phrase.

Actions stay small so the tune survives.

Acka Backa

Traditional playground rhyme · public domain · so–mi chant · 2/4

Spoken or so–mi

Game: Passing a beanbag on the beat. The child on “boo” is next leader — kindly, no out-shame.

Pulse first. Rhythm of the words later.
