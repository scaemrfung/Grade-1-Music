Ta: The Pulse Has a Name

The heartbeat pulse now gets a name: ta. We say ta, clap ta, and read hearts as ta.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Oct 26–30

Full week: every Grade 1 class gets all 3 classes.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Story suggestion

Chicka Chicka Boom Boom · Bill Martin Jr. & John Archambault (illus. Lois Ehlert)

Chanted word-rhythms (“chicka chicka boom boom”) vs keeping a steady underlying beat.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Buddy Son Storytime on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, in the idea slot (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “Two jobs today: laps keep the even beat, words make the rhythm.”
  • Put four even dots on the board and have everyone touch the air as you point.

During reading

  • First read: everyone chants “Chicka chicka boom boom! Will there be enough room?” while you pat the beat.
  • Split the class. Half pat the beat. Half say “A told B, and B told C, I’ll meet you at the top of the coconut tree.” Swap on the next refrain.
  • Write “chicka chicka boom boom” under the four dots. Notice how the syllables bunch up while the dots stay even.
  • When the letters fall out of the tree, everyone freezes. Restart the beat together.

After reading

Say the refrain, then say “ta ta ta ta” on the pulse under it.

Quick check: “Did the words stay even, or the ta?”

Adapt: Let children who find two jobs hard do only the beat. English learners can chant just “boom boom.”

Extend: Play the words on rhythm sticks and the beat on a drum at the same time.

Teacher notes

  • Ta = pulse for now. It is also the quarter note shape, but keep it simple.
  • Keep hearts and ta cards together on the board.
  • Chant ta softly so the song can be heard.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group A (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Clapping the words instead of ta.
  • Ta that speeds up.

Challenge

Point to the hearts while the class sings.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

so = G

Game: Finger rain on the beat. Freeze on the rests.

The classic so–mi reading song. Sign so (higher) and mi (lower) while you sing.

Bounce High, Bounce Low

Traditional singing game · public domain · so–mi–la · 2/4

so = G, la = A, mi = E

Game: Soft ball or beanbag. Underhand only. Swap in a class name for Shiloh.

La is the new neighbour above so. Sign it higher, sing it lightly.
