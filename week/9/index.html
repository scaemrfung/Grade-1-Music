Week 09 · Ta, Tempo & Contour · Oct 26–30

Ta: The Pulse Has a Name

The heartbeat pulse now gets a name: ta. We say ta, clap ta, and keep it under Engine Engine and Yankee Doodle. We also meet a short introduction before the singing starts, and we try the pulse fast and slow.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Oct 26–30

Full week: every Grade 1 class gets all 3 classes.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Weeks 1–12 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Later months use the same approach as each month is rewritten. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Day 1 · Prepare

Bring: Hand drum, 4 heart cards, ta cards

GAMEPLAN book, Day 1: pp. 26–27

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet children on so–mi. Pat the pulse and say ta under the last phrase.
  3. 5 minHearts become ta (book idea, Nov) — Point to four hearts. “Each heart is one pulse. We call it ta.” Put a ta card under each heart. Say and clap ta ta ta ta.
  4. 6 minEngine Engine Number Nine (book pp. 26–27) — Teach the chant from the book. Slide palms back and forth on the pulse, then transfer the pulse to a scraped drum. Echo by phrase. Try it slow, then a little faster — still even.
  5. 5 minEight-beat introduction — One child scrapes eight tas on the drum before the class speaks. Call that little wait the introduction: something short that happens at the beginning. Then the class comes in together.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Whisper ta ta ta ta to the door.

Use in class

  • 🔢Rhythm makerHearts become ta: read a row of tas.
  • 🚪Song doorwayEight-beat introduction: wait, then speak Engine Engine.

Music time: 20 min · plus 5 min devotional

Day 2 · Practice

Bring: Hand drum, barred instrument or xylophone (one), Chicka Chicka Boom Boom if you have it

GAMEPLAN book, Day 2: pp. 27–28

  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi; class answers on ta pats.
  3. 4 minEngine Engine with a two-note accompaniment (book p. 27) — Two children play a gentle two-note pulse on the bars after the drum introduction. Other children speak and march a short train in the aisle or carpet. Homeroom: one xylophone carried in; Music room next year: a short line of barred instruments.
  4. 5 minRead-aloud · Chicka Chicka Boom Boom — Use the book guide. Half pats ta; half chants.
  5. 5 minTa freeze — Walk and say ta; freeze when the drum stops. Restart on ta.
  6. 2 minTrain rotate — Drummer hands the drum to the next child and joins the train. Keep turns short.
  7. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Introduction of four tas on the drum, then walk.

Day 3 · Transfer

Bring: Hand drum, rhythm sticks (optional)

GAMEPLAN book, Day 3: p. 28

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi.
  3. 5 minYankee Doodle (book p. 28) — Teach or review from the book (title and page only here). Echo by phrase. Pat on phrases 1 and 3; clap on phrases 2 and 4. March in place on the pulse.
  4. 5 minTa under Yankee + 10-second checks — Whisper ta on the pulse while a few children pat. Give each checked child a short solo. Rotating check, group A (about 7 children): while the game runs, give each child a 10-second solo: scrape or pat four tas, then freeze for one silent beat. Mark Y or N on the record sheet.
  5. 6 minAsk the class — “Was Yankee Doodle new to you?” Keep answers short.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — March on ta to the door.

Story suggestion

Chicka Chicka Boom Boom · Bill Martin Jr. & John Archambault (illus. Lois Ehlert)

Chanted word-rhythms (“chicka chicka boom boom”) sit on a steady underlying beat — the same idea as naming that beat ta.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Buddy Son Storytime on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, after Engine Engine (about 5 min).

Before reading

  • “Two jobs today: our laps keep the even beat (ta), and the words make the rhythm.”
  • Put four heart cards on the board. Touch each one and say ta.

During reading

  • First read: everyone chants “Chicka chicka boom boom! Will there be enough room?” while you pat ta on your knees.
  • Split the class. Half pats ta. Half says “A told B, and B told C…” Swap on the next refrain.
  • When the letters fall out of the tree, everyone freezes (a silent beat). Restart together on ta.

After reading

Say the refrain once, then whisper “ta ta ta ta” under it.

Quick check: “Did the words stay even, or the ta?”

Adapt: Seated children can pat shoulders instead of knees.

Extend: Pairs invent a four-ta chant of their own names.

Activity · Eight-beat introduction

One child scrapes eight tas on the drum before the class speaks. Call that little wait the introduction: something short that happens at the beginning. Then the class comes in together.

Close

Whisper ta ta ta ta to the door.

Teacher notes

  • Ta is introduced this week (November). Keep the word simple: one pulse = one ta.

  • Pitched: one xylophone or one barred instrument. Percussion: hand drum (add rhythm sticks only if you can carry them).
  • Homeroom: train marches in place or down an aisle; Music room next year: room to wind a longer train.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group A (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Clapping the words instead of ta.
  • Starting before the introduction finishes.
  • Fast becoming a scramble.

Challenge

Lead the eight-beat introduction for the class.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Engine, Engine Number Nine

Traditional playground chant · public domain · so–mi (or spoken on the beat) · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk the beat like a train. Freeze on the rest.

Teach it spoken on the pulse first, then sung so–mi. Chicago can become a local town.

Hello Everybody

Traditional greeting · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

Sing so as G (or C if that sits better).

Game: Echo by phrase, then greet two children by name on so–mi.

Keep it light. If so is a strain, drop the whole song. The quiet hand starts and stops.
