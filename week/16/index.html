Week 16 · Long, Short, Silence · December

Sh — the Rest

Silence is music too. A rest is one beat of no sound, with a still body.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Play, rest, play, rest

Clap, open hands (silence), clap, open hands. The open hands are the rest. Faces stay ready — rest is not slump.

Teach

Rest is a beat with no sound

We still feel it. We do not skip it. We do not say “sh” loudly if that becomes a sound — a finger on lips or open palms works.

Write it

A squiggle, a Z, or the traditional rest. Pick one.

Read ta ta rest ta

The tricky part is staying still for the full beat. Count it in your head.

Put a rest in a known chant

Make beat 4 silent in Engine, Engine. Giggle once, then make the silence real.

Story suggestion

The Quiet Book · Deborah Underwood (illus. Renata Liwska)

Celebrates many kinds of quiet—ideal metaphor for musical silence and the rest.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Langworthy Public Library on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Close, or just before Mystery rest (about 5 min).

Before reading

  • “A rest is a beat with no sound. We still feel it.”
  • Practise open hands on the rest: clap, clap, clap, open.

During reading

  • Before each page, the class claps ta ta ta and opens hands on beat 4. Then read that kind of quiet.
  • Pause on the pages where the quiet has a still body: hide-and-seek quiet, top of the roller coaster quiet, right before you yell surprise quiet, before the concert starts quiet.
  • Remind them that a rest isn’t a slump. Bodies stay ready, like right before you yell surprise.
  • Read the last pages (story time quiet, bedtime kiss quiet, sound asleep quiet) more and more softly.

After reading

Mystery rest: clap four beats with one silent beat. Students point to the silent beat (1, 2, 3, or 4), then write their own pattern with one rest.

Quick check: “Which beat was the rest? Did we skip it, or feel it?”

Adapt: Children can hold up a picture of a “shh” finger during rests. Keep one quiet page per clap pattern so it stays short.

Extend: Children invent their own kind of quiet (“waiting for recess quiet”) and perform a four-beat pattern with one rest.

Activity · Mystery rest

Teacher claps a four-beat pattern with one rest. Students point to which beat was silent (1, 2, 3, or 4). Then they write their own with one rest and perform.

Close

Class statue for one full rest. Stamp if rests keep their full beat.

Materials

  • Board: rest icon (z or a sh finger)
  • Drum

Teacher notes

  • If they giggle through rests, make a game: who can keep the quietest rest?
  • Do not combine this with new pitches today.

Watch-outs

  • Rushing past the rest. Put your hand on the icon for the full beat.

Challenge

A pattern with two rests that still feels like music.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Pease Porridge Hot

English nursery rhyme · public domain · rhythm chant (ta and rest) · 2/4

Spoken. Add so–mi later if you wish.

Game: Partner clapping on the tas; open hands on the rest.

The rest after hot and cold is the whole point. Do not skip it.

Walk and Stop

Traditional movement game · public domain · chant on the beat · 4/4

Teacher drum is the boss.

Game: Walk the pulse. Statue on stop. Later: hop, then stop; tiptoe, then stop.

Own space. Soft knees. This is pulse, not racing.
