Week 16 · So–Mi, Notes & Reading · Jan 11–15

Label So–Mi and New-Year Pulse

A new-year poem keeps the pulse, gift words become a ta/ti-ti chain, Lucky Me! adds a chord bordun feel, and so and mi finally get their names with hand signs on Teddy Bear.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Jan 11–15

Full week: every Grade 1 class gets all 3 classes.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Weeks 1–19 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Later months use the same approach as each month is rewritten. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Day 1 · Prepare

Bring: Hand drum

GAMEPLAN book, Day 1: p. 47

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi. Pat a quiet ta under the last phrase.
  3. 5 minChristmas Now is Over (book p. 47) — Teach from the book (title and page only; use “someone” in place of any gift-giver name your school prefers). Echo by phrase while students pat the pulse. Choose a four-beat ostinato (for example pat, pat, clap, clap). After the poem, four children share a favourite gift or winter experience — unmetered and short. Homeroom: seated circle on the carpet.
  4. 6 minAsk the class — “What was one favourite gift or winter fun?” One or two answers only.
  5. 5 minPulse ostinato switch — Change the ostinato; class follows your lead for one more poem pass.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Whisper ta ta ta ta to the door.

Use in class

  • 💓Steady beatChristmas Now is Over: pulse under the poem.
  • 👏Body percussionPulse ostinato: pat and clap.

Music time: 20 min · plus 5 min devotional

Day 2 · Practice

Bring: Board and marker, rhythm sticks (one pair per child or shared), one bass xylophone or metallophone if you can carry it

GAMEPLAN book, Day 2: p. 48

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi; class pats ta.
  3. 5 minCreating a Word Chain (book p. 48) — From favourite gifts, choose words that fit two beats with ta, ti-ti, and rest (title and page only — write your own stick notation on the board). Speak and clap each gift; list text and rhythm. Class orders a chain; speak and clap, then play on rhythm sticks twice. Homeroom kit: sticks on desks.
  4. 6 minLucky Me! (book pp. 48–49) — Teach from the book (title and page only). Echo melody by two-measure phrases. Pat a simple chord-bordun feel, then march while singing (sticks rest on shoulders). Alternate song and the word chain (same/different). Homeroom: one carried-in barred instrument for the teacher bordun if available; Music room next year: full BX/BM.
  5. 5 minSame or different — Play song then chain; class shows same or different with arms.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — March four tas to the door.

Use in class

  • 🔢Rhythm makerWord chain: gift rhythms.
  • 🧱Song + ostinatoLucky Me!: song and chain alternate.

Music time: 20 min · plus 5 min devotional

Day 3 · Transfer

Bring: Hand-signal cards if you have them (Appendix C idea), Teddy Bear, Do Re Mi book if you have it

GAMEPLAN book, Day 3: p. 49

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi.
  3. 5 minLabeling SM (book p. 49) — After months of singing so–mi songs, name So and Mi and show Curwen hand signs (book sequence: high/low taps, then names and signs). Combine so and mi in four-beat patterns with both hands; so near mouth level, mi below the chin. Ask: “What songs have we sung on so and mi?”
  4. 5 minTeddy Bear with so–so–mi (book p. 49; also on this site) + 10-second checks — Review Teddy Bear. Discover that “Teddy bear” = so–so–mi. Sing again, substituting so–so–mi with hand signs on each “Teddy bear.” Rotating check, group D (about 7 children): while the game runs, give each child a 10-second solo: sing and sign a four-beat so–mi pattern, then freeze. Mark Y or N on the record sheet.
  5. 6 minSo–mi + Teddy checks — Checked children sing and sign one four-beat so–mi pattern, then show so–so–mi on “Teddy bear.”
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — So–so–mi tip-toe to the door.

Use in class

  • 🦜So–mi–la echoLabeling SM: so and mi with signs.
  • 🐱Copycat songsTeddy Bear: so–so–mi with signs.

Story suggestion

Do Re Mi: If You Can Read Music, Thank Guido D'Arezzo · Susan L. Roth

Guido names the pitches — the same idea as labeling so and mi with hand signs after months of singing them.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Mrs. Scharpenberg on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 3, after Labeling SM (about 5 min).

Before reading

  • “We already sang high and low. Today those notes get names.”
  • Show so (higher flat hand) and mi (lower flat hand) once.

During reading

  • When Guido’s choir echoes, freeze and echo a so–mi pattern with signs.
  • When hands become “lines,” hold up three fingers like a tiny staff.

After reading

Sing so–so–mi with signs for “Teddy bear.”

Quick check: “Which is higher — so or mi?”

Adapt: Seated signing; keep hands near the face.

Extend: A child leads one four-beat so–mi pattern; the class echoes.

Activity · Pulse ostinato switch

Change the ostinato; class follows your lead for one more poem pass.

Close

Whisper ta ta ta ta to the door.

Watch-outs

  • Long gift shares that eat the minutes.
  • Copying book notation onto the board from memory.
  • So and mi signs that sit too close together.

Challenge

Lead one four-beat so–mi pattern with signs.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Teddy Bear

Traditional action song · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Turn around, touch the ground, show your shoe, that will do — one action per phrase.

Actions stay small so the tune survives.

Hello Everybody

Traditional greeting · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

Sing so as G (or C if that sits better).

Game: Echo by phrase, then greet two children by name on so–mi.

Keep it light. If so is a strain, drop the whole song. The quiet hand starts and stops.

Practice studio

So–mi–la piano

G is so, E is mi, A is la. Tap to hear. Grade 1 lives on the first three.
