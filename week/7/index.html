This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. From Week 9 on, each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Weeks 1–8 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Day 1 · Prepare

Bring: Hand drum, four sheets of paper and tape, bean bag

GAMEPLAN book, Day 1: p. 20–21

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet children on so–mi.
  3. 4 minStirring My Brew (book p. 20) — Say the stirring chant in a whisper, then in a talking voice, then in a strong voice. Use the words quiet and strong, not “soft.”
  4. 7 minPaper beats: stand on the walk and jog (book p. 20) — Tape four sheets to the carpet or floor at the front, one for each beat. Children stand one to a sheet for a walking beat, and two to a sheet for a jogging beat. Use the rhythm of Big Black Cats.
  5. 5 minBig Black Cats body percussion (book p. 20) — Say the cat poem. Clap on walking beats and pat on jogging beats. Keep the silent beat if the poem has one.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Walk and jog to the door.

Use in class

  • 🔉Loud or soft?Stirring My Brew: loud or quiet? Use the words quiet and strong.
  • 👏Body percussionPaper beats: step and tap the beats.

Day 2 · Practice

Bring: Four woodblocks and beaters, glockenspiel, hand drum

GAMEPLAN book, Day 2: p. 21

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet children on so–mi.
  3. 2 minStirring My Brew (book p. 20) — One quick round: whisper, talking, strong.
  4. 9 minBig Black Cats on instruments (book p. 21) — Woodblocks play on lines 1, 2 and 4 of the poem and the glockenspiel (set to a pentatonic scale) plays on line 3. Pass the instruments around the circle during the interlude. Other children say the poem and clap and pat.
  5. 5 minHippity Hop (book p. 21) — Teach the nursery rhyme from the book. Pat the big beats, then echo by line.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Walk and freeze at the door.

Day 3 · Transfer

Bring: Yarn ball or bean bag, xylophone and glockenspiel, mallets, and My Family Plays Music by Judy Cox if you have it

GAMEPLAN book, Day 3: p. 21–22

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet children on so–mi.
  3. 6 minHippity Hop toss + 10-second checks (book p. 21) + 10-second checks — Sing the nursery rhyme while tossing a yarn ball or bean bag around the circle. The catcher answers a question such as “What is your favourite candy?” Give each child a short solo. Rotating check, group C (about 7 children): while the game runs, give each child a 10-second solo: say a favourite candy first in a whisper and then in a strong voice. Mark Y or N on the record sheet.
  4. 6 minCandy Store: steady two-note accompaniment (book p. 22) — Teach the song from the book. Two children play two bars on a pulse underneath while the class sings. Add a word-chain of candy words between verses and swap players.
  5. 4 minRead-aloud · My Family Plays Music — Use the book guide. For each instrument named, play it four times on the beat.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Walk on the drum and freeze at the door.

Use in class

  • 🏀Bouncing ballHippity Hop toss: the ball lands on each big beat.

Story suggestion

My Family Plays Music · Judy Cox (illus. Elbrite Brown)

Many instruments named and pictured—supports name/sound/picture instrument ID.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Grandma “B” Reads with Me on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 3, near the end of class (about 4 min).

Before reading

  • “The girl in this book plays with everyone in her family. Listen for her instrument on each page.”
  • “Is it hit, shaken, or scraped?”

During reading

  • When she names her instrument, hold up the real one (or the picture). Play it four times on the beat: tambourine with Mom, triangle with Dad, cymbals with her sister, cowbell with her brother, wood block with her aunt, maracas with her uncle, rhythm sticks with Grandpa.
  • The class names each one and shows the action: hit, shake, or scrape.
  • Point out the family’s big instruments too (fiddle, cello, clarinet, guitar, tuba). Their pictures help with the name game.
  • At the end she says she’s a percussionist. Talk about what that word means.

After reading

Match the table: hear an instrument, then point to its picture card.

Quick check: Play four sounds behind a screen. Students name each one.

Adapt: Put the word under each picture card. English learners can point to answer.

Extend: Children list a family member’s favourite music and pick a percussion instrument to play along.

Teacher notes

  • “Walk” and “jog” are felt only; the names come later (Weeks 9 and 21). Two-note accompaniment is named “bordun” in Week 18.
  • Use the words quiet and strong; piano and forte come in Week 16.
  • Pitched: one glockenspiel plus one xylophone only if the bars are limited to a pentatonic set.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group C (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Calling every quiet voice “soft.”
  • Passing instruments too slowly and losing the pulse.

Challenge

Lead a whisper-then-strong round of Stirring My Brew.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

The Muffin Man

English nursery rhyme and singing game · public domain · words on the pulse (simplified) · 4/4

Say it on a steady walking pulse; learn the real tune by ear from your voice or the recording.

Game: Eight plates on the board, one muffin on each. Point to a plate on every beat and the class says “yum.” Wipe one muffin away: that plate is a silent beat.

This page shows the words on the pulse, one syllable per beat, so the real tune and rhythm are simplified. The silent-beat game comes from the GAMEPLAN lesson (Oct Week 1, p. 13); the words and game here are our own.

Hello Everybody

Traditional greeting · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

Sing so as G (or C if that sits better).

Game: Echo by phrase, then greet two children by name on so–mi.

Keep it light. If so is a strain, drop the whole song. The quiet hand starts and stops.
