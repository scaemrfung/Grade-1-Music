Your jobs today

Warm-up · Heartbeat

Hand on chest. Feel your own heart if you can. Teacher plays a slow steady drum. Students pat that same speed on their legs. “The drum is the heart of the music.”

Teach

Beat does not stop for big words

Say “cat, cat, cat, cat” on the beat. Then “elephant, cat, elephant, cat.” The lap pats stay even. The words change. That even pat is the beat.

Show it three ways

Pat. Step in place. Quiet two-finger clap. Switch on your cue so they do not get stuck in one motion.

Song on top

Sing Rain, Rain or Hello while they pat. If pats follow the words instead of the beat, go back to drum only.

The beat can be slow or faster

Two tempos only today. Still even. Not “as fast as you can.”

Story suggestion

Hand, Hand, Fingers, Thumb · Al Perkins (illus. Eric Gurney)

Strong “dum ditty dum” pulse invites tapping a steady beat while reading.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Bookworm Bonny on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Hook at the start of Teach (about 5 min). It’s short, so read it twice.

Before reading

  • Hand on chest: “Feel your heartbeat. Does the drum in this book keep an even beat?”
  • “Listen for the words that repeat.”

During reading

  • First read: everyone pats a steady beat on laps the whole time. Chant “dum ditty dum ditty dum dum dum” together.
  • Let one child play the hand drum on the beat for the monkeys’ drumming pages.
  • Point out that the words change (rings on fingers, apples and plums, banjos that strum, fiddles that zum), but the pats stay even. That even pat is the beat.
  • Second read: a little faster, but still even. Not “as fast as you can.”

After reading

Go into Drum is the boss using the book’s chant. Fade the drum and see if the pats stay steady.

Quick check: “Did your pats follow the words or the beat? Which one stayed even?”

Adapt: If laps are too hard, tap shoulders or take steps in place. English learners can join on the repeated “dum ditty” line right away.

Extend: Split the class: half keep the beat on drums or laps, half chant only the words.

Activity · Drum is the boss

Teacher drum leads. Students match. Fade the drum and see if their pats stay even. Bring the drum back if they rush.

Close

Ask a student to play four even beats on the drum. Class pats with them. Stamp if most pats stay even without the words pulling them.

Materials

  • Heart drawn on the board
  • A drum
  • A known song

Teacher notes

  • Do not introduce ta yet. This week is only the even heart.
  • Sitting pats are easier than walking. Save walking for week 8.
  • A child who pats twice as fast is rushing — model beside them.

Watch-outs

  • Letting them pound. Quiet body percussion.
  • Talking about notes and stems. Not yet.

Challenge

Pat the beat with eyes closed for eight beats. Did you stay with the drum?

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Engine, Engine Number Nine

Traditional playground chant · public domain · so–mi (or spoken on the beat) · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk the beat like a train. Freeze on the rest.

Teach it spoken on the pulse first, then sung so–mi. Chicago can become a local town.

Walk and Stop

Traditional movement game · public domain · chant on the beat · 4/4

Teacher drum is the boss.

Game: Walk the pulse. Statue on stop. Later: hop, then stop; tiptoe, then stop.

Own space. Soft knees. This is pulse, not racing.
