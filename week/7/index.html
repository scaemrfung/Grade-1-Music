This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Story suggestion

My Family Plays Music · Judy Cox (illus. Elbrite Brown)

Many instruments named and pictured—supports name/sound/picture instrument ID.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Grandma “B” Reads with Me on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, in the idea slot (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “The girl in this book plays with everyone in her family. Listen for her instrument on each page.”
  • “Is it hit, shaken, or scraped?”

During reading

  • When she names her instrument, hold up the real one (or the picture). Play it four times on the beat: tambourine with Mom, triangle with Dad, cymbals with her sister, cowbell with her brother, wood block with her aunt, maracas with her uncle, rhythm sticks with Grandpa.
  • The class names each one and shows the action: hit, shake, or scrape.
  • Point out the family’s big instruments too (fiddle, cello, clarinet, guitar, tuba). Their pictures help with the name game.
  • At the end she says she’s a percussionist. Talk about what that word means.

After reading

Sort the book’s instruments onto three mats: wood, metal, skin.

Quick check: “Which family did the triangle belong to? The drum?”

Adapt: Put the word under each picture card. English learners can point to answer.

Extend: Children list a family member’s favourite music and pick a percussion instrument to play along.

Teacher notes

  • Skin = drum head (most classroom drums are plastic; call it the drum family).
  • Hold instruments yourself on Day 1; hand them out on Day 2.
  • Station switch on the quiet hand only.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group C (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Handing out instruments before the rules.
  • Letting stations play all at once.

Challenge

Find a new classroom object and say which family it sounds like.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Engine, Engine Number Nine

Traditional playground chant · public domain · so–mi (or spoken on the beat) · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk the beat like a train. Freeze on the rest.

Teach it spoken on the pulse first, then sung so–mi. Chicago can become a local town.

Rain, Rain, Go Away

English nursery rhyme · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Finger rain on the beat. Freeze on the rests.

The classic so–mi reading song. Sign so (higher) and mi (lower) while you sing.
