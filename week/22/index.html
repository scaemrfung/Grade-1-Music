This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. From Week 9 on, each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Weeks 1–8 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Story suggestion

Charlie Parker Played Be Bop · Chris Raschka

Playful long and short sound syllables make duration audible for young listeners.

Watch read-aloud · Watch Chris Raschka perform it (The Rabbit Hole Kansas City on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, in the idea slot (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “Listen for sounds that stretch long and sounds that pop short.”
  • Model a long “ooo” with arms opening and a short “ti” finger tap.

During reading

  • Second time through: stretch arms for long sounds, like the end of “Never leave your cat a-lone” and the buzzing “zznnzznn.”
  • Tap fingers for short sounds: “be bop,” “fisk, fisk,” “lollipop,” “boomba, boomba,” and the one-sound “BANG!”
  • Echo the playful word chains (“overshoes, overshoes…,” “chickadee, chickadee…”). Keep a steady pat underneath.
  • Ask: “Was that word long or short?” Answer with arms or fingers, not voices.

After reading

Arms for long sounds, fingers for short.

Quick check: “Was that sound long or short?”

Adapt: Use the arm and finger gestures for everything so English learners don’t need the words.

Extend: Play 30 seconds of a Charlie Parker recording. Do you hear long notes, short notes, or both?

Lucy Locket

Traditional singing game · public domain · so–mi–la · 2/4

so = G

Game: Circle. One walker drops a beanbag behind a friend on the last phrase. That friend walks next. Keep singing.

If the tune falls apart, sit and sing once, then play again.
