Your jobs today

Warm-up · So–mi neighbour

Review so–mi. Then teacher sings so–la–so–mi. Students echo on “loo.”

Teach

La is a step above so

Show the sign higher than so. Students copy. Sing la lightly — it should not punch.

Bounce High

“Bounce high, bounce low, bounce the ball to Shiloh.” Teach by echo. Put a class name in. Keep the ball bounce on the beat if you use one.

Sign the new note only

First pass, only sign la when it happens. Then sign the whole song.

Do not pile on do yet

Three pitches is plenty. Hot Cross Buns can wait until they own la.

Story suggestion

Up, Up, Down · Robert Munsch (illus. Michael Martchenko)

Climb phrases already used for so–mi can be extended by adding la on a third pitch.

Buy on Amazon · Listen: Robert Munsch reads it (Robert Munsch (official audio) on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Brain break before Name toss (about 5 min). Munsch’s reading is short.

Before reading

  • “Anna climbs higher and higher. Today we have a new higher step: la, above so.”
  • Sign mi, so, la from low to high.

During reading

  • On “up, up, up,” sing mi–so–la on the three “ups” and show the signs. On “down,” come back to mi.
  • When Anna reaches the top and shouts “I’m the king of the castle,” sing it on so–la–so–mi as a playground chant.
  • Keep la light. It should float, not punch.
  • When Mom and Dad fall, sing la–so–mi down the ladder. When Anna says “Be careful—don’t climb!” speak it.

After reading

Name toss with Bounce High: listen for la in “bounce the ball.”

Quick check: Silent signs: so–la–so–mi. “Which one is the new note?” (La, the highest.)

Adapt: Put a three-step ladder on the board (mi, so, la) and point as you sing. Hands-only signing is fine.

Extend: Children sing “I’m the king of the castle” with their own name, using so–la–so–mi.

Activity · Name toss

Bounce High with a soft ball or beanbag. The named child sings the last “Shiloh” as their own name on the same tune if they want. Optional.

Close

Silent signs: so–la–so–mi. Class sings. Stamp if la is in the neighbourhood of in-tune.

Materials

  • Bounce High, Bounce Low
  • La hand sign (relaxed hand drooping from the wrist, palm down, at eye height, just above so)

Teacher notes

  • Kodály la is a relaxed hand drooping from the wrist, palm and fingers facing down, at eye height, just above so. Keep the same signs all year.
  • Beanbag underhand. No dodgeball.

Watch-outs

  • A high la that becomes a squeal. Model a gentle one.

Challenge

Improvise four beats on so–mi–la with your voice. Class copies.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Bounce High, Bounce Low

Traditional singing game · public domain · so–mi–la · 2/4

so = G, la = A, mi = E

Game: Soft ball or beanbag. Underhand only. Swap in a class name for Shiloh.

La is the new neighbour above so. Sign it higher, sing it lightly.

Lucy Locket

Traditional singing game · public domain · so–mi–la · 2/4

so = G

Game: Circle. One walker drops a beanbag behind a friend on the last phrase. That friend walks next. Keep singing.

If the tune falls apart, sit and sing once, then play again.

So–mi–la piano

G is so, E is mi, A is la. Tap to hear. Grade 1 lives on the first three.
