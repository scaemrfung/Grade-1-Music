Week 03 · Voices & Pulse · Sept 14–18

Pulse as a Group

We keep one pulse together as a class: in a circle, passing, walking and playing, all on the same beat.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Sept 14–18

Full week: every Grade 1 class gets all 3 classes.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Story suggestion

Giraffes Can't Dance · Giles Andreae (illus. Guy Parker-Rees)

Gerald finds music he can move to—ideal for walking/dancing the beat with control.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (KidTimeStoryTime on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, in the idea slot (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “Gerald thinks he can’t dance. Listen for what finally helps him move.”
  • “Our feet follow the drum, not the words.”

During reading

  • At the Jungle Dance, give each animal its own drum groove. The class moves in place on the beat: warthogs waltzing (sway), rhinos rock ’n’ rolling, lions’ tango, chimps’ cha-cha-cha, baboons’ Scottish reel. Freeze at each page turn.
  • When the animals laugh at Gerald, stop the drum. Everyone freezes.
  • When the cricket plays his violin and says that sometimes when you’re different you just need a different song, play a slow, soft beat. Everyone sways gently.
  • For Gerald’s big dance, take bigger moves that stay on the beat.

After reading

Everyone does Gerald’s big dance on the pulse, then freezes together.

Quick check: “Did we all move on the same beat? How could you tell?”

Adapt: Chair dancers can move arms and heads only. Pairs can walk side by side.

Extend: Children pick a “different song” (drum, shaker, or piano on this site) and the class walks to its beat.

Activity · Pass on the pulse

Pass a beanbag around the circle, one pass per drum beat, while chanting Engine, Engine. Freeze on “line.”

Close

Walk as one train to the door.

Teacher notes

  • Stand where everyone can see you start and stop.
  • If passing breaks down, go back to patting only, then try again.
  • Sticks rest on the floor between turns (“rest position”).
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group C (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Letting the passing game go too fast.
  • Starting sticks before the count-in.

Challenge

Be the drum leader: keep the pulse for the class train.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Engine, Engine Number Nine

Traditional playground chant · public domain · so–mi (or spoken on the beat) · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk the beat like a train. Freeze on the rest.

Teach it spoken on the pulse first, then sung so–mi. Chicago can become a local town.

See-Saw

Traditional · public domain (also sung as Saw, Saw, Saw Away) · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Partners hold hands and bend on so, stretch on mi — or the reverse, as long as the class agrees.

A small knee bend on each so keeps the beat honest.
