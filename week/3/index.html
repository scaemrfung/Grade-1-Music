Week 03 · Voices & Pulse · Sept 14–18

Pulse as a Group

We keep one pulse together as a class: drawing it as hearts, playing it on woodblocks under a rhyme, and following a leader. We also move high and low and sing our names on so–mi.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Sept 14–18

Full week: every Grade 1 class gets all 3 classes.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. From Week 9 on, each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Weeks 1–8 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Day 1 · Prepare

Bring: Hand drum, woodblock and beater, whiteboard marker, bean bag

GAMEPLAN book, Day 1: p. 7–8

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet children on so–mi.
  3. 6 minI Think Music’s Neat: draw the beat (book p. 7) — Sing the song while everyone pats the pulse. As they pat, draw one heart for each beat on the board, eight to a line. Count the hearts together. The hearts are the steady beat; they have no other name yet.
  4. 6 minMoving high and low (book p. 7) — Play the low drum and everyone crouches, walking low. Play the high woodblock and everyone tiptoes tall. Mix them up and keep the pulse steady.
  5. 4 minSing Me Your Name (book p. 8) — Pass the bean bag around the circle. The holder sings “my name is ___” on so–mi and the class sings it back.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Walk on the pulse and freeze at the door.

Use in class

  • 🩺HeartbeatDraw the beat: start on a resting heartbeat, then pat along as hearts appear.
  • 🐦High or low?Moving high and low: hear a high or low sound and show it.

Music time: 20 min · plus 5 min devotional

Day 2 · Practice

Bring: Three to five woodblocks and beaters, hand drum, the Syncopated Clock recording

GAMEPLAN book, Day 2: p. 9

  3. 7 minHickory, Dickory, Dock: tick-tock pulse (book p. 9) — Split the class. Half whisper “tick-tock” on the pulse while the other half says the rhyme. Then swap. Swing arms like a pendulum.
  4. 6 minWoodblock tick-tock — Three to five children play the tick-tock pulse on woodblocks while the class says the rhyme. Rotate players every two times through.
  5. 3 minThe Syncopated Clock (book p. 9) — Play the LeRoy Anderson recording. Follow the leader: sway side to side, windshield-wiper fingers, then elbows up and down, all on the beat.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Tick-tock walk to the door.

Story suggestion

The Listening Walk · Paul Showers (illus. Aliki)

Models quiet listening, stopping to notice sounds, and sing–listen–move–stop classroom routines.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (KidTimeStoryTime on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 3, in the middle of the class (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “The girl in this book does not talk on her walk. Why might that help her hear more?”
  • Practise the quiet hand once: hand up, freeze, listen for five seconds. “What did you hear?”

During reading

  • Echo each sound word the way the book says it (Major’s toenails on the sidewalk, Dad’s shoes, the lawnmower, the sprinkler). Keep the echo the same size as your model.
  • After every two or three sounds, raise the quiet hand. Everyone freezes. Smile, then turn the page.
  • At the jackhammer, cover ears: that is a loud sound. In the park, whisper the soft sounds (pigeons, ducks, the woodpecker tapping, crickets, wind in the leaves).
  • Tap the woodpecker on your knee as a four-beat pattern. Class echoes.

After reading

Do our own listening walk sitting down: eyes closed for 20 seconds, then quiet hand. Two or three children name a sound.

Quick check: “What does the girl do so she can hear more?”

Adapt: English learners: show quick pictures (dog, shoe, lawnmower, duck, jet) and point as you read. Wigglers: tiptoe in place during the park pages.

Extend: Take a two-minute hallway listening walk. Back in class, children draw one sound they heard.

Teacher notes

  • Hearts are a picture of the beat. They get the name “ta” in Week 9.
  • Keep woodblock players to three to five so the pulse stays clear.
  • Every child gets a solo turn on Day 3, but invite rather than force.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group C (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Rushing the tick-tock pulse.
  • Making the game about winning instead of listening.

Challenge

Lead tick-tock for the class on the woodblock.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Hickory Dickory Dock

English nursery rhyme · public domain · feel in 6/8 (swinging) · 6/8

Swing the phrase. Do not force 6/8 counting.

Game: Arms swing like a pendulum. Freeze at one o’clock.

Grade 1 experiences 6/8. They do not name it. The body learns the swing.

Hello Everybody

Traditional greeting · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

Sing so as G (or C if that sits better).

Game: Echo by phrase, then greet two children by name on so–mi.

Keep it light. If so is a strain, drop the whole song. The quiet hand starts and stops.
