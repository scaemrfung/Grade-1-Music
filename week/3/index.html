Week 03 · Voice & Echo · September

Echo Me

Copy short claps and short sung patterns. Echo is how Grade 1 learns almost every song.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Class 1 introduces, Class 2 practises, Class 3 applies and checks. Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Class 1 · Introduce

Bring: Hand drum, 1 woodblock

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 5 minCopy cat claps — Four-beat claps, class echoes. Start with ta ta ta ta, then add one rest.
  3. 5 minEcho means copy — I go, you go. Same speed, same number, same tune. No extra claps.
  4. 5 minVoice echo — Sing so–mi on “yoo.” Change the last note sometimes so they listen, not guess.
  5. 5 minHello song review — Sing “Hello Everybody” from week 1 as an echo song.

Use in class

  • 🦜So–mi–la echoVoice echo: play a 3-note so–mi pattern and the class sings it back on “yoo.”

Class 2 · Practise

  4. 6 minRead-aloud · From Head to Toe — Brain break standing beside desks, per the book guide.

Class 3 · Apply & review

Bring: Hand drum, 1 woodblock

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.

  3. 3 minAlone and together — One brave child leads a clap echo. Sitting is fine.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Copy cat claps

Four-beat claps, class echoes. Start easy (ta ta ta ta). Add one rest. Keep a smile. If they rush, slow your model, do not say “you’re rushing.”

Teach

Echo means copy

I go. You go. Same speed, same number, same tune. We do not add extra claps.

Voice echo

Sing so–mi on “yoo.” Class echoes. Change the last note sometimes. They have to listen, not guess.

Engine, Engine

Chant “Engine, engine, number nine” in a speaking voice with a steady beat on laps. Then sing a so–mi version of the first line. Echo by two bars.

Alone and together

Once the class is strong, let one brave child be the leader for a clap echo. Sitting is fine. No one has to stand in front.

Story suggestion

From Head to Toe · Eric Carle

Call-and-echo structure (“Can you do it? I can do it!”) supports echoing claps and sung patterns.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (The World of Eric Carle on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Brain break between Teach and Leader for a turn, standing in own space (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “Every page asks a question: ‘Can you do it?’ Listen for the answer.”
  • “An echo copies exactly: same moves, same number, same speed.”

During reading

  • Read each animal line and model the move four times on a steady beat: penguin turns head, giraffe bends neck, buffalo raises shoulders, monkey waves arms, seal claps hands, gorilla thumps chest (gently), cat arches back, crocodile wiggles hips, camel bends knees, donkey kicks legs, elephant stomps foot.
  • Class answers “I can do it!” on so–mi, then echoes the move exactly four times. No extra moves.
  • On the seal page, clap a four-beat pattern and have the class echo it. On the elephant page, stomp softly: small feet, big listening.

After reading

Sing “Can you do it?” on so–mi with a four-beat clap pattern. Class sings “I can do it!” and echoes it. Then let two or three students lead a pattern, just like the Leader for a turn activity.

Quick check: “Did your echo have the same number of claps as mine, or did you add extra?”

Adapt: Offer a seated version of every move. English learners: touch and name each body part as you read.

Extend: Pairs invent a new animal move and teach it to the class as a four-beat echo.

Activity · Leader for a turn

Circle. Teacher leads three echoes. Then three students each lead one clap pattern. Class copies. Finish with Engine, Engine all together.

Close

Ask: “Does an echo add extra sounds?” No. Stamp when echoes stay the same length as the model.

Materials

  • Drum or lap
  • So–mi ready in your head
  • Space to stand

Teacher notes

  • Wait. The pause before they echo is where listening lives.
  • If they start early, freeze, smile, try again. Do not scold.
  • Keep sung echoes in so–mi this week. La can wait.

Watch-outs

  • Patterns that are too long. Four beats is enough.
  • Talking through their echo. Model, then silence.

Challenge

Sing an echo on “yoo” that goes so–mi–so–mi. Can a friend copy it?

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Engine, Engine Number Nine

Traditional playground chant · public domain · so–mi (or spoken on the beat) · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk the beat like a train. Freeze on the rest.

Teach it spoken on the pulse first, then sung so–mi. Chicago can become a local town.

Hello Everybody

Traditional greeting · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

Sing so as G (or C if that sits better).

Game: Echo by phrase, then greet two children by name on so–mi.

Keep it light. If so is a strain, drop the whole song. The quiet hand starts and stops.
