Week 06 · Sound, Silence & Timbre · Oct 5–9

Silent Beats and Walking and Jogging Beats

We keep the pulse through silence with the Muffin Man, feel walking beats and jogging beats in Big Black Cats, and find up and down in Eency Weency Spider.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Oct 5–9

Full week: every Grade 1 class gets all 3 classes.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. From Week 9 on, each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Weeks 1–8 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Day 1 · Prepare

Bring: Hand drum, whiteboard and marker, muffin pictures

GAMEPLAN book, Day 1: p. 17

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet children on so–mi.
  3. 6 minMuffin Man: the silent beat (book p. 17) — Sing the song. Eight plates on the board: sound where a muffin sits, and where a plate is empty everyone touches their shoulders in silence. Use the book’s chart if you have it.
  4. 6 minBig Black Cats (book p. 17) — Say the cat poem from the book. The class finds which lines match and which differ. Echo it phrase by phrase with a clap on the pulse.
  5. 4 minWalk or jog? (book p. 17) — Take one step on each beat for a walk, and two quick steps on a beat for a jog. Follow the poem’s words: some beats walk and some jog.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Walk and jog to the door on the drum.

Use in class

  • 🤫Find the restMuffin Man silent beat: find where the silence is.
  • 👏Body percussionWalk or jog: step and tap the beats with the body.

Music time: 20 min · plus 5 min devotional

Day 2 · Practice

Bring: Four woodblocks and beaters, xylophone, hand drum

GAMEPLAN book, Day 2: p. 18–19

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet children on so–mi.
  3. 5 minBig Black Cats body percussion (book p. 18) — Clap on walking beats and pat on jogging beats. Keep all other children on body percussion and voice.
  4. 6 minFour woodblocks in the interlude (book p. 18) — Four children play the pulse on woodblocks between verses. Pass the woodblocks around during the interlude so every child gets a turn over the three days.
  5. 5 minEency Weency Spider (book pp. 18–19) — Finger play with the tune. Then discover that the tune goes up and down. One child plays a simple one-mallet pattern up the xylophone and back down while the class sings.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Walk and freeze at the door.

Use in class

  • 🥁Percussion padWoodblocks in the interlude: hear the woodblock before the children play it.
  • 🐦High or low?Eency Weency Spider: does the sound go up or down?

Music time: 20 min · plus 5 min devotional

Day 3 · Transfer

Bring: Hand drum, shaker, and The Very Busy Spider by Eric Carle if you have it

GAMEPLAN book, Day 3: p. 19

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet children on so–mi.
  3. 6 minRead-aloud · The Very Busy Spider (book p. 19) — Use the book guide. Pat the pulse and say the spider’s repeating working line on the beat. When the animals interrupt, hold still for one beat.
  4. 4 minItsy Bitsy Spider movement (book p. 19) — Play the recording named in the book. Follow the leader: copy the pulse actions you lead, then a child leads.
  5. 6 minSilent-beat shoulders + 10-second checks + 10-second checks — Chant a four-beat line of your choice. Pat three beats and touch shoulders on the fourth. While the game runs, give each child a short solo. Rotating check, group B (about 7 children): while the game runs, give each child a 10-second solo: pat three beats on their knees and touch shoulders on the fourth. Mark Y or N on the record sheet.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Silent last beat at the door.

Use in class

  • 💓Steady beatItsy Bitsy movement: a steady beat for the follow-the-leader game.
  • 🧊Freeze danceSilent-beat shoulders: stay still and ready on the silent beat.

Story suggestion

The Very Busy Spider · Eric Carle

A spider keeps working while the farm animals interrupt her. The repeating working line is a pulse to chant and a phrase to feel, and it pairs with the spider songs this week.

How to use this book

When: Day 3, after Eency Weency Spider movement (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “A spider is working very hard. Listen for the line that comes back again and again.”
  • Keep a quiet pulse going on your knees so the repeating line can sit on it.

During reading

  • Read each animal’s question in a different voice (a high voice for the small animals, a low voice for the big ones). Between the question and the spider’s answer, the class holds still for one silent beat.
  • Each time the spider keeps working, the class pats the pulse and says the repeating working line on the beat.
  • When the web is finished, stand and make one big web with arms, then sit.

After reading

Sing Eency Weency Spider once while the class pats the pulse, then freeze on the last word.

Quick check: “Which line kept coming back? Did we keep the pulse through the animals’ questions?”

Adapt: Children can trace the web on their knee while the class chants. Seated children can pat shoulders instead of laps.

Extend: Pairs invent their own “keep working” line of four words and say it on the pulse.

Teacher notes

  • Walking and jogging beats are felt and moved only. The names “ta” and “ti-ti” come in Week 9 and Week 21.
  • A silent beat here is kept by touching shoulders; we write it later, in Week 10.
  • Pitched: one xylophone. Percussion: woodblocks and hand drum (max three types a day).
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group B (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Counting jog steps as two beats (a jog is two quick steps inside one beat).
  • Letting the woodblock group run the pulse faster.

Challenge

Show a four-beat walk and jog pattern for the class to copy.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

The Muffin Man

English nursery rhyme and singing game · public domain · words on the pulse (simplified) · 4/4

Say it on a steady walking pulse; learn the real tune by ear from your voice or the recording.

Game: Eight plates on the board, one muffin on each. Point to a plate on every beat and the class says “yum.” Wipe one muffin away: that plate is a silent beat.

This page shows the words on the pulse, one syllable per beat, so the real tune and rhythm are simplified. The silent-beat game comes from the GAMEPLAN lesson (Oct Week 1, p. 13); the words and game here are our own.

Eency Weency Spider (Itsy Bitsy Spider)

Traditional nursery rhyme with finger play · public domain · words on the pulse (simplified) · 6/8 (feel in two)

Say it on a steady swing; learn the tune by ear from your voice or the recording.

Game: Finger play: fingers climb up the spout, hands wash down as rain, a big sun circle, then climb again. Tap a body part in the pauses between verses.

This page shows the words on the big beats, so the real tune and rhythm are simplified. Feel two big swings in each line, not six small steps. The book’s own arrangement (Oct Week 2, p. 18) is not copied here.
