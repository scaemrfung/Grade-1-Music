Rest in the Body

A silent beat is a rest. We show rests with our bodies and play games where the rest matters.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Oct 5–9

Full week: every Grade 1 class gets all 3 classes.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Story suggestion

The Quiet Book · Deborah Underwood (illus. Renata Liwska)

Celebrates many kinds of quiet—ideal metaphor for musical silence and the rest.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Langworthy Public Library on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, in the idea slot (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “A rest is a beat with no sound. We still feel it.”
  • Practise open hands on the rest: clap, clap, clap, open.

During reading

  • Before each page, the class pats three beats and opens hands on beat 4 (the rest). Then read that kind of quiet.
  • Pause on the pages where the quiet has a still body: hide-and-seek quiet, top of the roller coaster quiet, before the concert starts quiet.
  • Remind them that a rest isn’t a slump. Bodies stay ready.
  • Read the last pages (story time, bedtime, sound asleep) more and more softly.

After reading

Rest game: pat three, rest one, with a new “kind of quiet” pose on each rest.

Quick check: “What does your body do on a rest?”

Adapt: Children can hold up a picture of a “shh” finger during rests. Keep one quiet page per clap pattern so it stays short.

Extend: Children invent their own kind of quiet (“waiting for recess quiet”) and perform a four-beat pattern with one rest.

Activity · Bee Bee pointing game

Sit in a circle. The leader points to one child per beat. Whoever gets “out!” becomes the next leader. Rests are open hands.

Close

Walk to the door; freeze on the rest.

Materials

  • This page on the screen

Teacher notes

  • The word “rest” is fine now; the symbol comes in Week 10.
  • Pointing game: count gently. The child who is “out” becomes the leader, so no one sits out.
  • For partner games, model the moves slowly without the song first.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group B (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Letting the pointing game speed up.
  • Partner games with too many moves at once.

Challenge

Lead the Bee Bee pointing game.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Bee, Bee, Bumblebee

Traditional counting-out rhyme · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Sit in a circle. The leader points to one child on each beat; whoever is pointed to on “out!” becomes the next leader.

Freeze hands on each rest. Keep the pointing slow and kind.

Bow Wow Wow

American folk song · public domain · do–mi–so–la · 2/4

so = G (do = C)

Game: Partners face each other: stamp on “bow wow wow,” shake a finger on “whose dog,” clap, then turn to a new partner on the last rest.

Teach the moves slowly without the song first.
