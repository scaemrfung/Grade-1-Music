This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Class 1 introduces, Class 2 practises, Class 3 applies and checks. Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Start and stop

Breathe together. Hand in: sing “loo” on so. Hand out: rest. Four tries. This is conducting for six-year-olds.

Teach

Unison means one tune

Not faster, not louder than your neighbour. Match. If you cannot hear the person beside you, you are too loud.

Polish the class song

Words, then tune, then both. Fix one muddy phrase by echo. Do not restart the whole song ten times — fix the sticky bar.

Standing tall

Feet quiet, faces toward the conductor. Hands still unless the song has actions.

One sharing take

Sing it through as if someone is listening. Celebrate the stop as much as the start.

Story suggestion

Pete the Cat: I Love My White Shoes · Eric Litwin (illus. James Dean)

Repetitive class song invites everyone to sing the same tune in unison.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read & sing-along (HarperKids on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Warm-up extension or hook, before you polish the class song (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “Pete sings the same song again and again. Can we sing it as one voice, like one big Pete?”
  • Practise hand in (start) and hand out (stop) once.

During reading

  • You sing Pete’s “I love my white shoes” line the first time. On each repeat, bring the class in with your conductor hand and stop them with a closed hand.
  • Before each page turn, ask what colour the shoes will be (red strawberries, blue blueberries, brown mud, wet water). Then sing the new colour together.
  • On “Did Pete cry? Goodness, no!” the class answers in a speaking voice. Then everyone goes back to the singing voice.
  • Unison check: “If you can’t hear the person beside you, you’re too loud.”

After reading

Move to Project 1. Use the same hand in and hand out on the class song. Do two practice runs and one sharing run.

Quick check: “What does unison mean?” (One tune, all together, no one louder or faster.)

Adapt: The words barely change, so English learners can join after one page. Let a child who needs a job hold the colour cards.

Extend: Add a verse with a new colour and a new mess. Sing it in unison.

Activity · Project 1 — class song

Two practice runs, one “sharing” run. Optional: record on a classroom device to send home or play later. No solos required.

Close

Name one thing the class did well (starts, soft voices, still feet). Stamp project 1 when the sharing run holds together.

Materials

  • The class song (See-Saw, Rain Rain, or Hello)
  • Quiet hand ready
  • Optional: record a take for families

Teacher notes

  • Rubric is tiny: singing voice, start/stop, words. Do not grade pitch perfectly in week 6.
  • A child who mouths the words still belongs in the video.
  • If you record, check district photo/video rules first.

Watch-outs

  • Restarting for one cough. Keep going.
  • Picking a brand-new song today. Use one they know.

Challenge

Add one action that matches a word, and keep the tune.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Rain, Rain, Go Away

English nursery rhyme · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Finger rain on the beat. Freeze on the rests.

The classic so–mi reading song. Sign so (higher) and mi (lower) while you sing.

See-Saw

Traditional · public domain (also sung as Saw, Saw, Saw Away) · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Partners hold hands and bend on so, stretch on mi — or the reverse, as long as the class agrees.

A small knee bend on each so keeps the beat honest.

Hello Everybody

Traditional greeting · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

Sing so as G (or C if that sits better).

Game: Echo by phrase, then greet two children by name on so–mi.

Keep it light. If so is a strain, drop the whole song. The quiet hand starts and stops.
