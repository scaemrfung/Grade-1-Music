Week 02 · Voices & Pulse · Sept 8–11

Pulse in the Body

Music has a steady pulse, like a heartbeat. We feel it by patting, walking and leading it, we change the speed, and we notice what stays the same and what is different.

Music room version, written for next year (2027–28) when classes are in the Music room: floor spots, instruments set out, room to move. In a homeroom (this year’s setting): sit at desks or on the carpet, bring the kit, and move in place or in the aisles.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. From Week 9 on, each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Weeks 1–8 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Bring: Hand drum

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.

  3. 6 minTwo Little Apples (book p. 4) — Say the apple rhyme with one action per line. Do one line the same way twice, then change the action on the next line. The class says “same” or “different.”

  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Walk to the door on the drum and freeze.

Story suggestion

Hand, Hand, Fingers, Thumb · Al Perkins (illus. Eric Gurney)

Strong “dum ditty dum” pulse invites tapping a steady beat while reading.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Bookworm Bonny on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, after Hickory, Dickory, Dock (about 4 min).

Before reading

  • Hand on chest: “Feel your heartbeat. Does the drum in this book keep an even beat?”
  • “Listen for the words that repeat.”

During reading

  • First read: everyone pats a steady beat on laps the whole time. Chant “dum ditty dum ditty dum dum dum” together.
  • Let one child play the hand drum on the beat for the monkeys’ drumming pages.
  • Point out that the words change (rings on fingers, apples and plums, banjos that strum, fiddles that zum), but the pats stay even. That even pat is the beat.
  • Second read: a little faster, but still even. Not “as fast as you can.”

After reading

Read it a second time, a little faster. Pats must stay even, not frantic.

Quick check: “Did your pats follow the words or the beat? Which one stayed even?”

Adapt: If laps are too hard, tap shoulders or take steps in place. English learners can join on the repeated “dum ditty” line right away.

Extend: Split the class: half keep the beat on drums or laps, half chant only the words.

Teacher notes

  • Hearts are pictures of the pulse. The word “ta” waits until Week 9.
  • Keep the tempo near a slow walk, about 72–84, and never race.
  • Children who speed up can whisper-pat.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group B (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Speeding up as the class gets excited.
  • Patting the words instead of the pulse.

Challenge

Keep patting the pulse while the teacher stops the drum for four beats. Did you stay steady?

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Hickory Dickory Dock

English nursery rhyme · public domain · feel in 6/8 (swinging) · 6/8

Swing the phrase. Do not force 6/8 counting.

Game: Arms swing like a pendulum. Freeze at one o’clock.

Grade 1 experiences 6/8. They do not name it. The body learns the swing.

Hello Everybody

Traditional greeting · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

Sing so as G (or C if that sits better).

Game: Echo by phrase, then greet two children by name on so–mi.

Keep it light. If so is a strain, drop the whole song. The quiet hand starts and stops.
