Week 02 · Voice & Echo · September

Talking Voice, Singing Voice

Feel the difference between a speaking voice and a singing voice, and find a light singing tone.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Four voices

Whisper “good morning,” speak it, call it (playground voice), then sing it on so–mi. Students copy each one. Name them: whisper, talk, call, sing.

Teach

Talking sits, singing floats

Say “hello” in a talking voice. Now sing “hello” on so–mi. Put a hand on your head for sing, on your chest for talk, so students have a body cue.

We do not shout songs

A shout hurts ears and does not stay in tune. If the class gets loud, drop to a whisper-sing and they will follow.

Match my pitch

Sing so on “loo.” Class matches. Then mi. Then so–mi. Smile and nod for close matches. Do not single out a child who is low — sing beside them later.

Song: Rain, Rain

“Rain, rain, go away” is so–mi. Echo by phrase. Add a gentle finger rain. Keep it light.

Optional story

Mina’s two voices

Mina used her talking voice to tell Sam about her new boots. Then Mrs. Cole sang “hello.” Mina tried to sing, but it came out like a yell. Mrs. Cole whispered the tune. Mina whispered it back. The whisper grew into a small, floating singing voice. Sam heard it and smiled. Two voices: one for words, one for songs.

Story suggestion

Mortimer · Robert Munsch (illus. Michael Martchenko)

Spoken family dialogue contrasts with Mortimer’s sung refrain (“Clang, clang, rattle-bing-bang”).

Buy on Amazon · Listen: Robert Munsch reads it (Robert Munsch (official audio) on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Mid-lesson, after Match my pitch and before the Sort the voice activity (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “Listen for two kinds of voices: people talking and Mortimer singing.”
  • Set the body cues: hand on chest for talk, hand on head for sing.

During reading

  • Every time Mortimer sings “Clang, clang, rattle-bing-bang, gonna make my noise all day,” the class sings it with you on a light so–mi tune, hand on head.
  • When his mother, his father, the 17 brothers and sisters, and then the two police officers tell him “Mortimer, be quiet,” say it in a talking voice, hand on chest. Try the police line in a low, slow talking voice.
  • Each time someone climbs the stairs, pat a slow, steady beat on laps.
  • Point out that the family gets louder and louder, but a shout is not a song. Keep Mortimer’s song light every time. Whisper-sing it at the end when he falls asleep.

After reading

Play Sort the voice with lines from the book. Teacher says “Mortimer, be quiet!” or sings “Clang, clang…”. Students show chest or head. Finish with Rain, Rain.

Quick check: “When Mortimer made his noise, was it a talking voice or a singing voice? How do you know?”

Adapt: Hold up a TALK card and a SING card. The refrain repeats, so new English speakers can join by the second time.

Extend: Invent a new “noise line” for Mortimer and sing it on so–mi. Class echoes.

Activity · Sort the voice

Teacher says or sings a line. Students show talk (hand on chest) or sing (hand on head). Mix them. End by singing Rain, Rain all the way through.

Close

Ask two students to demonstrate talk, then sing, on the word “hello.” Stamp when most hands sort correctly.

Materials

Teacher notes

  • Calling voice (playground) is useful to name so they do not use it for songs.
  • If a child only speaks the song, they are still in. Invite, do not force.
  • Pitch-match in a small group during the activity if one child is lost.

Watch-outs

  • Praising the loudest singer.
  • Skipping the body cue. Grade 1 needs something to hold.

Challenge

Sing your name on so–mi. The class echoes it.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Hello Everybody

Traditional greeting · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

Sing so as G (or C if that sits better).

Game: Echo by phrase, then greet two children by name on so–mi.

Keep it light. If so is a strain, drop the whole song. The quiet hand starts and stops.

Rain, Rain, Go Away

English nursery rhyme · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Finger rain on the beat. Freeze on the rests.

The classic so–mi reading song. Sign so (higher) and mi (lower) while you sing.

Practice studio

So–mi–la piano

G is so, E is mi, A is la. Tap to hear. Grade 1 lives on the first three.
