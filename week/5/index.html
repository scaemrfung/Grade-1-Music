This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Story suggestion

The Listening Walk · Paul Showers (illus. Aliki)

Models quiet listening, stopping to notice sounds, and sing–listen–move–stop classroom routines.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (KidTimeStoryTime on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, in the idea slot (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “The girl in this book does not talk on her walk. Why might that help her hear more?”
  • Practise the quiet hand once: hand up, freeze, listen for five seconds. “What did you hear?”

During reading

  • Echo each sound word the way the book says it (Major’s toenails on the sidewalk, Dad’s shoes, the lawnmower, the sprinkler). Keep the echo the same size as your model.
  • After every two or three sounds, raise the quiet hand. Everyone freezes. Smile, then turn the page.
  • At the jackhammer, cover ears: that is a loud sound. In the park, whisper the soft sounds (pigeons, ducks, the woodpecker tapping, crickets, wind in the leaves).
  • Tap the woodpecker on your knee as a four-beat pattern. Class echoes.

After reading

Silent-beat walk: walk the pulse; freeze whenever the quiet hand goes up, then carry on together.

Quick check: “Which sounds in the book were loud? Which were quiet? What did the silence feel like?”

Adapt: English learners: show quick pictures (dog, shoe, lawnmower, duck, jet) and point as you read. Wigglers: tiptoe in place during the park pages.

Extend: Take a two-minute hallway listening walk. Back in class, children draw one sound they heard.

Teacher notes

  • Silence is not a slump: bodies stay ready.
  • This year’s classes had the earlier Weeks 1–4. Use the pulse review openers in Weeks 5–6.
  • Keep the empty box on beat 4 first; move it only when most freeze well.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group A (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Letting silent beats become talking time.
  • Speeding up after the silence.

Challenge

Move the empty box for the class to read.

Optional faith note

Optional: we use our voices as a gift. We take turns so every voice has room.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Pease Porridge Hot

English nursery rhyme · public domain · rhythm chant (ta and rest) · 2/4

Spoken. Add so–mi later if you wish.

Game: Partner clapping on the tas; open hands on the rest.

The rest after hot and cold is the whole point. Do not skip it.

Engine, Engine Number Nine

Traditional playground chant · public domain · so–mi (or spoken on the beat) · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk the beat like a train. Freeze on the rest.

Teach it spoken on the pulse first, then sung so–mi. Chicago can become a local town.
