Week 05 · Sound, Silence & Timbre · Sept 28–Oct 1

Sound and Silence

Music has sounds and silences. In The Muffin Man every muffin is a sound and an empty plate is a silent beat: we keep the pulse going inside. We meet spider songs, puppets, and our first gentle mallet turns.

This year (2026–27): classes had the earlier Weeks 1–4; use the pulse review openers.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. From Week 9 on, each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Weeks 1–8 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Day 1 · Prepare

Bring: Hand drum, whiteboard and marker, eight muffin pictures (or paper circles) to put on plates

GAMEPLAN book, Day 1: p. 13

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 3 minHello and beat review — This year: walk and pat the pulse to the drum, freeze when it stops, and sing the Hello Song on so–mi. Use this review each time the class has missed earlier weeks.
  3. 5 minThe Muffin Man (book p. 13) — Sing the song in your own voice. Pat the pulse while you sing. A page on this site has the words.
  4. 5 minEight plates, eight muffins — Draw two rows of four plates. Put one muffin on each. Point to the plates one by one on the pulse and the class says “yum” for each. Use these eight yums between verses.
  5. 5 minTake away a muffin — One child wipes a muffin off a plate. Point to the plates on the pulse again: say “yum” where there is a muffin and stay silent but still on the empty plate.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Silent beats at the door: walk to the drum and freeze when it stops.

Use in class

  • 🤫Find the restMuffin Man silent beats: find where the silent beat is.
  • 💓Steady beatEight plates: a steady beat at the book’s walking speed to point to the plates.

Day 2 · Practice

Bring: Pentatonic xylophone and mallets, woodblock, hand drum

GAMEPLAN book, Day 2: p. 14–15

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet children on so–mi.
  3. 5 minFang and Twang (book p. 14) — Use the book’s two puppets for call-and-response hellos on so–mi. One puppet sings, the class and the other puppet answer. Pass the puppets around the circle.
  4. 5 minEency Weency Spider / Itsy Bitsy Spider (book p. 15) — Finger play on the pulse: climb, wash down, sun, climb. Between verses, tap a body part. A page on this site has the words.
  5. 6 minLegs and All on the xylophone (book p. 15) — Say the book’s poem together. One child plays the pulse on the xylophone with a gentle two-mallet bounce while the class pats the pulse; at the end of the line the next child steps in. Everyone gets a turn across the three days.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Walk on the drum and freeze at the door.

Day 3 · Transfer

Bring: Hand drum, tambourine, and The Quiet Book by Deborah Underwood if you have it

GAMEPLAN book, Day 3: p. 16

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet children on so–mi.
  3. 4 minMoving like a spider (book p. 16) — Spiders creep around the room on the drum. Stop the drum and everyone freezes. When it starts again, they creep on, a little faster or slower.
  4. 4 minCriss-Cross Applesauce (book p. 16) — Say the rhyme from the book with partner back actions. Keep the actions on the pulse.
  5. 4 minRead-aloud · The Quiet Book — Use the book guide. Pat three beats, hold still on the fourth, and read a quiet page.
  6. 5 minMuffin Man silent beats + 10-second checks + 10-second checks — Sing The Muffin Man with the plates on the board. Wipe a muffin and the class stays silent but still on that plate. While the game runs, give each child a short solo. Rotating check, group A (about 7 children): while the game runs, give each child a 10-second solo: say “yum” on the pulse for four plates and stay silent but still on the empty one. Mark Y or N on the record sheet.
  7. 1 minLine up on the pulse — Walk and freeze at the door.

Celebrates many kinds of quiet. A good picture of a silent beat: the body stays ready and the pulse carries on inside.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Langworthy Public Library on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 3, after the Applesauce partner game (about 4 min).

Before reading

  • “A silent beat is a beat where we make no sound, but the pulse keeps going inside us.”
  • Practise once: pat three beats on your knees and hold your hands still on the fourth.

During reading

  • Before each page, pat three beats and hold still on the fourth. Then read that kind of quiet.
  • Pick the pages where the quiet is a ready, still body: hide-and-seek quiet, top-of-the-roller-coaster quiet, the quiet just before a surprise.
  • A silent beat is not a slump. Bodies stay tall and ready.

After reading

Pat three beats and hold still on the fourth. Is the beat still there when we are silent?

Quick check: “Where did the silence come in? Did the beat stop, or keep going inside us?”

Adapt: Children can hold up a finger-to-lips picture on the silent beat.

Extend: Children invent their own kind of quiet (“waiting-for-recess quiet”) and show it with three pats and a still body.

Activity · Eight plates, eight muffins

Draw two rows of four plates. Put one muffin on each. Point to the plates one by one on the pulse and the class says “yum” for each. Use these eight yums between verses.

Close

Silent beats at the door: walk to the drum and freeze when it stops.

Teacher notes

  • This year (2026–27): classes had the earlier Weeks 1–4, so use the pulse review at the start of Days 1–2.
  • Silence is not a slump: bodies stay ready.
  • Keep the empty plate on the last beat first; move it only when most are steady.
  • Pitched: one xylophone. Percussion: hand drum, woodblock, tambourine (max three types a day).
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group A (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Letting silent beats become talking time.
  • Speeding up after the silence.

Challenge

Choose which plate will be empty for the class to read.

Optional faith note

Optional: we use our voices as a gift. We take turns so every voice has room.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

The Muffin Man

English nursery rhyme and singing game · public domain · words on the pulse (simplified) · 4/4

Say it on a steady walking pulse; learn the real tune by ear from your voice or the recording.

Game: Eight plates on the board, one muffin on each. Point to a plate on every beat and the class says “yum.” Wipe one muffin away: that plate is a silent beat.

This page shows the words on the pulse, one syllable per beat, so the real tune and rhythm are simplified. The silent-beat game comes from the GAMEPLAN lesson (Oct Week 1, p. 13); the words and game here are our own.

Eency Weency Spider (Itsy Bitsy Spider)

Traditional nursery rhyme with finger play · public domain · words on the pulse (simplified) · 6/8 (feel in two)

Say it on a steady swing; learn the tune by ear from your voice or the recording.

Game: Finger play: fingers climb up the spout, hands wash down as rain, a big sun circle, then climb again. Tap a body part in the pauses between verses.

This page shows the words on the big beats, so the real tune and rhythm are simplified. Feel two big swings in each line, not six small steps. The book’s own arrangement (Oct Week 2, p. 18) is not copied here.

Hello Everybody

Traditional greeting · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

Sing so as G (or C if that sits better).

Game: Echo by phrase, then greet two children by name on so–mi.

Keep it light. If so is a strain, drop the whole song. The quiet hand starts and stops.
