Class 1 introduces, Class 2 practises, Class 3 applies and checks. Your jobs today

Warm-up · Body and breath

Shake out. Tall. One so–mi on loo. Quiet hand. That is the whole warmup — do not tire them.

Teach

This is the share, not a new lesson

No new songs. Remind order. Remind still feet. Remind smile.

If the audience is late

Sing the warmup song sitting. Do not lose the room.

You run it

Announcer (teacher or a child) names piece 1. Conduct. Nod. Piece 2. Piece 3. Thank the audience.

After

Sit. Two comments from you that are specific (“Your rest in the rhythm piece was real silence”).

Story suggestion

Zin! Zin! Zin! A Violin · Lloyd Moss (illus. Marjorie Priceman)

Orchestra gathers and the audience shouts “Encore!”—perfect sharing-concert celebration.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Reading Rainbow (official) on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Before the concert as a calm-down, or after the concert as a celebration (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “We’re going to count the musicians as they join, like our class joining the concert.”
  • “What do audiences shout at the end if they want more?” (Encore!)

During reading

  • Count instruments on fingers as they arrive (solo, duo, trio… up to ten). Talk about how our songs came together one step at a time.
  • When the orchestra walks on stage and waits, the class practises concert posture: tall, still, eyes on the conductor.
  • When the music soars, the class sways gently. Take one deep breath together.
  • At the ending, practise a real audience clap, then a quiet “encore” whisper.

After reading

Run the concert (Project 4). Afterwards, sit and give two specific compliments, like the book’s audience.

Quick check: “What did you do today that real musicians do?”

Adapt: Keep the reading short if nerves are high: just the counting pages. Let anxious children hold the book.

Extend: Children draw their class as the orchestra, with a label for each group’s part.

Activity · Project 4 — share

Do the program. If no visitors, invite another class or perform for the principal on a walk-by. Stuffed animal audience is a real backup and still counts.

Close

Thank the musicians. Stamp project 4. Take a still photo if allowed.

Materials

  • Program on the board
  • Spots marked
  • Audience (buddy class, families, or stuffed animals if needed)

Teacher notes

  • Have water and a tissue. Have a second adult if families come.
  • Rubric: unison, a bit of expression, audience manners. Do not dock a child for a wobble.

Watch-outs

  • Stopping to reteach mid-concert. Keep going.

Challenge

Help a friend find their spot without talking.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Rain, Rain, Go Away

English nursery rhyme · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Finger rain on the beat. Freeze on the rests.

The classic so–mi reading song. Sign so (higher) and mi (lower) while you sing.

Bounce High, Bounce Low

Traditional singing game · public domain · so–mi–la · 2/4

so = G, la = A, mi = E

Game: Soft ball or beanbag. Underhand only. Swap in a class name for Shiloh.

La is the new neighbour above so. Sign it higher, sing it lightly.

Pease Porridge Hot

English nursery rhyme · public domain · rhythm chant (ta and rest) · 2/4

Spoken. Add so–mi later if you wish.

Game: Partner clapping on the tas; open hands on the rest.

The rest after hot and cold is the whole point. Do not skip it.
