This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Weeks 1–19 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Later months use the same approach as each month is rewritten. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Hello Song, so–mi–la

Greet on so–mi–la.

Teach

Rhythm-card ostinato · Otto Goes to the Beach (book pp. 104–105)

Rhythm cards: one student chooses a card as an ostinato for half the class while the others read remaining cards. Otto Goes to the Beach (Todd Parr): read and discuss patience/kindness; discover Poor = ta, Otto = ti-ti; eight students fill board boxes with ta or ti-ti; class speaks “Poor / Otto” phrases; reread with spoken inserts; last time swap “Lucky” for “Poor.” Speak expressively. Homeroom: board boxes; book on the doc cam if available.

I Lost My Glove (book p. 105)

I Lost My Glove (Trinidad) by title and page only — seated circle; find three phrases (1 and 2 the same); echo by phrase; chase game with a glove dropped on the final “drop!”; tagged runner to the mush pot (centre). Homeroom: tight circle beside desks; one glove.

Willum improv · game review · 10-second checks (book p. 106)

Willum (review from April p. 87) by title and page only — class identifies the song; seven students improvise eight measures each on unpitched instruments after each verse while you accompany. Then class chooses favourite singing games to review before summer (book list includes Walk and Stop, Jim Along Josie, Old Grey Cats, Go All Around the Village, Punchinella, Jump Jim Joe — titles only). Book ends with The Finish Line reflection for the teacher. Rotating check, group C (about 7 children): 10-second solo — speak one Poor/Otto phrase or improvise four beats on an unpitched instrument. Mark Y or N.

How to use this book

When: Day 1, as the Otto speech lesson (about 10 min with the rhythm work).

Before reading

  • “Otto wants a friend at the beach. Show a patient wait with quiet hands.”
  • Soft ta on “Poor.”

During reading

  • Speak “Poor Otto” on the pulse when it appears; freeze on lonely pages.
  • Smile on kind/lucky pages.

After reading

Build one eight-box Poor/Otto phrase on the board.

Quick check: “What stayed kind in the story?”

Adapt: Teacher speaks inserts; class only on the last Lucky.

Extend: Swap one child’s name into a ti-ti box.

Activity · Read-aloud · Otto Goes to the Beach

Use the book guide. Poor / Lucky phrases.

Close

Speak “Lucky Otto” once, then walk.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

See-Saw

Traditional · public domain (also sung as Saw, Saw, Saw Away) · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Partners hold hands and bend on so, stretch on mi — or the reverse, as long as the class agrees.

A small knee bend on each so keeps the beat honest.

Pease Porridge Hot

English nursery rhyme · public domain · rhythm chant (ta and rest) · 2/4

Spoken. Add so–mi later if you wish.

Game: Partner clapping on the tas; open hands on the rest.

The rest after hot and cold is the whole point. Do not skip it.

Rhythm pad

Tap a box to cycle ta, ti-ti, rest. Play to hear eight beats.

So–mi–la piano

G is so, E is mi, A is la. Tap to hear. Grade 1 lives on the first three.
