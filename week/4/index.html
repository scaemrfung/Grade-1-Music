So and Mi

Two pitches: so (higher) and mi (lower). Hand signs and a tiny song.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Class 1 introduces, Class 2 practises, Class 3 applies and checks. Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Class 1 · Introduce

Bring: No instruments (voices and body percussion). Music Studio on the screen

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 4 minElevator — Hands low, hum mi; hands high, hum so. Ride it three times. Give the pitches with your voice.
  3. 7 minSo is higher, mi is lower — Show the so and mi hand signs. Copy them silently, then with “so” and “mi.”
  4. 5 minI sing, you sign — You sing so or mi, they sign. Then reverse: you sign, they sing.
  5. 4 minDo not stretch so too high — If so looks like a strain, start the song lower. Check it on the site’s Music Studio piano on the classroom screen if you like. Sing so–mi hellos.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Elevator

Hands low: hum a low mi. Hands high: hum a higher so. Ride the elevator three times. No words yet.

Teach

So is higher, mi is lower

Show the Kodály signs. Mi: flat hand held level, palm down, at chest height. So: flat hand, palm facing your body, fingers pointing sideways, thumb on top, higher at chin or shoulder height. Students copy the signs without sound, then with “so” and “mi.”

I sing, you sign

Teacher sings so or mi. Students only show the hand sign. Then reverse: teacher signs, students sing.

See-Saw

“See-saw, up and down” sits on so–mi. Bounce a gentle knee bend on the beat. Echo, then all.

Do not stretch so too high

If so is a strain, drop the whole song a bit. Grade 1 should not look tight in the neck.

Story suggestion

Rain, Rain, Go Away! (Sing and Read Storybook) · illustrated by Viviana Garofoli

Nursery song commonly taught as a classic so–mi piece for Grade 1.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (The Bookworms on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Close (5 min). The book is the song, so sing it instead of reading it.

Before reading

  • “This whole book is a song. Listen for the words ‘rain, rain’. Which word is higher?”
  • Review the signs: mi is a flat hand, palm down, at the chest; so is a flat hand, palm facing you, thumb on top, higher at the chin.

During reading

  • Sing each page on the Rain, Rain tune. Students show so–mi hand signs on “rain, rain” every time it comes back.
  • Use the changing verses to keep interest: the boys and girls who want to play, flowers that bloom in May, coming again on Saturday, the animals, and the sun that wants to stay.
  • Add gentle finger rain on laps during the rainy pages. When the sun comes out at the end, hands open wide and rest.

After reading

Sing one verse again. Choose one child to be the “hand teacher” while the class sings.

Quick check: Sing “rain, rain” and ask: “Show me the sign for the higher note.” (So.)

Adapt: Children who are not ready to sign can point up for so and down for mi. Everyone can sing just “rain, rain” if the full verse is too much.

Extend: Make up a new verse with a class name or a weekday (“come again on Monday”).

Activity · Sign the song

Sing See-Saw. Students show so and mi while they sing. Then a few students take turns being the “hand teacher” while the class sings.

Close

Silent hand signs: class sings what they see. Two patterns. Stamp when most signs match the sound.

Materials

  • Space for hands
  • See-Saw or Rain, Rain
  • Board: so (high) / mi (low)

Teacher notes

  • Hand signs stay in front of the body, not wildly over the head.
  • If a child reverses so and mi, stand beside them and do it together. Do not quiz them in front of the class.
  • See-Saw, Rain Rain, and Star Light are enough repertoire this week.

Watch-outs

  • Adding la this week. Two pitches is the win.
  • Tiny hand signs no one can see. Make them clear.

Challenge

Teacher points to two friends: they sing a so–mi conversation (“Hi.” “Hi.”).

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

See-Saw

Traditional · public domain (also sung as Saw, Saw, Saw Away) · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Partners hold hands and bend on so, stretch on mi — or the reverse, as long as the class agrees.

A small knee bend on each so keeps the beat honest.

Rain, Rain, Go Away

English nursery rhyme · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Finger rain on the beat. Freeze on the rests.

The classic so–mi reading song. Sign so (higher) and mi (lower) while you sing.

Practice studio

So–mi–la piano

G is so, E is mi, A is la. Tap to hear. Grade 1 lives on the first three.
