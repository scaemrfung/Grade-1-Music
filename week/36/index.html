This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Weeks 1–19 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Later months use the same approach as each month is rewritten. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Hello Song, so–mi–la

Greet on so–mi–la.

Teach

Favourites vote and first requests

Post a short favourites menu of songs and games already taught this year (titles only — no new GAMEPLAN pages). Class votes with quiet hands or stand-up tallies. Sing or play the top one or two requests with the same game rules used earlier in the year. Keep the Hello Song on so–mi–la. Homeroom kit: favourites list on the board; desks as seats for voting; clear one aisle if a circle game wins.

Read-aloud · We Belong to the Drum · respectful listening

Use the book guide. Cultural care: listen and appreciate only; do not teach or copy sacred songs. After the story, a short listening moment with a recording chosen with your school’s guidance — audience manners, quiet hands, notice pulse or mood. Homeroom kit: book on the doc cam if available; listening chairs at desks; no classroom drumming along.

Student leaders · celebrate the year · 10-second checks

Children lead favourite songs or games from the same year-end menu (titles only). Keep turns short so several leaders get a chance. Quick celebrate: class names one thing Grade 1 musicians learned (pulse, ta/ti-ti, so–mi–la, listening manners). Rotating check, group D (about 7 children): while a request game runs, give each child a 10-second solo — start a favourite song on the pulse or lead four beats of a known game. Mark Y or N.

Optional story

The listening chair

Sam wanted to bang along the first time the drum came through the speaker. Ms. Cole lifted the quiet hand. “This drum already has players,” she said. “Today we borrow our ears, not their song.” Sam sat on his hands and heard the pattern he had missed. After, he said the drum was like walking. That was enough.

Story suggestion

We Belong to the Drum · Sandra Lamouche (illus. Azby Whitecalf)

Cree-authored story about the drum, belonging, and culturally safe listening—not a sacred-song how-to. Fits a light year-end listening day.

Cultural care: listen and appreciate only; do not teach or copy sacred songs.

Buy on Amazon · Watch the author read the opening (sample) (Baker & Taylor on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, in the idea slot (about 6–8 min).

Before reading

  • “Nikosis heard the drum before he was born, in his mother’s heartbeat. Put a hand on your heart. Can you feel yours?”
  • “Today our job is to listen and to care.”

During reading

  • Read it straight through as a listening story. No drumming along and no pretend powwow singing.
  • Stop once when Nikosis feels scared at daycare. “How do you think he feels? What might help?”
  • When the teacher plays powwow music and he feels he belongs, ask: “How did the music help him?”
  • Show the Plains Cree words in the book, and credit the author, Sandra Lamouche, and the illustrator, Azby Whitecalf.

After reading

A short listening moment with a recording chosen with your school’s guidance.

Quick check: “How did the music help Nikosis?”

Adapt: Show the pictures slowly and let children point to what they notice. Some children may connect to their own family music. Welcome that without asking them to perform.

Extend: Invite a local Indigenous musician or Elder through your school’s protocols, and follow their lead.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Hello Everybody

Traditional greeting · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

Sing so as G (or C if that sits better).

Game: Echo by phrase, then greet two children by name on so–mi.

Keep it light. If so is a strain, drop the whole song. The quiet hand starts and stops.

Bounce High, Bounce Low

Traditional singing game · public domain · so–mi–la · 2/4

so = G, la = A, mi = E

Game: Soft ball or beanbag. Underhand only. Swap in a class name for Shiloh.

La is the new neighbour above so. Sign it higher, sing it lightly.

Rain, Rain, Go Away

English nursery rhyme · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Finger rain on the beat. Freeze on the rests.

The classic so–mi reading song. Sign so (higher) and mi (lower) while you sing.
