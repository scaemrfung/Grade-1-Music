Your jobs today

Warm-up · Stretch and tap

Slide a long “oooo” with arms opening. Then two short “ti ti” finger taps. Alternate.

Teach

Long takes more time

Hold “luuuu.” Short is “ti-ti.” Both can sit on a beat: one long fills a beat, two shorts share a beat. Say it in kid words: one long sound for one step, two quick sounds for one step.

Draw icons

A long macaroni. Two little dots. No stems required. Grade 1 can write these.

Names come next week

Today: long and short. Tomorrow: ta and ti-ti. Let the ears go first.

Song hunt

Sing Rain, Rain. Which words feel longer? “Rain” can be long. “Go a-way” has shorter bits. Do not over-analyse.

Story suggestion

Charlie Parker Played Be Bop · Chris Raschka

Playful long and short sound syllables make duration audible for young listeners.

Watch read-aloud · Watch Chris Raschka perform it (The Rabbit Hole Kansas City on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Warm-up extension or quick hook (about 4 min). The book is very short, so read or play it twice.

Before reading

  • “Listen for sounds that stretch long and sounds that pop short.”
  • Model a long “ooo” with arms opening and a short “ti” finger tap.

During reading

  • Second time through: stretch arms for long sounds, like the end of “Never leave your cat a-lone” and the buzzing “zznnzznn.”
  • Tap fingers for short sounds: “be bop,” “fisk, fisk,” “lollipop,” “boomba, boomba,” and the one-sound “BANG!”
  • Echo the playful word chains (“overshoes, overshoes…,” “chickadee, chickadee…”). Keep a steady pat underneath.
  • Ask: “Was that word long or short?” Answer with arms or fingers, not voices.

After reading

Compose with yarn: pairs lay long and short sounds into four beats, then say them with book words (long “a-lone,” short “bop bop”).

Quick check: Teacher says “bop” or “a-looone.” Students show short (finger tap) or long (arms). Most correct = stamp.

Adapt: Use the arm and finger gestures for everything so English learners don’t need the words.

Extend: Play 30 seconds of a Charlie Parker recording. Do you hear long notes, short notes, or both?

Activity · Compose with yarn

Pairs get four beats of space on the floor or paper. They lay one long or two shorts in each beat. Clap it. Switch with a neighbour.

Close

Class claps one pair’s pattern. Stamp if they can tell long from short by ear.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Pease Porridge Hot

English nursery rhyme · public domain · rhythm chant (ta and rest) · 2/4

Spoken. Add so–mi later if you wish.

Game: Partner clapping on the tas; open hands on the rest.

The rest after hot and cold is the whole point. Do not skip it.

Engine, Engine Number Nine

Traditional playground chant · public domain · so–mi (or spoken on the beat) · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk the beat like a train. Freeze on the rest.

Teach it spoken on the pulse first, then sung so–mi. Chicago can become a local town.
