The Introduction (Song Doorway)

Many songs start with an introduction: a little doorway before the singing starts. We listen, wait, and come in together.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Nov 30–Dec 4

Full week: every Grade 1 class gets all 3 classes.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. From Week 9 on, each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Weeks 1–8 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Story suggestion

Zin! Zin! Zin! A Violin · Lloyd Moss (illus. Marjorie Priceman)

Introduces orchestra instruments with joyful respect—lead-in to careful instrument handling.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Reading Rainbow (official) on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, in the idea slot (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “One instrument joins at a time. Count them with your fingers.”
  • “Watch how real musicians hold their instruments and wait.”

During reading

  • Hold up a finger for each new player: trombone solo, trumpet duo, French horn trio, cello quartet, violin quintet, flute, clarinet, oboe, bassoon, then the harp makes ten.
  • Mime holding each instrument gently (a slide for trombone, a bow for violin). Then rest hands in laps.
  • When the orchestra comes on stage and waits to begin, everyone sits in instrument “rest position.”
  • At the end, the audience shouts “Encore!” Talk about how careful players get asked back.

After reading

Everyone waits in rest position while you play a 4-beat triangle doorway, then sings Snail, Snail.

Quick check: “What happened before the orchestra played?”

Adapt: Show photos of the book’s instruments. Children who worry about instruments can be the conductor first.

Extend: Sort the book’s instruments by how they make sound: blow, bow, or pluck.

Teacher notes

  • The introduction here is short: 2 or 4 beats.
  • Triangle: hold by the string so it rings.
  • Snail spiral: leader walks slowly.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group A (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Starting before the introduction ends.
  • Spiral walks that wind too tightly.

Challenge

Choose and play the introduction.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Snail, Snail

Traditional · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk in a spiral line on the pulse; wind in, then unwind.

Try it with a triangle introduction (Week 13).

See-Saw

Traditional · public domain (also sung as Saw, Saw, Saw Away) · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Partners hold hands and bend on so, stretch on mi — or the reverse, as long as the class agrees.

A small knee bend on each so keeps the beat honest.
