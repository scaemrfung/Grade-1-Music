Week 13 · Introduction, Phrase & Loud/Quiet · Nov 30–Dec 4

Phrase Echo and Holiday Pulse

We feel how long a musical phrase is by echoing four-measure patterns, sing a so–mi weekend chat, and keep a steady pulse in a December holiday song with a short introduction before the dance.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Nov 30–Dec 4

Full week: every Grade 1 class gets all 3 classes.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Weeks 1–15 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Later months use the same approach as each month is rewritten. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Day 1 · Prepare

Bring: Hand drum

GAMEPLAN book, Day 1: pp. 38–39

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi. Pat a quiet ta under the last phrase.
  3. 5 minRhythmic Training — phrase echo (book p. 38) — In a standing circle, clap a four-measure pattern in 2/4 that ends on the first beat of measure four. Class echoes. Repeat until steady. Homeroom: stay beside desks or on the carpet circle.
  4. 6 minWeekend Fun (book pp. 38–39) — Teach from the book (title and page only). Echo the melody by two-measure phrases. Sing while patting ta. Then a short so–mi chat: you ask who had weekend fun; a few children answer on so–mi. Keep chats short and kind.
  5. 5 minAsk the class — “What is one fun thing you did?” One or two so–mi answers only.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Whisper ta ta ta ta to the door.

Day 2 · Practice

Bring: Hand drum, triangles or finger cymbals (2–4), one bass xylophone or metallophone if you can carry it, Zin! Zin! Zin! A Violin if you have it

GAMEPLAN book, Day 2: pp. 39–40

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi; class pats ta.
  3. 5 minHanukah is Here (book pp. 39–40) — Teach from the book (title and page only). Lead a short, respectful talk about December holidays — invite children who celebrate Hanukkah to share a custom if they wish. Echo the text by phrase. Pat ta while singing (A). Partner hand-game claps on the pulse. B section: tap a half-note pulse (fist on fist) counting to eight. Perform ABA with body first.
  4. 6 minAdd colour (homeroom kit) — Map patting to a gentle two-note pulse on one carried-in barred instrument (teacher or two careful players). Map the fist pulse to triangles or finger cymbals. Rotate short turns. Music room next year: BX/BM lines and more metals.
  5. 5 minRead-aloud · Zin! Zin! Zin! A Violin — Use the book guide. Wait, then play together.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Eight quiet fist pulses, then walk.

Day 3 · Transfer

Bring: Hand drum or recording device for the dance track, Jim Along Josie

GAMEPLAN book, Day 3: p. 40

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi.
  3. 5 minDance for Hanukkah (book p. 40) — Teach Part One body levels: stamp, pat, shoulders, head — eight each. Part Two: partner clap / own clap, then elbow swing. After an eight-beat introduction, the dance repeats. Call that wait the introduction (song doorway). Homeroom: partners beside desks; use a recording if you have Rhythmically Moving 6, or keep a steady drum pulse and count the sets.
  4. 5 minJim Along, Josie review (book p. 40; also on this site) + 10-second checks — Add verses the class suggests (march, jump, crawl). Keep moves on ta. Rotating check, group A (about 7 children): while the game runs, give each child a 10-second solo: clap or drum an eight-beat introduction, then freeze for the class to enter. Mark Y or N on the record sheet.
  5. 6 minPhrase + doorway checks — Echo a four-measure clap phrase. Checked children play an eight-beat introduction for the class, then everyone comes in on Jim Along.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Jim Along steps to the door.

How to use this book

When: Day 2, after Hanukah is Here body work (about 5 min).

Before reading

  • “Listen for when each new instrument joins. That wait can feel like a doorway.”
  • Pat a quiet ta on your knees.

During reading

  • Each time a new instrument arrives, freeze for one silent beat, then pat again.
  • At the end, whisper “we waited, then we played.”

After reading

Four triangle taps (doorway), then pat ta ta ta ta.

Quick check: “What happened before the whole group played?”

Adapt: Seated children can finger-tap the pulse on their palms.

Extend: A child chooses triangle or drum for a four-beat doorway; the class enters on ta.

Activity · Ask the class

“What is one fun thing you did?” One or two so–mi answers only.

Close

Whisper ta ta ta ta to the door.

Teacher notes

  • GAMEPLAN December Week One (pp. 38–40). Weekend Fun and Hanukah is Here are listed by title and page only — no book melody or text copied here.
  • Ta was named in November; keep using the word. Phrase length is felt by echo, not by naming “A/B” yet.
  • Inclusive note: invite Hanukkah sharing; do not require it. Families celebrate differently.
  • Pitched: one barred instrument if you can carry it. Metals: a few triangles or finger cymbals.

Watch-outs

  • Starting before the introduction ends.
  • Partner claps that rush off the pulse.
  • Long weekend chats that eat the minutes.

Challenge

Lead the eight-beat introduction for Dance for Hanukkah.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Jim Along Josie

American play-party song · public domain · do–mi–so · 2/4

so = G (do = C)

Game: Walk a circle; the leader changes the move (march, tiptoe, hop) each verse.

March in 2: strong on beat 1.

Hello Everybody

Traditional greeting · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

Sing so as G (or C if that sits better).

Game: Echo by phrase, then greet two children by name on so–mi.

Keep it light. If so is a strain, drop the whole song. The quiet hand starts and stops.
