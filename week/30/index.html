This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Story suggestion

Pete the Cat: I Love My White Shoes · Eric Litwin (illus. James Dean)

Repetitive class song invites everyone to sing the same tune in unison.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read & sing-along (HarperKids on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, in the idea slot (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “Pete sings the same song again and again. Can we sing it as one voice, like one big Pete?”
  • Practise hand in (start) and hand out (stop) once.

During reading

  • You sing Pete’s “I love my white shoes” line the first time. On each repeat, bring the class in with your conductor hand and stop them with a closed hand.
  • Before each page turn, ask what colour the shoes will be (red strawberries, blue blueberries, brown mud, wet water). Then sing the new colour together.
  • On “Did Pete cry? Goodness, no!” the class answers in a speaking voice. Then everyone goes back to the singing voice.
  • Unison check: “If you can’t hear the person beside you, you’re too loud.”

After reading

Pete’s line repeats: draw a repeat sign around it on the board.

Quick check: “Which part did we sing again?”

Adapt: The words barely change, so English learners can join after one page. Let a child who needs a job hold the colour cards.

Extend: Add a verse with a new colour and a new mess. Sing it in unison.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Rain, Rain, Go Away

English nursery rhyme · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Finger rain on the beat. Freeze on the rests.

The classic so–mi reading song. Sign so (higher) and mi (lower) while you sing.

See-Saw

Traditional · public domain (also sung as Saw, Saw, Saw Away) · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Partners hold hands and bend on so, stretch on mi — or the reverse, as long as the class agrees.

A small knee bend on each so keeps the beat honest.
