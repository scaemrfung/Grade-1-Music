This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Weeks 1–19 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Later months use the same approach as each month is rewritten. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Hello Song, so–mi–la

Greet on so–mi–la.

Teach

Little Red Caboose (book pp. 89–90)

Little Red Caboose by title and page only — echo while clapping the cowbell rhythm; two train lines follow engineers (trains may not cross); last student in each line plays a cowbell; class invents a four-beat speech ostinato (“All aboard!”) repeated four times as B; engineers rotate to the caboose. Homeroom: one line in the aisle; one cowbell.

Notating SML · Drip, Drop (book pp. 90–91)

Review three-line staff so–mi–la (idea from p. 74). Echo short fragments (Acka Backa or Hammer) then notate. Prepare “la–so–mi–so” and extend into Drip, Drop by title and page only — flower word-chain B spoken twice; ABA with chord bordun and one-hand glockenspiel if available; rain stick free during A. Homeroom: paper staffs on clipboards; one barred instrument.

All Around the Buttercup (book p. 91)

All Around the Buttercup by title and page only — echo melody; discuss buttercup and clever; circle game: leader travels inside on A; on B the leader points on the pulse and the last child becomes the new leader with a new locomotor move (cumulative followers). Homeroom: smaller circle; tip-toe and march only if space is tight.

How to use this book

When: Day 1, before Little Red Caboose (about 5 min).

Before reading

  • “Trains have a caboose at the end. Show me the end of our line.”
  • Pat a slow train pulse.

During reading

  • Chug on laps as each car colour passes.
  • Freeze on the caboose page.

After reading

Whisper “all aboard” four times as a speech B.

Quick check: “Where is the caboose?”

Adapt: Seated chug only.

Extend: Two colour teams chug on A only.

Activity · Read-aloud · Freight Train

Use the book guide. Train pulse.

Close

Chug four beats, then walk.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Rain, Rain, Go Away

English nursery rhyme · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Finger rain on the beat. Freeze on the rests.

The classic so–mi reading song. Sign so (higher) and mi (lower) while you sing.

See-Saw

Traditional · public domain (also sung as Saw, Saw, Saw Away) · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Partners hold hands and bend on so, stretch on mi — or the reverse, as long as the class agrees.

A small knee bend on each so keeps the beat honest.

Practice studio

So–mi–la piano

G is so, E is mi, A is la. Tap to hear. Grade 1 lives on the first three.
