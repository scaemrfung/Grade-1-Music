Week 10 · Ta, Tempo & Contour · Nov 2–6

Ta and Rest

We keep ta steady and add a silent beat we can see: rest. Squirrel songs and a train chart give us practice. We also meet a repeat sign so a pattern can be spoken twice.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Nov 2–6

Full week: every Grade 1 class gets all 3 classes.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Weeks 1–12 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Later months use the same approach as each month is rewritten. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Day 1 · Prepare

Bring: Hand drum (optional squirrel puppet)

GAMEPLAN book, Day 1: pp. 29–30

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi; pat ta underneath.
  3. 5 minThe Squirrel poem (book p. 29) — Read the poem from the book. Class guesses the animal. Second time, children hop, twirl, and crouch in place as the words suggest — keep a quiet ta in the feet.
  4. 6 minChase the Squirrel (book pp. 29–30) — Teach the song from the book (title and page only). Pat the pulse while you sing. Two children at a carried-in xylophone play a gentle two-note pulse (chord bordun feel) under the singing; others wait in a short line. Homeroom: one or two instruments; Music room next year: four barred instruments in short lines.
  5. 5 minHop, Old Squirrel (book p. 30) — Sing from the book. Hop three times on “Hop old squirrel,” then freeze and wiggle on the nonsense words. Choose a quiet unpitched sound (shaker or woodblock) for the nonsense bits.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Hop on ta to the door; freeze on rest.

Use in class

  • 🧊Freeze danceThe Squirrel: freeze between poem lines.
  • 🌈Xylophone & bellsChase the Squirrel: preview the two bars for the pulse.

Music time: 20 min · plus 5 min devotional

Day 2 · Practice

Bring: Hand drum, train/choo-choo cards or paper rectangles, board

GAMEPLAN book, Day 2: pp. 30–31

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi.
  3. 5 minEngine Engine with train cards (book p. 31) — Review Engine Engine. Show four cards (two “train” = ta, two “choo-choo” spoken as two even sounds on one beat — feel only; we name ti-ti in February). Build a four-beat row. Open hands on an empty car for rest.
  4. 6 minLabel the repeat sign (book p. 31) — Point to the book’s repeat sign (or draw :|| ). Speak the row twice without stopping. Sing–speak–sing (ABA): sing Engine Engine, speak the row twice, sing again.
  5. 5 minAmerica (book p. 31) — Introduce from the book (patriotic repertoire; title and page only). Soft listening or echo of the first phrase if time is short. November is a good time to plant it for later review.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Speak a ta/rest row once, then walk.

Use in class

  • 🔢Rhythm makerTrain cards: build a row with a rest.
  • 🔁Repeat signRepeat sign: speak the row twice.

Bring: Hand drum, xylophone, This Jazz Man if you have it

  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi.
  3. 5 minChase the Squirrel turns — Rotate two players on the two-note pulse while the class sings. Keep mallets relaxed.

How to use this book

When: Day 3, after Chase the Squirrel turns (about 5 min).

Before reading

  • “This book is a song you already know, ‘This Old Man.’ Listen for the numbers — each one can sit on a ta.”
  • Show four cards: ta ta ta rest.

During reading

  • Sing each verse. Students pat ta on every beat.
  • On “give the man a hand,” clap three tas and open hands on the rest.
  • Echo a short musician sound, then hold still for one silent beat.

After reading

Clap ta ta ta rest from the cards, then play it once on the drum.

Quick check: “Where was the silent beat?”

Adapt: Whisper the verses if voices get loud.

Extend: A child places the rest card in a new box for the class to clap.

Activity · Hop, Old Squirrel (book p. 30)

Sing from the book. Hop three times on “Hop old squirrel,” then freeze and wiggle on the nonsense words. Choose a quiet unpitched sound (shaker or woodblock) for the nonsense bits.

Close

Hop on ta to the door; freeze on rest.

Teacher notes

  • Name ta and rest this week. Two sounds on one beat can be felt in “choo-choo,” but the name ti-ti waits until Week 21.
  • Repeat sign: keep the drawing simple (:||) and say “go back and do it again.”
  • Pitched: one xylophone. Unpitched: hand drum plus one shaker or woodblock.
  • America is repertoire only — no full score on this site.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group B (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Clapping on the rest.
  • Overplaying the xylophone.
  • Skipping the repeat.

Challenge

Build a new four-beat row with one rest for the class.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Engine, Engine Number Nine

Traditional playground chant · public domain · so–mi (or spoken on the beat) · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk the beat like a train. Freeze on the rest.

Teach it spoken on the pulse first, then sung so–mi. Chicago can become a local town.

Hello Everybody

Traditional greeting · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

Sing so as G (or C if that sits better).

Game: Echo by phrase, then greet two children by name on so–mi.

Keep it light. If so is a strain, drop the whole song. The quiet hand starts and stops.
