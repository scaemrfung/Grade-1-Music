Ta and Rest

We read and play ta and rest. A rest is a silent beat we can see on a card.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Nov 2–6

Full week: every Grade 1 class gets all 3 classes.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. From Week 9 on, each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Weeks 1–8 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Story suggestion

This Jazz Man · Karen Ehrhardt (illus. R.G. Roth)

Counting chant (“this jazz man, he plays one”) supports one sound per beat (ta) while singing.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read/sung aloud (KidTimeStoryTime on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, in the idea slot (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “This book is a song you already know, ‘This Old Man.’ Listen for the numbers.”
  • Put four tas on the board: ta ta ta ta.

During reading

  • Sing each verse. Students step or pat ta on every beat.
  • On “give the man a hand,” clap four even tas while you point to the board.
  • Echo each musician’s sound words (snap-snap for the thumb, tap-shuffle for the shoes, bippity bop for the conga). The ta beat stays steady on laps underneath.
  • At ten, the band plays “again,” so sing the ending one more time with everyone clapping tas.

After reading

Read a card row: ta ta ta rest, clap it, then play it on the drum.

Quick check: “Where was the silent beat?”

Adapt: Children who can’t sing and step at the same time can just step the beat. The counting helps English learners.

Extend: The back of the book names the real players (Louis Armstrong, Duke Ellington, Charlie Parker, and more). Play 20 seconds of one of them.

Teacher notes

  • Rest card: use the one you prefer (quarter rest or “sh”). Keep it the same all year.
  • Pairs share one set of 4 cards.
  • Play rows on one drum and one woodblock only, passed around.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group B (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Clapping on the rest.
  • Too many cards at once.

Challenge

Build a row with two rests and clap it.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Pease Porridge Hot

English nursery rhyme · public domain · rhythm chant (ta and rest) · 2/4

Spoken. Add so–mi later if you wish.

Game: Partner clapping on the tas; open hands on the rest.

The rest after hot and cold is the whole point. Do not skip it.

Rain, Rain, Go Away

English nursery rhyme · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Finger rain on the beat. Freeze on the rests.

The classic so–mi reading song. Sign so (higher) and mi (lower) while you sing.
