Strong Beats and Quiet Beats

Some beats feel heavier. We can show a strong beat without getting loud and wild.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Your jobs today

Warm-up · March in place

Left is stronger, right is lighter. “STRONG, quiet, STRONG, quiet.” Keep it small so knees stay safe.

Teach

Strong is not shout

A strong beat is a little heavier, not angry. Model a tiny knee bend on 1.

Groups of 2

See-Saw. Bend on “See,” light on “saw.” Pat-clap. Pat is strong.

Groups of 4

Engine, Engine. Strong on “En-gine” downbeats you choose — keep it simple: beat 1 is stronger, 2 3 4 stay light.

Conducting

Teacher’s hand is bigger on the strong beat. Students’ bodies match.

Story suggestion

The Loud Book! · Deborah Underwood (illus. Renata Liwska)

Catalogues many kinds of loud—pair with soft moments to contrast strong vs quiet beats.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Kellogg Public Library on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Brain break before Statue on 1 (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “This book has lots of different louds. Is a strong beat a yelling beat?”
  • Model STRONG–light–light–light with a tiny knee bend on beat 1.

During reading

  • After each “loud” (alarm clock loud, applause loud, parade in the park loud, Aunt Tilly’s banjo band loud, fireworks loud), pat four beats. Make beat 1 heavier, not louder.
  • For applause loud, clap beat 1 strong and beats 2–4 light. For parade in the park loud, march in place: STRONG, light.
  • On “deafening silence loud,” freeze for four beats.
  • If voices get wild, drop to a whisper. “Strong is not shout.”

After reading

Statue on 1: walk the beat with a tiny freeze-frame on each strong beat. Then sit and pat-clap See-Saw (pat = strong).

Quick check: “Is the strong beat the yelling beat?” (No. It’s a little heavier.)

Adapt: Use a big hand and a small hand to show strong and light. Children can tap strong beats on a drum and light beats on their knee.

Extend: Pair with The Quiet Book (week 16). Which kinds of quiet could still have a strong beat?

Activity · Statue on 1

Walk the beat. On each strong beat, a tiny freeze-frame, then keep going. Silly but controlled. Then sit and pat-clap a known song.

Close

Ask: “Is the strong beat the yelling beat?” No. Stamp if strong/weak is visible in their bodies.

Materials

  • Drum
  • A 2-beat song (See-Saw) and a 4-beat chant
  • Board: STRONG quiet STRONG quiet

Teacher notes

  • Do not say 2/4 and 4/4. Say groups of two and groups of four.
  • If they stomp, take shoes off the stomp and go back to finger pats.

Watch-outs

  • Only playing the strong beats. Weak beats matter.

Challenge

Conduct the class with a big hand on 1 and small hands on the rest.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

See-Saw

Traditional · public domain (also sung as Saw, Saw, Saw Away) · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Partners hold hands and bend on so, stretch on mi — or the reverse, as long as the class agrees.

A small knee bend on each so keeps the beat honest.

Engine, Engine Number Nine

Traditional playground chant · public domain · so–mi (or spoken on the beat) · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk the beat like a train. Freeze on the rest.

Teach it spoken on the pulse first, then sung so–mi. Chicago can become a local town.

Practice studio

Rhythm pad

Tap a box to cycle ta, ti-ti, rest. Play to hear eight beats.
