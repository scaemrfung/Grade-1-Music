This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Story suggestion

Clap Your Hands · Lorinda Bryan Cauley

Clap, stomp, and move cues map directly to clap–pat–stamp–snap body percussion.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Brightly Storytime on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, in the idea slot (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “Our four body sounds: clap, pat, stamp, snap (or finger tap). Listen for which one the book asks for.”
  • “We should still hear the words over our sounds.”

During reading

  • Read the rhyming commands on a steady beat. Do each one four times: clap your hands, stomp your feet (gently), slap your knee (pat), wiggle your fingers (snap or tap).
  • After each rhyming pair, play a four-beat body ostinato: clap–pat–clap–rest.
  • On “quiet as a mouse, now don’t say a word,” freeze for a silent beat.
  • Skip or change the lines that don’t fit a music room (blow a kiss instead of a kiss, no somersault: spin once slowly).

After reading

Four-beat body pattern: clap–pat–clap–rest, then jingle the pulse.

Quick check: “Which moves were on the pulse?”

Adapt: Offer seated versions of every move. English learners can copy your actions without the words.

Extend: Children write a four-beat body pattern with icons (hand, lap, foot) and the class performs it.

Teacher notes

  • Families celebrate differently; this is a winter song, not a religious one. Offer another winter song if needed.
  • Keep the chorus only.
  • Shakers: rest on the floor between turns.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group B (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Rushing the chorus.
  • Shaking constantly instead of on the pulse.

Challenge

Lead the class moves for the pulse.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Jingle Bells (chorus, first phrase)

James Lord Pierpont, 1857 · public domain · do–re–mi–so · 4/4

mi = E (do = C)

Game: Jingle shakers or bells on the pulse while half the class sings.

A winter song, not a religious one. The chorus only.

Engine, Engine Number Nine

Traditional playground chant · public domain · so–mi (or spoken on the beat) · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk the beat like a train. Freeze on the rest.

Teach it spoken on the pulse first, then sung so–mi. Chicago can become a local town.
