Week 14 · Introduction & Phrase · Dec 7–11

Toys, Marionettes and Pulse

A toy theme carries the week: we move like marionettes to a famous ballet pulse, speak toy word-chains on unpitched instruments, play Jack-in-the-Box, and prepare so–mi with Teddy Bear.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Dec 7–11

Full week: every Grade 1 class gets all 3 classes.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Weeks 1–19 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Later months use the same approach as each month is rewritten. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Day 1 · Prepare

Bring: Hand drum, optional Nutcracker / Sugar Plum Fairy recording

GAMEPLAN book, Day 1: p. 41

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi. Clap a short phrase-echo warm-up (review book p. 38 idea).
  3. 5 minMarionette Game (book p. 41) — Talk about marionettes vs other puppets. Move one “hinge” at a time (wrist, elbow, knee) on a quarter-note pulse while you drum or play Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy. First theme: bend big hinges. Accents: slump and recover. Soft ending: tiny finger/wrist moves. Homeroom: stay in self-space beside desks; Music room next year: more scatter space.
  4. 6 minAsk the class — “Have you heard this music before? When?” Keep answers short.
  5. 5 minPulse freeze — Move on ta; freeze when the drum stops.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Tiny marionette steps to the door.

Use in class

  • 💓Steady beatMarionette Game: quarter-note hinge moves.
  • 🧊Freeze dancePulse freeze: move on ta, freeze on stop.

Music time: 20 min · plus 5 min devotional

Day 2 · Practice

Bring: Toy picture cards or board sketches (soldier, airplane, rocking horse, bike), tambourine, woodblock, cowbell or similar, vibraslap or shaker, Clap Your Hands if you have it

GAMEPLAN book, Day 2: pp. 42–43

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi.
  3. 4 minTime for Toys (book pp. 42–43) — Teach from the book (title and page only). Show four toy cards. Speak each toy’s rhythm (soldier, airplane, rocking horse, bike). Bike stays last so the chain ends on beat seven. Four children hold cards; class speaks the chain twice. Then song (A) twice and word chain (B) twice. Homeroom kit: put four unpitched instruments on the floor with a card beside each; first player in each short line plays only their toy’s rhythm; rotate back during the song.
  4. 7 minRead-aloud · Clap Your Hands — Use the book guide. Moves on the pulse.
  5. 5 minSame or different — Speak two toy chains; class shows same or different with arms.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Speak “bike” on the last beat, then walk.

Use in class

  • 🥁Percussion padTime for Toys: unpitched toy rhythms.
  • 👏Body percussionClap Your Hands: body levels on the pulse.

Music time: 20 min · plus 5 min devotional

Day 3 · Transfer

Bring: Ratchet or scraper, hand drum, Teddy Bear

GAMEPLAN book, Day 3: p. 43

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi.
  3. 5 minJack-in-the-Box (book p. 43) — Speak the poem from the book (title and page only) while tapping shoulders on the pulse. Press a “Jack,” mime winding, then a drum pop. That child becomes the next leader. Add ratchet for wind and drum for pop. Extension: 1, then 2, then 4 Jacks.
  4. 5 minTeddy Bear (book p. 43; also on this site) + 10-second checks — Sing with the usual actions. On “Teddy bear,” show high–high–low (head, head, shoulders). Perform three times: sing / think (inner hearing) with actions / sing — same, different, same. Rotating check, group B (about 7 children): while the game runs, give each child a 10-second solo: play one toy rhythm once on an unpitched instrument, then freeze. Mark Y or N on the record sheet.
  5. 6 minToy + Teddy checks — Checked children play one toy rhythm on an unpitched instrument, then show high-high-low for “Teddy bear.”
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Teddy Bear tip-toe to the door.

Use in class

  • 🐱Copycat songsTeddy Bear: actions and high-high-low.
  • 🐦High or low?Teddy checks: high and low on “Teddy bear.”

Music time: 20 min · plus 5 min devotional

How to use this book

When: Day 2, after Time for Toys word chain (about 5 min).

Before reading

  • “Every move in this book sits on the pulse — like our toy rhythms.”
  • Practise clap–pat–stamp once, still even.

During reading

  • Do each move four times on ta.
  • When the book changes the move, change with it — keep the pulse.

After reading

Clap four tas, pat four tas, freeze.

Quick check: “Did the moves stay with the pulse?”

Adapt: Stomp can be a gentle heel tap beside desks.

Extend: A child picks the next body level; the class matches for four tas.

Activity · Pulse freeze

Move on ta; freeze when the drum stops.

Close

Tiny marionette steps to the door.

Teacher notes

  • GAMEPLAN December Week Two (pp. 41–43). Time for Toys and Jack-in-the-Box by title and page only. Teddy Bear is scored on this site.
  • Toy rhythms may include two sounds on one beat — feel them; the name ti-ti is formalised in February.
  • Unpitched kit: four different sounds is enough (tambourine, woodblock, cowbell, shaker/vibraslap).
  • Sugar Plum Fairy: preview the recording; keep moves non-locomotor in a homeroom.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group B (Day 3).
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group B (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Toy chains that forget bike at the end.
  • Locomotor dancing in a tight homeroom.
  • Shouting on high Teddy notes.

Challenge

Lead Jack-in-the-Box once (wind and pop).

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Teddy Bear

Traditional action song · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Turn around, touch the ground, show your shoe, that will do — one action per phrase.

Actions stay small so the tune survives.

Jim Along Josie

American play-party song · public domain · do–mi–so · 2/4

so = G (do = C)

Game: Walk a circle; the leader changes the move (march, tiptoe, hop) each verse.

March in 2: strong on beat 1.
