This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Weeks 1–19 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Later months use the same approach as each month is rewritten. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Hello Song, so–mi–la

Greet on so–mi–la.

Teach

Rhythm cards · All About Success · Which Melody (book p. 95)

Rhythm cards (review idea from p. 56): speak “ti-ti” on eighths; internalize quarter notes and rests; point beat by beat left to right. All About Success by title and page only — substitute your school name or mascot; echo contour on the solfege ladder; add text one measure at a time; class sings, then teacher (then volunteers) improvise short so–mi–la fragments with hand signs for the class to echo. Which Melody Do You Hear?: teacher sings so–mi–la fragments with hand signs; class echoes and identifies the matching pattern on the chart — prepare for Written Assessment #4. Homeroom: ladder card and rhythm cards on the board.

Assessing So–Mi–La · Mexican Counting Song (book pp. 96–97)

Written Assessment #4 (Appendix D idea) if you are assessing: students echo a so–mi–la fragment with hand signs, then circle the matching notation. Mexican Counting Song by title and page only — count to ten in Spanish with pronunciation support; speak A rhythmically then add melody and B (“La, la, la…”); partners show fingers on A and elbow-swing on B. Homeroom: partners standing beside desks; no need to copy book notation onto the board.

Allison’s Camel · share + 10-second checks (book p. 97)

Allison’s Camel (traditional): seated circle copy-cat game — pat, push hands away, sway; repeat while decreasing humps from ten to one; coda “So Allison has a horse, of course!” Rotating check, group D (about 7 children): while the camel game runs, give each child a 10-second solo — echo one so–mi–la hand-sign pattern from All About Success. Mark Y or N.

How to use this book

When: Day 3, before Allison’s Camel (about 5 min).

Before reading

  • “Gerald learns his own dance. Show a shy giraffe neck, then a brave sway.”
  • Soft so–mi on “dance.”

During reading

  • Sway on jungle-dance pages; freeze when Gerald feels left out.
  • Cheer quietly when he finds his rhythm.

After reading

Echo one so–mi–la success pattern with hand signs.

Quick check: “Did Gerald give up or try again?”

Adapt: Seated sway only.

Extend: One child leads a four-beat sway; class copies.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Hickory Dickory Dock

English nursery rhyme · public domain · feel in 6/8 (swinging) · 6/8

Swing the phrase. Do not force 6/8 counting.

Game: Arms swing like a pendulum. Freeze at one o’clock.

Grade 1 experiences 6/8. They do not name it. The body learns the swing.

Jack and Jill

English nursery rhyme · public domain · do–mi–so–la · 6/8 (feel in two)

so = G (do = C)

Game: Swing arms on the two big beats of each line.

Simplified here to one note per word; feel two big beats per bar, not six.

So–mi–la piano
