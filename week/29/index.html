Your jobs today

Warm-up · Follow my feet

Walk slow, walk faster, freeze. Then clap piano (quiet) and forte (loud) — use the kid words plus the music words once.

Teach

Two pairs

Fast/slow is speed. Loud/quiet is volume. Do not mix the cards at first.

The song stays the same

Rain, Rain slow. Rain, Rain faster. Same tune. Then quiet vs strong. Same tune.

Conductor tools

Small hand = quiet. Big hand = stronger. Slow scoop vs quick tick.

Piano and forte

Say the words once. They do not need to spell them. Quiet and loud remain on the cards.

Story suggestion

Trombone Shorty · Troy Andrews (illus. Bryan Collier)

Energetic New Orleans music story supports talking about tempo and dynamics in performance.

Buy on Amazon · Watch Angela Bassett read it (Storyline Online on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Hook for Teach (about 7 min). It’s a longer read, so show key pages and summarise the middle.

Before reading

  • “Listen for fast music and slow music, loud music and quiet music in Troy’s story.”
  • Hold up the four cards: fast, slow, loud, quiet.

During reading

  • Every time you read “Where y’at?” the class echoes it. Use the cards to change it: quiet, loud, slow, fast.
  • On the Mardi Gras parade pages, march in place with a faster beat. For Troy practising alone at night, use a slow, quiet beat.
  • When he picks up a broken trombone twice his size, do slow arm slides for the trombone. Then pick up the tempo as the parade passes.
  • When he plays along with Bo Diddley, the class follows your conductor hand: big for loud, small for quiet.

After reading

Four-card song: a student shows a card and the class sings Rain, Rain that way. Rotate helpers.

Quick check: “Fast and slow is about ___? Loud and quiet is about ___?” (Speed. Volume.)

Adapt: Keep only two cards at first (fast/slow). English learners can match arm size and speed without the words.

Extend: Listen to 20 seconds of a New Orleans brass band. Is it fast or slow? Loud or quiet?

Activity · Four-card song

A student helper shows a card. Class sings the known song that way. Rotate helpers.

Close

One child conducts quiet to loud on a long loo. Stamp if the class actually changes.

Materials

  • Known song
  • Cue cards: fast / slow / loud / quiet
  • Drum

Teacher notes

  • Faster + louder often glue together. Pull them apart with separate rounds.
  • Never ask Grade 1 for fortissimo. Strong and kind is the ceiling.

Watch-outs

  • Faster meaning sloppy. Keep the words clear.

Challenge

Change in the middle of the song, not just at the start.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Hickory Dickory Dock

English nursery rhyme · public domain · feel in 6/8 (swinging) · 6/8

Swing the phrase. Do not force 6/8 counting.

Game: Arms swing like a pendulum. Freeze at one o’clock.

Grade 1 experiences 6/8. They do not name it. The body learns the swing.

Teddy Bear

Traditional action song · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Turn around, touch the ground, show your shoe, that will do — one action per phrase.

Actions stay small so the tune survives.
