Story suggestion

My Family Plays Music · Judy Cox (illus. Elbrite Brown)

Many instruments named and pictured—supports name/sound/picture instrument ID.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Grandma “B” Reads with Me on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Hook for Teach (about 7 min). Have the real instruments or picture cards ready.

Before reading

  • “The girl in this book plays with everyone in her family. Listen for her instrument on each page.”
  • “Is it hit, shaken, or scraped?”

During reading

  • When she names her instrument, hold up the real one (or the picture). Play it four times on the beat: tambourine with Mom, triangle with Dad, cymbals with her sister, cowbell with her brother, wood block with her aunt, maracas with her uncle, rhythm sticks with Grandpa.
  • The class names each one and shows the action: hit, shake, or scrape.
  • Point out the family’s big instruments too (fiddle, cello, clarinet, guitar, tuba). Their pictures help with the name game.
  • At the end she says she’s a percussionist. Talk about what that word means.

After reading

Match the table: hear an instrument, then walk to its picture card, or use the site quiz in pairs.

Quick check: Play four sounds behind a screen. Students name each one.

Adapt: Put the word under each picture card. English learners can point to answer.

Extend: Children list a family member’s favourite music and pick a percussion instrument to play along.

Activity · Match the table

Stations: hear, then walk to the picture card. Or a paper match on the worksheet. Then the site quiz in pairs if devices exist — otherwise teacher-led on the projector.

Close

Four sounds, four names, fast. Stamp if most matches are right.

Materials

  • 3–6 real instruments or pictures
  • Studio quiz on this site
  • A cloth to hide one instrument

Teacher notes

  • Include at least one instrument they have not played yet so listening stays sharp.
  • If you have a guest from a local ensemble, this is a good week — but the lesson stands without one.

Watch-outs

  • Ten instruments. Four is a Grade 1 set.

Challenge

Be the mystery player. Class guesses with eyes closed.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Bell Horses

Traditional · public domain · so–mi–la · 2/4

so = G

Jingle bells on the beat, rest them on “time to go away.”

Somebody’s Knocking at Your Door

African American spiritual · traditional, public domain · call and response · 2/4

so = G

Game: Teacher call, class knock-knock on the response. Credit the spiritual tradition.

Knock on the beat, not the words, if you are practising pulse.
