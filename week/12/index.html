Week 12 · Ta, Tempo & Contour · Nov 23–27

Melody Shape: Contour

A melody has a shape: up, down, or the same. We draw it in the air, build ta and rest with sticks, keep the pulse in Hello Everybody, and move with Jim Along Josie.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Nov 23–27

Full week: every Grade 1 class gets all 3 classes.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Weeks 1–12 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Later months use the same approach as each month is rewritten. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Day 1 · Prepare

Bring: Hand drum

GAMEPLAN book, Day 1: p. 35

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Everybody (book p. 35) — Sing while patting ta. Invite leaders to show a new way to keep the pulse (tap head, march in place, pound soft fists). Pass the lead around the circle.
  3. 5 minVoice shapes — Slide up, down, then stay on one sound. Draw each shape in the air.
  4. 6 minBody notation (book p. 35) — Four children stand as beats: one child alone = ta; two children side by side = two sounds on one beat (feel only). Rearrange and clap the row. Add one child with hands on shoulders for rest.
  5. 5 minAsk the class — “What things in your house keep a beat?”
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — New pulse leader walks the class out on ta.

Use in class

Day 2 · Practice

Bring: Popsicle sticks (about 12 per pair) or paper strips, Little Melba if you have it

GAMEPLAN book, Day 2: p. 36

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi; draw the hello shape in the air.
  3. 5 minPopsicle Stick Game (book p. 36) — Pairs build four-beat rows: one stick = ta; three sticks as a beam = two sounds; a shoulder touch or stick “Z” = rest. Speak and clap. Homeroom: sticks on desks or carpet bags; Music room next year: same game on the floor.
  4. 6 minRead-aloud · Little Melba and Her Big Trombone — Use the book guide. Trace trombone lines.
  5. 5 minYuki Song (book p. 36) — Play or hum the book’s Native American melody (title and page only; no text in the book). Class pats ta. A few children play the pulse on a drum while others listen for the shape of the tune.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Hum a hill shape, then walk.

Use in class

  • 🔢Rhythm makerPopsicle Stick Game: build and clap a row.
  • ✏️Draw the soundYuki Song: draw the shape you hear.

How to use this book

When: Day 2, after Gobble Gobble high/low review (about 5 min).

Before reading

  • “Listen for the sound words. Does each one go up, down, or stay the same?”
  • Finger ready to draw in the air.

During reading

  • On the sound-word page, say each word with a voice shape and trace it.
  • When Melba gets her trombone, slide your arm (and voice) up and down.
  • Her first try is a wobbly line; after practice, a smooth hill. Draw both on the board.

After reading

Hum a short shape; the class draws it in the air.

Quick check: “Did that go up, down, or stay?”

Adapt: Two children can show high and low with yarn or hands for the class to follow.

Extend: Pairs hum a hill or valley for a partner to draw.

Activity · Ask the class

“What things in your house keep a beat?”

Teacher notes

  • Contour = the shape of a melody. Keep examples to up, down, same, hill, valley.
  • Two sounds on one beat stay unnamed (ti-ti is Week 21); sticks can still show them.
  • Yuki Song: honour the source; teach by listening and humming, not by inventing words.
  • Pitched: none required this week unless you add a drum pulse. Unpitched: hand drum + one woodblock.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group D (Day 3).
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group D (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Shapes that are too complicated.
  • Throwing popsicle sticks.
  • Inventing words for Yuki Song.

Challenge

Hum a shape for the class to draw.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Hello Everybody

Traditional greeting · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

Sing so as G (or C if that sits better).

Game: Echo by phrase, then greet two children by name on so–mi.

Keep it light. If so is a strain, drop the whole song. The quiet hand starts and stops.

Jim Along Josie

American play-party song · public domain · do–mi–so · 2/4

so = G (do = C)

Game: Walk a circle; the leader changes the move (march, tiptoe, hop) each verse.

March in 2: strong on beat 1.

Practice studio

So–mi–la piano

G is so, E is mi, A is la. Tap to hear. Grade 1 lives on the first three.
