This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Class 1 introduces, Class 2 practises, Class 3 applies and checks. Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Class 1 · Introduce

Bring: Hand drum, shakers, woodblocks, triangle, tambourine

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 4 minSilent parade — Walk the beat quietly in a loop around the carpet (or the desk aisles). Hand drum only. Freeze. Sit.
  3. 4 minWhat “done” looks like — Even feet or even instrument. Freeze on stop. Instruments quiet until the start hand.
  4. 7 minRead-aloud · Max Found Two Sticks — Hook, per the book guide. Tap the beat on laps as you read.
  5. 1 minInstruments out & back — Hand out shakers and woodblocks in rest position; collect them the same way at the end.
  6. 4 minIf you have a shaker or sticks — Play the beat on shakers and woodblocks, not the words. Quiet volume.

Use in class

  • 🐘Animal paradeSilent parade: march the loop with the animal parade keeping the beat.

Music time: 20 min · plus 5 min devotional

Class 2 · Practise

Bring: Hand drum, shakers, woodblocks, triangle, tambourine

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 3 minSilent parade — Warm up.
  3. 6 minThe song is the street — You sing (Walk and Stop or Engine, Engine) and the parade moves around the carpet loop. Drum stops = statues.
  4. 5 minAudience spots — Half parade, half watch from their desks. Watchers check: even? safe? freeze? Swap.
  5. 1 minInstruments out & back — Hand out shakers, woodblocks, triangle, tambourine in rest position; collect them the same way at the end.
  6. 5 minProject 2 · rehearsal parade 1 — Shakers, woodblocks, triangle and tambourine on the beat around the carpet loop; you lead on the hand drum.

Use in class

  • 💓Steady beatRehearsal parade: a steady walking beat under the shakers, woodblocks, triangle and tambourine.

Class 3 · Apply & review

Bring: Hand drum, shakers, woodblocks, triangle, tambourine

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 3 minSilent parade — Warm up.
  3. 1 minInstruments out & back — Hand out the parade instruments in rest position; collect them the same way at the end.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Silent parade

Walk the beat with no talking. Drum only. Freeze. Sit.

Teach

What “done” looks like

Even feet or even instrument. Freeze on stop. Instruments stay quiet until the start hand.

If you have a shaker or sticks

Play the beat, not the words. Two-finger volume. Sticks are not swords.

The song is the street

Teacher sings. Parade moves. At the fermata or drum stop, statues.

Audience spots

Half parade, half watch. Watchers check: even? safe? freeze? Swap.

Story suggestion

Max Found Two Sticks · Brian Pinkney

Max’s steady drumming joins a marching band parade—perfect parade-with-beat lesson.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (The Cleveland Orchestra on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Hook at the start of Teach (about 7 min).

Before reading

  • “Max doesn’t talk. He answers with his sticks. Listen for the sounds he copies.”
  • Practise instrument rest position once before reading.

During reading

  • For each object Max plays, model a short pattern. The class echoes on laps: his thighs (pigeons), Grandpa’s bucket (rain on the window), the hatboxes (tom-toms in a marching band), the soda bottles (church bells), the garbage cans (the train).
  • Keep a steady drum beat under the whole book so the parade feeling builds.
  • When the marching band comes around the corner (“thump dee dee thump”), stand and march in place on the beat.
  • When the drummer tosses Max the sticks, freeze. Then sit.

After reading

Project 2, Beat parade: two rehearsal parades and one sharing parade. Watchers say “I saw even feet.”

Quick check: “Did Max play the beat when the band came by? How did you show it?”

Adapt: Children who find marching hard can stay seated and play the beat on laps or a drum.

Extend: Children choose one of Max’s objects and play its pattern on a classroom instrument inside the parade.

Activity · Project 2 — parade

Two rehearsal parades, one sharing parade. Watchers give one kind comment: “I saw even feet.”

Close

Collect instruments carefully — that is still music class. Stamp project 2.

Materials

  • Classroom percussion for half the class if you have it
  • Parade path
  • Known song

Teacher notes

  • Not enough instruments? Body percussion is a full project.
  • Rubric: even beat, freeze, safety. That is all.

Watch-outs

  • A long winding parade in a crowded room. A circle is enough.

Challenge

Play the beat on a drum while the class walks. You are the heart.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Walk and Stop

Traditional movement game · public domain · chant on the beat · 4/4

Teacher drum is the boss.

Game: Walk the pulse. Statue on stop. Later: hop, then stop; tiptoe, then stop.

Own space. Soft knees. This is pulse, not racing.

Engine, Engine Number Nine

Traditional playground chant · public domain · so–mi (or spoken on the beat) · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk the beat like a train. Freeze on the rest.

Teach it spoken on the pulse first, then sung so–mi. Chicago can become a local town.

Acka Backa

Traditional playground rhyme · public domain · so–mi chant · 2/4

Spoken or so–mi

Game: Passing a beanbag on the beat. The child on “boo” is next leader — kindly, no out-shame.

Pulse first. Rhythm of the words later.

Rhythm pad

Tap a box to cycle ta, ti-ti, rest. Play to hear eight beats.
