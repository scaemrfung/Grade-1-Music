This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. From Week 9 on, each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Weeks 1–8 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Story suggestion

Tanka Tanka Skunk! · Steve Webb

Two-syllable names (Tan-ka) vs one-syllable (Skunk) map cleanly to ti-ti and ta.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Mr. Simon Music on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, in the idea slot (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “Say the animal names. Do they have one sound or two sounds on a beat?”
  • Review: one finger = ta, two fingers = ti-ti.

During reading

  • Chant “Tan-ka Tan-ka Skunk” with the beat on laps. Tanka is ti-ti and Skunk is ta. Rest on the next beat: ti-ti ti-ti ta sh.
  • For each animal page, say the name and show one or two fingers: skunk, fox, yak, ox = ta; tiger, cheetah, llama, zebra, panda = ti-ti.
  • Longer names like kangaroo and caterpillar: just enjoy them. No need to notate.
  • Play the Tanka chant on a drum and the Skunk beat on a shaker.

After reading

Sort animal names into one sound per beat or two sounds per beat.

Quick check: “Was ‘tiger’ one sound or two?”

Adapt: Show animal pictures with dots underneath (one dot or two). Wigglers can step ta and jog ti-ti.

Extend: Children invent their own four-beat animal parade and play it on drums.

Watch-outs

  • Uneven ti-ti.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Pease Porridge Hot

English nursery rhyme · public domain · rhythm chant (ta and rest) · 2/4

Spoken. Add so–mi later if you wish.

Game: Partner clapping on the tas; open hands on the rest.

The rest after hot and cold is the whole point. Do not skip it.

Lucy Locket

Traditional singing game · public domain · so–mi–la · 2/4

so = G

Game: Circle. One walker drops a beanbag behind a friend on the last phrase. That friend walks next. Keep singing.

If the tune falls apart, sit and sing once, then play again.
