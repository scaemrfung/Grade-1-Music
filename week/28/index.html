Ostinato: A Pattern That Repeats

An ostinato is a short pattern that repeats under a song. We start with speech ostinatos.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Apr 19–23

Full week: every Grade 1 class gets all 3 classes.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. From Week 9 on, each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Weeks 1–8 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Story suggestion

Max Found Two Sticks · Brian Pinkney

Max’s steady drumming joins a marching band parade—perfect parade-with-beat lesson.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (The Cleveland Orchestra on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, in the idea slot (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “Max doesn’t talk. He answers with his sticks. Listen for the sounds he copies.”
  • Practise instrument rest position once before reading.

During reading

  • For each object Max plays, model a short pattern. The class echoes on laps: his thighs (pigeons), Grandpa’s bucket (rain on the window), the hatboxes (tom-toms in a marching band), the soda bottles (church bells), the garbage cans (the train).
  • Keep a steady drum beat under the whole book so the parade feeling builds.
  • When the marching band comes around the corner (“thump dee dee thump”), stand and march in place on the beat.
  • When the drummer tosses Max the sticks, freeze. Then sit.

After reading

Each group plays one of Max’s patterns over and over under the parade drum.

Quick check: “Which pattern kept repeating?”

Adapt: Children who find marching hard can stay seated and play the beat on laps or a drum.

Extend: Children choose one of Max’s objects and play its pattern on a classroom instrument inside the parade.

Teacher notes

  • Ostinato = repeats. Keep it to one bar.
  • Start the ostinato first, then the song.
  • Sticks rest on the floor between turns.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group D (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Ostinato speeding up.
  • Too many instruments at once.

Challenge

Invent a new ostinato for the class.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

English nursery rhyme · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Finger rain on the beat. Freeze on the rests.

The classic so–mi reading song. Sign so (higher) and mi (lower) while you sing.

Acka Backa

Traditional playground rhyme · public domain · so–mi chant · 2/4

Spoken or so–mi

Game: Passing a beanbag on the beat. The child on “boo” is next leader — kindly, no out-shame.
