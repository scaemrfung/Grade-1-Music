High and Low

Pitch can be high or low. We hear it, we show it with our bodies, and we sing it.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Class 1 introduces, Class 2 practises, Class 3 applies and checks. Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Mountain and valley

Teacher slides a siren up. Students stretch tall. Siren down. Students crouch. No words. Do it four times.

Teach

High is not loud

A mouse can be high and quiet. A drum can be low and soft. Separate pitch from volume right away.

Two notes only

Play a high G and a low C (or so and do). Students point up or down. Then they sing “high” and “low” on those pitches.

Xylophone or site piano

If you have a glockenspiel, let two children play high and low while the class shows with bodies. If not, use the studio on this website.

Song: Star Light

Star Light, Star Bright sits in a small range. Listen for the drop. Trace it in the air.

Story suggestion

Up, Up, Down · Robert Munsch (illus. Michael Martchenko)

Literal climbing up/down gives a clear high–low pitch metaphor for Grade 1.

Buy on Amazon · Listen: Robert Munsch reads it (Robert Munsch (official audio) on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Brain break before Sort the sound (about 5 min).

Before reading

  • “Anna loves to climb. When she climbs up, let your voice slide up. When she falls, slide down.”
  • “High is not loud. Keep your siren soft.”

During reading

  • Every time Anna goes “up, up, up,” slide your voice higher on each “up” while bodies stretch taller. On “down,” slide low and crouch.
  • Point out that the words in the book climb up the page, just like her voice should.
  • Keep the sirens quiet: a high sound can be soft, like a mouse.
  • When Mom and then Dad try to climb the tree and fall, repeat the up–down slide. Two children can play high and low bars on a glockenspiel.

After reading

Sort the sound: stand for high, sit for low. Then pairs quiz each other with the site piano or a bell.

Quick check: “If I sing softly, can it still be high?” (Yes.)

Adapt: Use hands only (up and down) for children who can’t crouch. Point to the high and low parts of the picture of the tree.

Extend: Children take turns being Anna: they play a high or low note and the class climbs or falls.

Activity · Sort the sound

Teacher plays or sings high/low in mixed order. Students stand for high, sit for low. Then pairs take the site piano or a bell and quiz their partner.

Close

Ask: “If I sing softly, can it still be high?” Yes. Stamp if bodies match pitch, not volume.

Materials

  • Piano on this site or a real xylophone
  • Space to crouch and stretch
  • Board: high / low

Teacher notes

  • Avoid “higher is better.” Low voices are music too.
  • A child who only mouths can still show with their body and be fully in.

Watch-outs

  • Using a tiny pitch difference they cannot hear. Make high and low obvious today.

Challenge

Sing a so–mi “cuckoo” (high–low) and let the class echo.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Star Light, Star Bright

Traditional · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Quiet ending. Good concert voice practice.

See-Saw

Traditional · public domain (also sung as Saw, Saw, Saw Away) · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Partners hold hands and bend on so, stretch on mi — or the reverse, as long as the class agrees.

A small knee bend on each so keeps the beat honest.

Practice studio

So–mi–la piano

G is so, E is mi, A is la. Tap to hear. Grade 1 lives on the first three.
