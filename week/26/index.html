Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Class 1 introduces, Class 2 practises, Class 3 applies and checks. Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Rest position

Instruments on the floor or on shoulders, not making sound. Practice pick up, play four beats, rest. That loop is the whole classroom management system.

Teach

One job today: the beat

Not the words. Even sounds with the drum or with me.

How each one speaks

Sticks tap. Shakers shake once per beat (not a sandstorm). Bells ding and wait. Drums are open palm, not fists, unless it is a proper hand drum technique you teach.

Quiet is a skill

The best players can play so the song is still the star.

Passing out and in

You pass out. They do not help themselves. Collect the same way. Build this ritual now.

Story suggestion

Zin! Zin! Zin! A Violin · Lloyd Moss (illus. Marjorie Priceman)

Introduces orchestra instruments with joyful respect—lead-in to careful instrument handling.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Reading Rainbow (official) on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Hook at the start of Teach (about 6 min). The story comes before the extra footage in the Reading Rainbow clip.

Before reading

  • “One instrument joins at a time. Count them with your fingers.”
  • “Watch how real musicians hold their instruments and wait.”

During reading

  • Hold up a finger for each new player: trombone solo, trumpet duo, French horn trio, cello quartet, violin quintet, flute, clarinet, oboe, bassoon, then the harp makes ten.
  • Mime holding each instrument gently (a slide for trombone, a bow for violin). Then rest hands in laps.
  • When the orchestra comes on stage and waits to begin, everyone sits in instrument “rest position.”
  • At the end, the audience shouts “Encore!” Talk about how careful players get asked back.

After reading

Pick up, play four beats, rest. Pass out classroom instruments with the book’s rule: play together, then rest together. Move through stations.

Quick check: “What is rest position? Why do musicians wait before they play?”

Adapt: Show photos of the book’s instruments. Children who worry about instruments can be the conductor first.

Extend: Sort the book’s instruments by how they make sound: blow, bow, or pluck.

Activity · Rotate colours

If you have four types, four stations, two minutes each, beat only, then rest. A song underneath the whole time.

Close

Collect. Count. Thank the instruments. Stamp if rest position worked.

Materials

  • Whatever you have: sticks, shakers, triangles, hand drums
  • Rest position taught on day one of instruments

Teacher notes

  • Fewer instruments is better than a riot. Half the class can body-perc and switch.
  • Triangles need a fat yarn handle and a rule: one ding per beat.

Watch-outs

  • Passing out before teaching rest. Always rest first.

Challenge

Play the beat with eyes on the conductor, not on the instrument.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Bell Horses

Traditional · public domain · so–mi–la · 2/4

Jingle bells on the beat, rest them on “time to go away.”

Engine, Engine Number Nine

Traditional playground chant · public domain · so–mi (or spoken on the beat) · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk the beat like a train. Freeze on the rest.

Teach it spoken on the pulse first, then sung so–mi. Chicago can become a local town.
