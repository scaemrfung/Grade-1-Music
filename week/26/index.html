Jack and Jill, Contour and Instrument Families

We copy Irish Washerwoman moves, trace contour cards with voices, sing and move Jack and Jill in ABA with a chord bordun, classify unpitched instruments as wood, metal, or skin in One, Two, Three, and form shamrock or clover groups from temple-block cues.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Apr 5–9

Full week: every Grade 1 class gets all 3 classes.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Weeks 1–19 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Later months use the same approach as each month is rewritten. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Hello Song, so–mi–la

Greet on so–mi–la. One contour siren.

Teach

Irish Washerwoman · Contour cards (book p. 77)

Copycat moves while the jig plays — wash, wring, hang, sweep, stir (title and page only). Then present contour cards one at a time; class traces and matches vocal sounds; try one upside down. Homeroom: washer moves beside desks.

Jack and Jill ABA (book pp. 77–78)

Title and page only. Trace contour on a chart; echo nonsense syllables then text; pat the beat and transfer to a chord bordun if you have bars; B section speech with boy/girl jump–crouch–sway actions; perform song/speech/song with an eight-beat introduction and name the form ABA. Homeroom: actions in two seated teams; bordun on one instrument or laps.

One, Two, Three · Shamrocks and Four-Leaf Clovers (book p. 79)

One, Two, Three by title and page only: identify clapped rhythms on cards; speak the poem; label instrument families wood / metal / skin; players perform the poem rhythm by family. Shamrocks game: move to a drum pulse; freeze; temple blocks play three or four sounds; silent groups of three or four. Homeroom: march in place; form groups in the carpet rows.

How to use this book

When: Day 3, before One, Two, Three (about 5–8 min).

Before reading

  • “Listen for instruments made of wood, metal, or skin (drum heads).”
  • Show three sorting signs.

During reading

  • On each new instrument, freeze and show wood, metal, or skin with fingers 1–2–3.
  • Keep voices off during the listening.

After reading

Sort one classroom instrument into a family.

Quick check: “Is a triangle wood, metal, or skin?”

Adapt: Two families only: wood and metal.

Extend: A child plays one sound; the class shows the family.

Activity · Read-aloud · The Remarkable Farkle McBride

Use the book guide. Instrument families.

Close

Show wood, metal, or skin with fingers, then walk.

Teacher notes

  • GAMEPLAN March Week Three (pp. 77–79). Irish Washerwoman, contour cards, Jack and Jill, One, Two, Three, and Shamrocks and Four-Leaf Clovers by title and page only — draw your own contour chart; do not copy book notation.
  • Homeroom kit: contour paths on paper, one barred instrument if possible, a few wood/metal/skin instruments, drum and temple blocks or two woods.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group B (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Shouting during contour sirens.
  • Running to form shamrock groups.
  • Mixing families without listening first.

Challenge

Lead one contour card for the class.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

See-Saw

Traditional · public domain (also sung as Saw, Saw, Saw Away) · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Partners hold hands and bend on so, stretch on mi — or the reverse, as long as the class agrees.

A small knee bend on each so keeps the beat honest.

Engine, Engine Number Nine

Traditional playground chant · public domain · so–mi (or spoken on the beat) · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk the beat like a train. Freeze on the rest.

Teach it spoken on the pulse first, then sung so–mi. Chicago can become a local town.

Practice studio

Rhythm pad

Tap a box to cycle ta, ti-ti, rest. Play to hear eight beats.

So–mi–la piano

G is so, E is mi, A is la. Tap to hear. Grade 1 lives on the first three.

Name the sound

Play a mystery sound, then tap the name.
