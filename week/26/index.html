AB: Two Different Parts

Same/different gets a name: A and B. A song or piece can have two different parts.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. From Week 9 on, each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Weeks 1–8 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Story suggestion

We're Going on a Bear Hunt · Michael Rosen (illus. Helen Oxenbury)

Same refrain returns after different obstacle verses—clear same/different musical form.

Buy on Amazon · Watch Michael Rosen perform it (Walker Books on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, in the idea slot (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “Listen for the part that is the same every time and the part that changes.”
  • Hold up a blue SAME card and a yellow DIFFERENT card.

During reading

  • Every time the chorus returns (“We’re going on a bear hunt…” and “We can’t go over it, we can’t go under it…”), a helper holds up SAME.
  • For each new place (the grass, the river, the mud, the forest, the snowstorm, the cave), a helper holds up DIFFERENT. The sound words change every time.
  • Map it on the board with coloured squares: blue, yellow, blue, yellow… Children see the pattern.
  • The run home goes through the places backwards. Ask: “Same or different from before?”

After reading

Map the story with coloured squares: A for the chorus, B for each new place.

Quick check: “Was that A or B?”

Adapt: Use colours only, not words, for English learners. Movers can stand up on SAME and sit on DIFFERENT.

Extend: Write a class verse: same chorus, new place, new sound word.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

See-Saw

Traditional · public domain (also sung as Saw, Saw, Saw Away) · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Partners hold hands and bend on so, stretch on mi — or the reverse, as long as the class agrees.

A small knee bend on each so keeps the beat honest.

Engine, Engine Number Nine

Traditional playground chant · public domain · so–mi (or spoken on the beat) · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk the beat like a train. Freeze on the rest.

Teach it spoken on the pulse first, then sung so–mi. Chicago can become a local town.
