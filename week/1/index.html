Your jobs today

Warm-up · Name wave

Teacher sings “Hello, _____” on so–mi. The class echoes. Go around as many names as five minutes allow. If time is short, greet the whole class: “Hello, Grade One.”

Teach

How we do music

In this room we sing, we play, we move, and we listen. We take turns with our voices. We keep our bodies in our own space.

The quiet hand

When the teacher’s hand goes up, voices and instruments rest. Practice three times: talk, hand up, freeze. Smile when they freeze. Do not shout over them.

A hello song

Teach “Hello Everybody” (or “Hello, Hello”) by echo. Phrase one, class copies. Phrase two, class copies. Then the whole thing. Keep it high enough for children’s voices — not an adult low voice.

Sit like a singer

Feet on the floor, tall backs, hands in laps. “If your chair is a drum, we are not playing it yet.”

Story suggestion

The Listening Walk · Paul Showers (illus. Aliki)

Models quiet listening, stopping to notice sounds, and sing–listen–move–stop classroom routines.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (KidTimeStoryTime on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Hook for the Teach block, right after the Name wave (about 7 min). Stop at the park if time is short and finish next week.

Before reading

  • “The girl in this book does not talk on her walk. Why might that help her hear more?”
  • Practise the quiet hand once: hand up, freeze, listen for five seconds. “What did you hear?”

During reading

  • Echo each sound word the way the book says it (Major’s toenails on the sidewalk, Dad’s shoes, the lawnmower, the sprinkler). Keep the echo the same size as your model.
  • After every two or three sounds, raise the quiet hand. Everyone freezes. Smile, then turn the page.
  • At the jackhammer, cover ears: that is a loud sound. In the park, whisper the soft sounds (pigeons, ducks, the woodpecker tapping, crickets, wind in the leaves).
  • Tap the woodpecker on your knee as a four-beat pattern. Class echoes.

After reading

Do our own listening walk sitting down: eyes closed for 20 seconds, then quiet hand. Two or three children name one sound. Then sing the hello song once more, standing.

Quick check: “What does the girl do so she can hear more?” “What does our quiet hand ask us to do?”

Adapt: English learners: show quick pictures (dog, shoe, lawnmower, duck, jet) and point as you read. Wigglers: tiptoe in place during the park pages.

Extend: Take a two-minute hallway listening walk. Back in class, children draw one sound they heard.

Activity · Echo the teacher

Teacher claps a 4-beat pattern. Class claps it back. Then teacher sings a 4-beat so–mi snippet on “loo.” Class sings it back. End with the hello song once more, standing.

Close

Ask: “What does the quiet hand mean?” Stamp the lesson when most of the class can freeze without a reminder.

Materials

  • Open space or chairs in a circle
  • This page on the projector
  • A small drum or clap

Teacher notes

  • Grade 1 is six. Keep instructions to one sentence, then do it.
  • If a child will not sing yet, let them mouth the words. Do not force a solo on week 1.
  • Stand where every child can see your hand. The quiet hand only works if they can see it.
  • Names matter. Misheard names break trust on day one — ask once if you are unsure.

Watch-outs

  • Starting with a long talk. Sing first.
  • Using a low adult key. Children need a higher starting pitch.
  • Praising volume. We want a singing voice, not a shout.

Challenge

Be the leader: clap a tiny pattern and let the class echo you once.

Optional faith note

Optional: we use our voices as a gift. We take turns so every voice has room.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Hello Everybody

Traditional greeting · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

Sing so as G (or C if that sits better).

Game: Echo by phrase, then greet two children by name on so–mi.

Keep it light. If so is a strain, drop the whole song. The quiet hand starts and stops.

Walk and Stop

Traditional movement game · public domain · chant on the beat · 4/4

Teacher drum is the boss.

Game: Walk the pulse. Statue on stop. Later: hop, then stop; tiptoe, then stop.

Own space. Soft knees. This is pulse, not racing.
