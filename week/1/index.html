Week 01 · Voices & Pulse · Aug 31–Sept 4

Four Voices

Welcome to music class. We meet our four voices (whisper, talk, call, sing), learn the quiet hand, and start every class on a pulse.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Aug 31–Sept 4

Full week: every Grade 1 class gets all 3 classes.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Day 1 · Prepare

Bring: Hand drum, four voice picture cards (whisper, talk, call, sing)

GAMEPLAN book, Day 1: p. __

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello on the pulse — Tap a slow drum pulse. Sing “Hello, Grade One” on so–mi over it. The class echoes. Start and stop with the drum.
  3. 2 minThe quiet hand — Talk, raise your hand, freeze. Practise three times and smile at each freeze. This is our stop signal all year.
  4. 8 minMeet four voices — Say “good morning” in a whisper, a talking voice, a calling (playground) voice, then sing it. The class copies each one. Hold up the matching card.
  5. 6 minVoice switch — Point to a card; the class says “good morning” in that voice. Switch fast. The quiet hand stops the game.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Drum a slow pulse. One table at a time walks to the door on the drum, then freezes.

Use in class

  • 🗣️Four voicesMeet four voices: tap whisper, speak, call and sing so the class copies each one on “good morning.”

Day 3 · Transfer

Bring: Hand drum, four voice cards

GAMEPLAN book, Day 3: p. __

  2. 2 minHello on the pulse — Sing Hello Everybody over a light drum pulse.

Optional story

Mina’s two voices

Mina used her talking voice to tell Sam about her new boots. Then Mrs. Cole sang “hello.” Mina tried to sing, but it came out like a yell. Mrs. Cole whispered the tune. Mina whispered it back. The whisper grew into a small, floating singing voice. Sam heard it and smiled. Two voices: one for words, one for songs.

Story suggestion

Mortimer · Robert Munsch (illus. Michael Martchenko)

Spoken family dialogue contrasts with Mortimer’s sung refrain (“Clang, clang, rattle-bing-bang”).

Buy on Amazon · Listen: Robert Munsch reads it (Robert Munsch (official audio) on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, in the idea slot (about 6 min), after the four-voices echo.

Before reading

  • “Listen for two kinds of voices: people talking and Mortimer singing.”
  • Set the body cues: hand on chest for talk, hand on head for sing.

During reading

  • Every time Mortimer sings “Clang, clang, rattle-bing-bang, gonna make my noise all day,” the class sings it with you on a light so–mi tune, hand on head.
  • When his mother, his father, the 17 brothers and sisters, and then the two police officers tell him “Mortimer, be quiet,” say it in a talking voice, hand on chest. Try the police line in a low, slow talking voice.
  • Each time someone climbs the stairs, pat a slow, steady beat on laps.
  • Point out that the family gets louder and louder, but a shout is not a song. Keep Mortimer’s song light every time. Whisper-sing it at the end when he falls asleep.

After reading

Play Voice detective with lines from the book: say “Mortimer, be quiet!” or sing “Clang, clang…”. Students show the matching voice card or hand spot.

Quick check: “Which voice did Mortimer use for his noise song? Which voice did his family use?”

Adapt: Hold up a TALK card and a SING card. The refrain repeats, so new English speakers can join by the second time.

Extend: Invent a new “noise line” for Mortimer and sing it on so–mi. Class echoes.

Teacher notes

  • Model every voice at a gentle volume; the calling voice is short and happens once.
  • If a child only speaks the song, they are still in. Invite, do not force.
  • Keep the drum pulse slow (about walking speed). Pulse is the thread through all of September.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group A (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Letting the calling voice take over songs.
  • Talking over the class instead of using the quiet hand.

Challenge

Lead the class in “good morning” using a voice card of your choice.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Hello Everybody

Traditional greeting · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

Sing so as G (or C if that sits better).

Game: Echo by phrase, then greet two children by name on so–mi.

Keep it light. If so is a strain, drop the whole song. The quiet hand starts and stops.

Engine, Engine Number Nine

Traditional playground chant · public domain · so–mi (or spoken on the beat) · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk the beat like a train. Freeze on the rest.

Teach it spoken on the pulse first, then sung so–mi. Chicago can become a local town.
