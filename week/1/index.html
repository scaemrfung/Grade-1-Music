Week 01 · Voices & Pulse · Aug 31–Sept 4

Welcome to the Music Room

We learn how our Music room works: where we sit on the floor, our three rules, the quiet hand, and how to move to a steady pulse. We meet the hello song, our first instruments, and a freeze game.

Music room version, written for next year (2027–28) when classes are in the Music room: floor spots, instruments set out, room to move. In a homeroom (this year’s setting): sit at desks or on the carpet, bring the kit, and move in place or in the aisles.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

Aug 31–Sept 4

Full week: every Grade 1 class gets all 3 classes.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Weeks 1–12 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Later months use the same approach as each month is rewritten. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Day 1 · Prepare

Open this lesson in Chalkie↗ (opens in a new tab)

Bring: Hand drum, floor-spot markers (tape or paper dots), seating-chart sheet and pencil

GAMEPLAN book, Day 1: p. 1

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 3 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet the class on so and mi at the door of the Music room. Sing the Hello Song for Grade One from the book’s master sheet, then ask “what’s your name?” on so–mi for the class to echo.
  3. 6 minFloor spots and seating chart — In the Music room, children sit on the floor in three or four rows with the shortest at the front. Put a marker at each spot and fill in the seating chart as they settle. Rehearse walking in, sitting on a spot, and walking out.
  4. 5 minOur Music room rules — Talk through three short rules in your own words: listen, wait to be invited to play, and kind hands and voices. For each rule show a thumbs-up example and a thumbs-down example, and practise the quiet hand once.
  5. 4 minFollow the leader on the pulse — Sit tall and copy your simple moves to the drum: pat knees, clap, tap shoulders. Start with both hands together, eight beats for each move, and stop the drum to freeze.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Drum a slow pulse. One row at a time walks to the door on the drum and freezes.

Use in class

  • 👋Hello, name!Hello Song: the bells sing a greeting on so–mi and each child sings it back.
  • 💓Steady beatFollow the leader: a slow walking beat to move to, then stop it to freeze.

Music time: 20 min · plus 5 min devotional

Day 2 · Practice

Open this lesson in Chalkie↗ (opens in a new tab)

Bring: Hand drum, tambourine, woodblock and beater

GAMEPLAN book, Day 2: p. 2

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet children by name on so–mi and let them echo it back.
  3. 5 minWelcome Back to School (book p. 2) — Teach the song from the book, patting the pulse on your knees. Then everyone pats and counts to eight together. (The song words are in the book and are not copied here.)
  4. 5 minInstrument manners — Show the three instruments one at a time: how it is held, where it is hit, and its rest position (resting on your lap, hands still). Children copy the hold in the air with no sound.
  5. 6 minPass and play — Pass the three instruments around the circle while everyone pats the pulse. When you stop, the three holders play eight beats while the others count on their fingers. Then they pass and you go again.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — One row at a time walks to the door on the drum.

Use in class

  • 🥁Percussion padInstrument manners: hear each instrument before the children try it.
  • 🫘Beanbag passPass and play: pass the beanbag around the circle while the class pats the pulse.

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.

  3. 5 minMove and Freeze + 10-second checks + 10-second checks — Everyone moves around the room to the drum: tiptoe, march, crawl low. Stop the drum to freeze. Then ask whether the drum was the same as before or different (faster, slower, higher, lower). Rotating check, group A (about 7 children): while the game runs, give each child a 10-second solo: tiptoe four steps to the drum and freeze when it stops. Mark Y or N on the record sheet.
  4. 4 minFive Little Monkeys (book p. 3) — Say the monkey rhyme from the book. Hands go high on the high lines and low on the low lines, and fingers count down.
  5. 5 minRead-aloud · Mortimer — Use the book guide. The class sings Mortimer’s noise line in a singing voice and speaks the family’s lines in a talking voice. Name each voice as it comes.
  6. 2 minFollow the leader review — Repeat one move from Day 1 for sixteen beats while the drum fades out. Did we stay together?
  7. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Walk and freeze at the door.

Optional story

Mina’s two voices

Mina used her talking voice to tell Sam about her new boots. Then Mrs. Cole sang “hello.” Mina tried to sing, but it came out like a yell. Mrs. Cole whispered the tune. Mina whispered it back. The whisper grew into a small, floating singing voice. Sam heard it and smiled. Two voices: one for words, one for songs.

Story suggestion

Mortimer · Robert Munsch (illus. Michael Martchenko)

Spoken family dialogue contrasts with Mortimer’s sung refrain (“Clang, clang, rattle-bing-bang”).

Buy on Amazon · Listen: Robert Munsch reads it (Robert Munsch (official audio) on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 3, after Five Little Monkeys (about 5 min).

Before reading

  • “Listen for two kinds of voices: people talking and Mortimer singing.”
  • Set the body cues: hand on chest for talk, hand on head for sing.

During reading

  • Every time Mortimer sings “Clang, clang, rattle-bing-bang, gonna make my noise all day,” the class sings it with you on a light so–mi tune, hand on head.
  • When his mother, his father, the 17 brothers and sisters, and then the two police officers tell him “Mortimer, be quiet,” say it in a talking voice, hand on chest. Try the police line in a low, slow talking voice.
  • Each time someone climbs the stairs, pat a slow, steady beat on laps.
  • Point out that the family gets louder and louder, but a shout is not a song. Keep Mortimer’s song light every time. Whisper-sing it at the end when he falls asleep.

After reading

Voice detective: say or sing a line and the class shows whisper, talking, calling or singing with a hand on lips, chest, cupped mouth or head.

Quick check: “Which voice did Mortimer use for his noise song? Which voice did his family use?”

Adapt: Hold up a TALK card and a SING card. The refrain repeats, so new English speakers can join by the second time.

Extend: Invent a new “noise line” for Mortimer and sing it on so–mi. Class echoes.

Teacher notes

  • Everything on Day 1 happens on the Music room floor, so the same spots can be used all year.
  • Keep the rules to three and teach them by acting them out, not by reading a list.
  • Introduce instruments only after the rules have been practised once; keep them in rest position until invited.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group A (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Handing out instruments before the rules are practised.
  • Counting past eight when passing instruments (keep the cycle short).

Challenge

Lead a four-beat move for the class to copy with the drum.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Hello Everybody

Traditional greeting · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

Sing so as G (or C if that sits better).

Game: Echo by phrase, then greet two children by name on so–mi.

Keep it light. If so is a strain, drop the whole song. The quiet hand starts and stops.

Walk and Stop

Traditional movement game · public domain · chant on the beat · 4/4

Teacher drum is the boss.

Game: Walk the pulse. Statue on stop. Later: hop, then stop; tiptoe, then stop.

Own space. Soft knees. This is pulse, not racing.
