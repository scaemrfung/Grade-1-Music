Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Class 1 introduces, Class 2 practises, Class 3 applies and checks. Your jobs today

Warm-up · Echo warmup

Teacher claps four, class echoes. Student volunteer claps four, class echoes. Set the project mechanic.

Teach

The recipe

Four beats. At least two of: ta, ti-ti, rest. Must be echoable — not so wild the class cannot copy.

Practice alone

Write. Clap. Fix. If you cannot clap it, change it.

How we share

Child claps. Class echoes once. Kind applause (two claps). Next child. You will not get through 24 solos in 12 minutes — do a row today, another row if you have extra time, or split with week 19’s warmup.

If they freeze

They may clap all tas. That still counts. Pride matters more than complexity.

Story suggestion

Jam, Too? · JaNay Brown-Wood (illus. Jacqueline Alcántara)

A child invents and joins percussion patterns—supports composing your own 4-beat idea.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (DeSotoTrailSchool on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Hook at the start of Teach, before the recipe (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “A man plays a conga on the shore. Listen for how each new person asks to join.”
  • “Everyone brings their own sound. Today you bring your own four-beat pattern.”

During reading

  • Each time someone arrives, the class chants “Can I jam too?” and you answer “Sure thing, my friend!”
  • Echo the drum sounds (slap slap, tip tap) as four-beat body patterns.
  • Add one classroom instrument for each new player: drum for the conga, then a djembe, a shaker, finger cymbals or a triangle for the zills, maracas, and bongos. Layer them on a steady beat.
  • The child in the story has no drum and chooses to dance instead. Invite a few children to dance on the beat.

After reading

Project 3 share. Before each child claps, the class asks “Can you jam too?” The child claps their pattern and the class echoes it once.

Quick check: “What does your pattern need?” (Four beats, at least two of ta, ti-ti, rest, and the class must be able to echo it.)

Adapt: Dancing, or clapping all tas, still counts as joining the jam. English learners can join the repeated question right away.

Extend: Put three student patterns in a row to make a class “jam” and play it on instruments.

Activity · Project 3 — share

As many students as time allows. Teacher notes who still needs a turn next class. Collect papers.

Close

Name the musical bravery, not the fanciest pattern. Stamp project 3 as you go, or stamp the week when papers are in.

Materials

  • Worksheet
  • Laps or sticks
  • A way to notate (icons)

Teacher notes

  • Do not force a solo from a child who is not ready. They can clap with you in unison as their share.
  • Rubric: four beats, readable, echoable. That is a 4-point load if you split as in the project sheet.

Watch-outs

  • Using this day to teach a new note. No new content.

Challenge

Add your pattern under a known song as a quiet ostinato with two friends.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Pease Porridge Hot

English nursery rhyme · public domain · rhythm chant (ta and rest) · 2/4

Spoken. Add so–mi later if you wish.

Game: Partner clapping on the tas; open hands on the rest.

The rest after hot and cold is the whole point. Do not skip it.

Practice studio

Rhythm pad

Tap a box to cycle ta, ti-ti, rest. Play to hear eight beats.
