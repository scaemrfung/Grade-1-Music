This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Weeks 1–19 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Later months use the same approach as each month is rewritten. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Day 1 · Prepare

Bring: Board and marker, Max Found Two Sticks if you have it

GAMEPLAN book, Day 1: p. 53

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi. One short ta/ti-ti board review.
  3. 5 minLabeling Quarter Note and Eighth Note (book p. 53) — Draw ta/ti-ti/rest in stick notation; students identify. Add note heads and name quarter and eighth notes — still called ta and ti-ti when speaking. Point to mixes while the class speaks. Homeroom: board or document camera.
  4. 6 minAsk the class — “Point to a quarter note. Point to eighth notes.”
  5. 5 minRead-aloud · Max Found Two Sticks — Use the book guide. Sticks become ta and ti-ti.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Clap ta ti-ti ta rest to the door.

Use in class

  • 🔢Rhythm makerLabel notes: quarter and eighth.
  • 🥁Percussion padMax’s sticks: ta and ti-ti.

Day 2 · Practice

Bring: Hand drum, optional BX/BM if carried in, Lucy Locket (on this site)

GAMEPLAN book, Day 2: pp. 53–54

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi.
  3. 5 minThe Old Gray Cats Are Sleeping (book pp. 53–54) — Teach from the book (title and page only). Verses alternate sleepy cats (rest feel) and creeping/running mice (jogging pulse). Split class into cats and mice; pantomime safely (jog, do not catch). Match movement to the pulse you support.
  4. 6 minLucy Locket loud/quiet (book p. 54; also on this site) — Hide a “pocket.” Seeker covers eyes; class sings quieter when far and louder when near — loud is not a shout. Homeroom: hide near the carpet edge so a corner stays visible.
  5. 5 minI Hope! mail game (book pp. 52–54) — Optional if time: title and page only. Conga or hand drum plays a numbered rhythm card after a Valentine delivery. Alternate hands on the drum.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Quiet mouse steps, then loud lion steps (still kind).

Use in class

  • 👏Body percussionOld Gray Cats: rest vs jogging pulse.
  • 🔉Loud or soft?Lucy Locket: quiet far, louder near.

Music time: 20 min · plus 5 min devotional

Day 3 · Transfer

Bring: Chef’s hat or paper baker hat, board chart for Baker’s Hat, Bounce High Bounce Low, Somebody’s Knocking at Your Door

GAMEPLAN book, Day 3: pp. 54–55

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 2 minHello Song, so–mi — Greet on so–mi.
  3. 5 minBaker’s Hat (book pp. 54–55) — Teach from the book (title and page only). Speak and clap the baker rhythm; build a so–mi melody with hat visuals so each “Baker’s hat” motive repeats. Pat the pulse; transfer to one barred instrument if available. Pass the hat; holder says what they would bake; class chants the reply four times as a contrasting section.
  4. 5 minSomebody’s Knocking / Bounce High (book p. 55) + 10-second checks — Somebody’s Knocking by title and page only (spiritual; optional woodblock taps). Bounce High, Bounce Low is on this site — bounce on each “bounce,” then to a friend. Homeroom: soft playground ball or beanbag. Rotating check, group B (about 7 children): while the game runs, give each child a 10-second solo: clap one baker rhythm and freeze. Mark Y or N on the record sheet.
  5. 6 minBaker + bounce checks — Checked children clap the baker rhythm and bounce once on the pulse.
  6. 2 minLine up on the pulse — Bounce quietly to the door.

Use in class

  • 🦜So–mi–la echoBaker’s Hat: so–mi melody.
  • 🐱Copycat songsBounce High: bounce on the pulse.

Story suggestion

Max Found Two Sticks · Brian Pinkney

Stick rhythms become music — the same idea as labeling ta and ti-ti, then playing patterns on a drum or sticks.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (The Cleveland Orchestra on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, after Labeling Quarter and Eighth (about 5 min).

Before reading

  • “Max’s sticks can be ta or ti-ti — watch how many sounds fit on one beat.”
  • Clap ta ta ti-ti ta once.

During reading

  • Pat ta on single taps; pat ti-ti on quick doubles.
  • Freeze on quiet moments like a rest.

After reading

Speak one four-beat card: ta, ti-ti, rest.

Quick check: “How many sounds on that beat — one or two?”

Adapt: Two fingers on the palm instead of sticks.

Extend: A child claps a card; the class says ta / ti-ti / rest.

Activity · Read-aloud · Max Found Two Sticks

Use the book guide. Sticks become ta and ti-ti.

Close

Clap ta ti-ti ta rest to the door.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Lucy Locket

Traditional singing game · public domain · so–mi–la · 2/4

so = G

Game: Circle. One walker drops a beanbag behind a friend on the last phrase. That friend walks next. Keep singing.

If the tune falls apart, sit and sing once, then play again.

Bounce High, Bounce Low

Traditional singing game · public domain · so–mi–la · 2/4

so = G, la = A, mi = E

Game: Soft ball or beanbag. Underhand only. Swap in a class name for Shiloh.

La is the new neighbour above so. Sign it higher, sing it lightly.

Somebody’s Knocking at Your Door

African American spiritual · traditional, public domain · call and response · 2/4

so = G

Game: Teacher call, class knock-knock on the response. Credit the spiritual tradition.

Knock on the beat, not the words, if you are practising pulse.
