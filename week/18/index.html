Bordun and Pulse

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. From Week 9 on, each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Weeks 1–8 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Story suggestion

Jam, Too? · JaNay Brown-Wood (illus. Jacqueline Alcántara)

A child invents and joins percussion patterns—supports composing your own 4-beat idea.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (DeSotoTrailSchool on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, in the idea slot (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “A man plays a conga on the shore. Listen for how each new person asks to join.”
  • “Everyone brings their own sound. Today you bring your own four-beat pattern.”

During reading

  • Each time someone arrives, the class chants “Can I jam too?” and you answer “Sure thing, my friend!”
  • Echo the drum sounds (slap slap, tip tap) as four-beat body patterns.
  • Add one classroom instrument for each new player: drum for the conga, then a djembe, a shaker, finger cymbals or a triangle for the zills, maracas, and bongos. Layer them on a steady beat.
  • The child in the story has no drum and chooses to dance instead. Invite a few children to dance on the beat.

After reading

Layer one instrument at a time on a steady pulse, starting with the two-drum bordun.

Quick check: “Which sound stayed the same all the way through?”

Adapt: Dancing, or clapping all tas, still counts as joining the jam. English learners can join the repeated question right away.

Extend: Put three student patterns in a row to make a class “jam” and play it on instruments.

Teacher notes

  • A bordun is two notes together; here, low and higher drum.
  • Start the bordun first, then the song.
  • Rotate players every two songs.
  • Rotating 10-second checks this week: group B (Day 3).

Watch-outs

  • Bordun speeding up when the song starts.
  • Drums too loud for the singers.

Challenge

Play the bordun while you sing.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

See-Saw

Traditional · public domain (also sung as Saw, Saw, Saw Away) · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Partners hold hands and bend on so, stretch on mi — or the reverse, as long as the class agrees.

A small knee bend on each so keeps the beat honest.

Snail, Snail

Traditional · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk in a spiral line on the pulse; wind in, then unwind.

Try it with a triangle introduction (Week 13).
