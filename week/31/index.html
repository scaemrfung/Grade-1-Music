This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Each 20 minutes has the same shape: hello on the pulse (2), voice or body (2), one idea (8), a game (6), close and line up on the pulse (2). Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Story suggestion

From Head to Toe · Eric Carle

Call-and-echo structure (“Can you do it? I can do it!”) supports echoing claps and sung patterns.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (The World of Eric Carle on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 2, in the idea slot (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “Every page asks a question: ‘Can you do it?’ Listen for the answer.”
  • “An echo copies exactly: same moves, same number, same speed.”

During reading

  • Read each animal line and model the move four times on a steady beat: penguin turns head, giraffe bends neck, buffalo raises shoulders, monkey waves arms, seal claps hands, gorilla thumps chest (gently), cat arches back, crocodile wiggles hips, camel bends knees, donkey kicks legs, elephant stomps foot.
  • Class answers “I can do it!” on so–mi, then echoes the move exactly four times. No extra moves.
  • On the seal page, clap a four-beat pattern and have the class echo it. On the elephant page, stomp softly: small feet, big listening.

After reading

A: the animal moves. B: a class ta/rest row on the drum. A again.

Quick check: “What came back at the end?”

Adapt: Offer a seated version of every move. English learners: touch and name each body part as you read.

Extend: Pairs invent a new animal move and teach it to the class as a four-beat echo.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

See-Saw

Traditional · public domain (also sung as Saw, Saw, Saw Away) · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Partners hold hands and bend on so, stretch on mi — or the reverse, as long as the class agrees.

A small knee bend on each so keeps the beat honest.

Pease Porridge Hot

English nursery rhyme · public domain · rhythm chant (ta and rest) · 2/4

Spoken. Add so–mi later if you wish.

Game: Partner clapping on the tas; open hands on the rest.

The rest after hot and cold is the whole point. Do not skip it.
