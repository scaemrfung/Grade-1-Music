Week 31 · Ostinato, ABA & Pitched Percussion · May 10–14

All Around the Buttercup and Higglety Pop

We read rhythm cards speaking only ta while thinking ti-ti and rests, warm up so–mi–la, orchestrate All Around the Buttercup with bordun, glockenspiel, and unpitched hits, and layer Higglety, Pigglety Pop with a “Please stop eating the mop!” ostinato.

3 classes a week × 30 min · Each class: devotional song 5 min + music 20 min

May 10–14

Full week: every Grade 1 class gets all 3 classes.

This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Day 1 prepares, Day 2 practises, Day 3 transfers and checks. Weeks 1–19 follow the GAMEPLAN lesson order, so their blocks vary in length but always add up to 20. Later months use the same approach as each month is rewritten. Weeks 5 on are planned for a homeroom (carpet circle or beside desks, kit carried in). Weeks 1–4 are written for the Music room (floor spots, instruments set out, room to move) and carry a short homeroom note. Pitches come from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and each class has a short “Bring” list.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Hello Song, so–mi–la

Greet on so–mi–la.

Teach

Rhythm cards · SML warm-up · Buttercup instruments (book pp. 92–93)

Rhythm-card challenge: speak “ta” on quarters only; internalize eighths and rests; track left to right. Echo so–mi–la on the ladder. All Around the Buttercup by title and page only — review song; prepare BX/BM/CBB = pat and AG/SG = snap, then transfer; clap (then unpitched) on “one, two, three” and “just choose me!”; add the circle game and repeat AB. Homeroom: one bordun, one glockenspiel, two unpitched.

Higglety, Pigglety Pop (book p. 93)

Higglety, Pigglety Pop by title and page only — echo text on a dotted-quarter feel while patting; discuss funny vocabulary; class speaks poem alone; learn the ostinato “Please stop eating the mop!”; poem over ostinato in two groups; invent a short intro/coda (animal sounds or poem fragments); perform. Homeroom: seated two groups facing each other.

Buttercup + Higglety share + 10-second checks

Run Buttercup once with instruments, then Higglety with ostinato. Rotating check, group C (about 7 children): while Higglety runs, give each child a 10-second solo — speak the ostinato four times or pat the Buttercup bordun pulse. Mark Y or N.

Story suggestion

The Tiny Seed · Eric Carle

A seed’s journey to a flower matches buttercups, spring word chains, and gentle AB games.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (StoryTime at Awnie's House on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Day 1, before Buttercup instruments (about 5 min).

Before reading

  • “A tiny seed can become a flower. Show a seed in your fist, then open to a blossom.”
  • Soft so–mi on “grow.”

During reading

  • Fist closed on windy pages; open blossom on flower pages.
  • Freeze as a flower on the last page.

After reading

Name one spring flower for a word chain.

Quick check: “Did the seed stay tiny or grow?”

Adapt: Hand blossom only.

Extend: Speak a four-flower chain twice as a B.

Activity · Read-aloud · The Tiny Seed

Use the book guide. Seed to flower.

Close

Speak the mop ostinato once, then walk.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

See-Saw

Traditional · public domain (also sung as Saw, Saw, Saw Away) · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Partners hold hands and bend on so, stretch on mi — or the reverse, as long as the class agrees.

A small knee bend on each so keeps the beat honest.

Pease Porridge Hot

English nursery rhyme · public domain · rhythm chant (ta and rest) · 2/4

Spoken. Add so–mi later if you wish.

Game: Partner clapping on the tas; open hands on the rest.

The rest after hot and cold is the whole point. Do not skip it.

Practice studio

Rhythm pad

Tap a box to cycle ta, ti-ti, rest. Play to hear eight beats.

So–mi–la piano

G is so, E is mi, A is la. Tap to hear. Grade 1 lives on the first three.

Name the sound

Play a mystery sound, then tap the name.
