Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Class 1 introduces, Class 2 practises, Class 3 applies and checks. Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Flash cards

Three cards: all tas; ta ti-ti ta ta; ta ta sh ta. See, wait, clap. No talking between.

Teach

Eyes, then hands

We read first in our heads. Then we clap. This stops blurting.

Play it on sticks

Same pattern, new timbre. Quiet sticks.

Error hunt

Teacher claps a written pattern with one mistake. Students catch it. Kind voices: “Beat 3 was a rest on the paper.”

Link to a song

Use a pattern as an ostinato under Rain, Rain — teacher sings, a small group plays the pattern twice.

Story suggestion

The Jazz Fly · Matthew Gollub (illus. Karen Hanke)

Scat and drum patterns invite echoing and building short rhythmic (four-beat) ideas.

Buy on Amazon · Listen: author’s full audio (Matthew Gollub (author) on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Brain break before Write, trade, play (about 7 min). The author’s audio has music and sound.

Before reading

  • “The fly is lost and asks animals for directions. Listen for the sounds they answer with.”
  • Put four beat boxes on the board.

During reading

  • Turn each animal answer into a four-beat pattern and clap it: frog “rib-bit rib-bit” (ti-ti ti-ti), hog “oink, oink” (ta ta), donkey “hee-haw,” dog “ruff.”
  • Write one pattern in the boxes, e.g. “rib-bit, oink, rib-bit, sh” = ti-ti ta ti-ti rest. Read it, clap it, then play it on sticks.
  • When the Queen Bee demands a new beat, stop and ask: “How will the fly make one?”
  • When the fly mixes the animal sounds into his drumming, the class performs two of their patterns back to back.

After reading

Write, trade, play: each child writes four beats using animal words (at least two kinds: ta, ti-ti, rest). Partner plays it.

Quick check: “How many beats are in our pattern? Which beat is the rest?”

Adapt: Provide animal picture cards with the rhythm printed underneath. English learners can say the animal sounds even without the words.

Extend: Layer two patterns at once, half the class on each, like the fly’s band.

Activity · Write, trade, play

Each child writes four beats using at least two kinds (ta / ti-ti / sh). Trade with a partner. Play your partner’s.

Close

Perform two student patterns as a class. Stamp if reading is becoming independent.

Materials

  • Flash patterns
  • Rhythm sticks or laps
  • Studio on this site

Teacher notes

  • Walk the room and rewrite backwards letters or eight tas in one box.
  • Ostinato groups should be three or four children, not the whole loud class.

Watch-outs

  • Patterns longer than four. Four is the job.

Challenge

Play your pattern twice without a hole in the middle. That is an ostinato.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Rain, Rain, Go Away

English nursery rhyme · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Game: Finger rain on the beat. Freeze on the rests.

The classic so–mi reading song. Sign so (higher) and mi (lower) while you sing.

Pease Porridge Hot

English nursery rhyme · public domain · rhythm chant (ta and rest) · 2/4

Spoken. Add so–mi later if you wish.

Game: Partner clapping on the tas; open hands on the rest.

The rest after hot and cold is the whole point. Do not skip it.
