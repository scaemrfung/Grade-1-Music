This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Class 1 introduces, Class 2 practises, Class 3 applies and checks. Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Class 1 · Introduce

Bring: 1–2 xylophones + mallets

  1. 5 minDevotional song — Your opening song. Not part of the music plan below.
  2. 4 minPitch bank — Sing so, mi, la as you point. Then tap them on the xylophone (G, E, A) or on the site’s Music Studio piano on the classroom screen.
  3. 6 minRead-aloud · Mole Music — Hook, per the book guide.
  4. 5 minRecipe — Four beats of so, mi, maybe la. Start and end on so or mi.
  5. 5 minVoice first — Hum it before you write it. Model two tunes together.

  • 🌈Xylophone & bellsPitch bank: only so, mi and la bars are on, with hand signs.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Pitch bank

Class sings so, mi, la as you point. Then they tap those three on the studio piano.

Teach

Recipe

Four beats. Use so, mi, and maybe la. Start and end on so or mi so it feels finished.

Voice first

If they write letters they cannot sing, it is not done. Hum it. Then write so/mi/la under four beats.

Add rhythm they know

Each beat is ta (one pitch) or ti-ti (two pitches). Rest is allowed.

Share with a partner, not always the whole class

Grade 1 composition is quiet and small. A few volunteers can share out.

Story suggestion

Mole Music · David McPhail

Mole practices a small violin melody that grows—tiny tune becomes meaningful music.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (Story Time with Charles on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Hook for Teach (about 6 min).

Before reading

  • “Mole’s first try is not beautiful. Listen for how he gets better.”
  • “Today you’ll make a tiny tune, like Mole’s first song.”

During reading

  • When Mole first draws the bow and it screeches, the class makes one tiny squeak sound (then rests).
  • “He could play one note, then two, then a scale”: sing so, then so–mi, then mi–so–la with signs.
  • When he learns a simple song, you sing a four-beat so–mi–la tune. Class echoes. This is “Mole’s song.”
  • Mole hums his music while he digs. Everyone hums the tune softly.

After reading

Write and hum: each child makes a four-beat tune with so, mi, and maybe la, starting and ending on so or mi. Teach it to a partner.

Quick check: “Where does your tune start and end? Can you hum it before you write it?”

Adapt: Offer a fill-in frame (so __ mi __) so beginners only pick two notes. Humming counts.

Extend: Collect three “Mole songs” and play them on the site piano for the class.

Activity · Write and hum

Compose. Try on piano. Teach your partner. Two volunteers share.

Close

Collect tunes. We may reuse one in the concert unit. Stamp when papers have four beats of pitches.

Teacher notes

  • Do not require staff notation. Letter names or so-mi-la words are enough.

Watch-outs

Challenge

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Star Light, Star Bright

Traditional · public domain · so–mi · 2/4

so = G

Quiet ending. Good concert voice practice.

Bounce High, Bounce Low

Traditional singing game · public domain · so–mi–la · 2/4

so = G, la = A, mi = E

Game: Soft ball or beanbag. Underhand only. Swap in a class name for Shiloh.

La is the new neighbour above so. Sign it higher, sing it lightly.
