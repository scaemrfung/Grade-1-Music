This week in 3 classes

Each class is about 30 minutes: your devotional song (5 min), then 20 minutes of music from this page, with a few minutes for transitions. Class 1 introduces, Class 2 practises, Class 3 applies and checks. Planned for a homeroom: carpet circle or beside desks, pitches from your voice or the Music Studio on the screen, and a short “Bring” list for each class.

Your jobs today

Warm-up · Seat beat, then stand

Eight pats sitting. Stand. Eight steps in place. Sit. Repeat. Check shoes and space.

Teach

Walk the drum

Teacher plays even beats. Students walk the room on those beats. Not a race. Soft knees. Own space.

Freeze is part of music

Drum stops, feet stop. Practice five times. Celebrate still statues.

The song walks too

Sing a known song while walking the beat. If they skip to the words, go back to drum only.

Directions

Walk forward. Walk in a circle. Walk back to your spot by the last phrase. Give the “home” cue early.

Story suggestion

Giraffes Can't Dance · Giles Andreae (illus. Guy Parker-Rees)

Gerald finds music he can move to—ideal for walking/dancing the beat with control.

Buy on Amazon · Watch read-aloud (KidTimeStoryTime on YouTube)

How to use this book

When: Brain break right before Walk and freeze (about 7 min). Clear the floor space first.

Before reading

  • “Gerald thinks he can’t dance. Listen for what finally helps him move.”
  • “Our feet follow the drum, not the words.”

During reading

  • At the Jungle Dance, give each animal its own drum groove. The class moves in place on the beat: warthogs waltzing (sway), rhinos rock ’n’ rolling, lions’ tango, chimps’ cha-cha-cha, baboons’ Scottish reel. Freeze at each page turn.
  • When the animals laugh at Gerald, stop the drum. Everyone freezes.
  • When the cricket plays his violin and says that sometimes when you’re different you just need a different song, play a slow, soft beat. Everyone sways gently.
  • For Gerald’s big dance, take bigger moves that stay on the beat.

After reading

Walk and freeze: use a slow “moon music” beat and a faster “jungle dance” beat. End by walking home to spots and sitting on the last beat.

Quick check: “Did your feet follow the drum or the words? What did you do when the drum stopped?”

Adapt: Chair dancers can move arms and heads only. Pairs can walk side by side.

Extend: Children pick a “different song” (drum, shaker, or piano on this site) and the class walks to its beat.

Activity · Walk and freeze

Three songs or drum grooves. Freeze on silence. End by walking home to spots and sitting on the last beat.

Close

Sitting: “Did your feet follow the drum or the words?” Stamp if freezes are clean.

Materials

  • Clear floor
  • Drum or song
  • Tape spots optional

Teacher notes

  • Gallop and skip wait until bodies are ready — many Grade 1s still need a walk.
  • If the room is tiny, walk in place or in a circle only.
  • A child who cannot walk the beat can pat on a chair and still be in.

Watch-outs

  • Turning it into tag.
  • A tempo they cannot walk. Slow is kind.

Challenge

Be a quiet leader: four students match your walk without a drum, only your feet.

Scores

Public-domain folk songs on a three-line so–mi–la staff. Play the line, then sing. These are our notations, not GAMEPLAN arrangements.

Walk and Stop

Traditional movement game · public domain · chant on the beat · 4/4

Teacher drum is the boss.

Game: Walk the pulse. Statue on stop. Later: hop, then stop; tiptoe, then stop.

Own space. Soft knees. This is pulse, not racing.

Engine, Engine Number Nine

Traditional playground chant · public domain · so–mi (or spoken on the beat) · 2/4

so = G

Game: Walk the beat like a train. Freeze on the rest.

Teach it spoken on the pulse first, then sung so–mi. Chicago can become a local town.
