Curriculum

Alberta, and GAMEPLAN

Alberta Elementary Music (Grade 1 column) is the legal program. GAMEPLAN Grade 1 is the commercial sequence many specialists teach from. This site honours both and reprints neither book.

Alberta Grade 1 (1989 POS, still widely used)

Rhythm

  • Music may move to a steady beat.
  • Music may move evenly or unevenly.
  • Music is made up of long sounds, short sounds and silences.
  • There are strong and weak beats in music.
  • Ta, ti-ti, and rest can show those sounds and silences.

Melody

  • Sounds may be high or low.
  • A melody can move up, down, or stay the same.
  • Grade 1 sings so–mi and then so–mi–la, with hand signs.

Expression

  • Music can be loud or quiet, fast or slow.
  • We start and stop together when a conductor’s hand asks us to.

Timbre

  • A speaking voice and a singing voice sound different.
  • Classroom instruments and found sounds have their own colour.

Form

  • Music can be the same or different.
  • Echo, call and response, AB, ABA, introduction, phrase and the repeat sign are forms Grade 1 can feel.

Skills

  • Sing in unison, alone and with the group.
  • Move to the beat: walk, pat, freeze, hop as the body is ready.
  • Play a steady beat on rhythm instruments.
  • Listen with care, including music of First Nations, Métis, Inuit, and many cultures.

GAMEPLAN Grade 1 conceptual year

Public objectives as listed by the publisher. This site follows the same month-by-month order, with original lesson text.

Rhythm

  • Demonstrate pulse (individual / group).
  • Identify pulse as ta.
  • Differentiate sound and silence; identify silence as rest.
  • Experience and identify fast / slow.
  • Identify ti-ti / ta.
  • Experience meter (2/4, 4/4, 6/8).

Melody

  • Differentiate high / low and experience up / down.
  • Build a folk, seasonal, multicultural, and patriotic repertoire.
  • Experience so–mi and so–mi–la melodies.
  • Identify SM / SML tones and hand signs.
  • Demonstrate contour.

Harmony

  • Develop proper mallet technique.
  • Demonstrate a simple bordun (chord).
  • Experience ostinato.

Form

  • Experience same / different.
  • Identify phrase, introduction, and the repeat sign.
  • Identify same / different as AB (and ABA).

Timbre

  • Identify unpitched percussion.
  • Differentiate vocal qualities: speak, sing, whisper, shout.
  • Demonstrate loud / quiet.
  • Classify unpitched as wood / metal / skin.
  • Identify four levels of body percussion: snap, clap, pat, stamp.
  • Classify pitched percussion as wood / metal.

Big I-can statements for the year

Fifteen kid-friendly goals, in teaching order. Each week also has three smaller I-can checks.

  1. I can use my whisper, talking, calling and singing voices.
  2. I can keep a steady pulse by myself and with my class.
  3. I can show high and low, up and down.
  4. I can freeze on a silent beat and show a rest.
  5. I can sort instruments into wood, metal and skin.
  6. I can tell when music is the same or different.
  7. I can read and clap ta and rest.
  8. I can move to fast and slow, loud and quiet music.
  9. I can show the shape of a melody.
  10. I can wait for an introduction and feel a phrase.
  11. I can play a mallet instrument with manners and a bounce.
  12. I can sing and sign so, mi and la.
  13. I can read and clap ti-ti and ta.
  14. I can show AB and ABA, march in 2, sway in 4 and swing in 6/8.
  15. I can make a four-beat piece and share it as a kind performer and audience.
