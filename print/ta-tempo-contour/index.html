Ta, Tempo & Contour

The pulse becomes ta, ta and rest rows, fast and slow, and melody shape.

Week 9 · Ta: The Pulse Has a Name

The heartbeat pulse now gets a name: ta. We say ta, clap ta, and keep it under Engine Engine and Yankee Doodle. We also meet a short introduction before the singing starts, and we try the pulse fast and slow.

Warm-up. Greet children on so–mi. Pat the pulse and say ta under the last phrase.

Engine Engine Number Nine (book pp. 26–27). Teach the chant from the book. Slide palms back and forth on the pulse, then transfer the pulse to a scraped drum. Echo by phrase. Try it slow, then a little faster — still even.

Read-aloud · Chicka Chicka Boom Boom. Use the book guide. Half pats ta; half chants.

Yankee Doodle (book p. 28). Teach or review from the book (title and page only here). Echo by phrase. Pat on phrases 1 and 3; clap on phrases 2 and 4. March in place on the pulse.

Activity. One child scrapes eight tas on the drum before the class speaks. Call that little wait the introduction: something short that happens at the beginning. Then the class comes in together.

Close. Whisper ta ta ta ta to the door.

Week 10 · Ta and Rest

We keep ta steady and add a silent beat we can see: rest. Squirrel songs and a train chart give us practice. We also meet a repeat sign so a pattern can be spoken twice.

Warm-up. Greet on so–mi; pat ta underneath.

Chase the Squirrel (book pp. 29–30). Teach the song from the book (title and page only). Pat the pulse while you sing. Two children at a carried-in xylophone play a gentle two-note pulse (chord bordun feel) under the singing; others wait in a short line. Homeroom: one or two instruments; Music room next year: four barred instruments in short lines.

Label the repeat sign (book p. 31). Point to the book’s repeat sign (or draw :|| ). Speak the row twice without stopping. Sing–speak–sing (ABA): sing Engine Engine, speak the row twice, sing again.

Read-aloud · This Jazz Man + 10-second checks. Use the book guide. Pat ta; open hands on rest. Rotating check, group B (about 7 children): while the game runs, give each child a 10-second solo: clap a four-beat row with one rest (open hands on the rest). Mark Y or N on the record sheet.

Activity. Sing from the book. Hop three times on “Hop old squirrel,” then freeze and wiggle on the nonsense words. Choose a quiet unpitched sound (shaker or woodblock) for the nonsense bits.

Close. Hop on ta to the door; freeze on rest.

Week 11 · Fast and Slow

The pulse can go fast or slow and still stay steady. We walk and freeze in Walk and Stop, wait through a whole musical sentence, pass a woodblock on the pulse, and share what we are thankful for.

Warm-up. Greet on so–mi. Pat slow ta, then a little faster.

Walk and Stop (book p. 32). Teach the song from the book (also scored on this site). Walk on the pulse while singing; freeze on “stop.” Change how long you wait. Freeze in a high or low statue that matches the end of the phrase.

Read-aloud · We’re Going on a Bear Hunt. Use the book guide. Slow pats, then faster for the run home.

Gobble Gobble Gobble (book p. 34). Teach the song from the book (title and page only). Show high and low turkey pictures; sing high or low (so–mi feel) as you point. Add the circle game: one child walks the pulse around the outside and taps a head on the last word.

Activity. A child picks slow or fast for the next Walk and Stop. Class matches.

Close. Slow walk; freeze at the door.

Week 12 · Melody Shape: Contour

A melody has a shape: up, down, or the same. We draw it in the air, build ta and rest with sticks, keep the pulse in Hello Everybody, and move with Jim Along Josie.

Warm-up. Sing while patting ta. Invite leaders to show a new way to keep the pulse (tap head, march in place, pound soft fists). Pass the lead around the circle.

Body notation (book p. 35). Four children stand as beats: one child alone = ta; two children side by side = two sounds on one beat (feel only). Rearrange and clap the row. Add one child with hands on shoulders for rest.

Read-aloud · Little Melba and Her Big Trombone. Use the book guide. Trace trombone lines.

Jim Along, Josie (book p. 37). Teach from the book (also scored on this site). Walk the pulse on the first part; add the face-the-centre claps and turn. Homeroom: circle beside desks or on the carpet.

Activity. “What things in your house keep a beat?”

Close. New pulse leader walks the class out on ta.
