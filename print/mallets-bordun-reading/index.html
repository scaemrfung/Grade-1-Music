Unit 5 · Print pack

So–Mi, Notes & Reading

Label so–mi and hand signs, so–mi on bars and staff, label quarter and eighth notes, winter singing games, rhythm cards and The Kangaroo.

Week 16 · Label So–Mi and New-Year Pulse

A new-year poem keeps the pulse, gift words become a ta/ti-ti chain, Lucky Me! adds a chord bordun feel, and so and mi finally get their names with hand signs on Teddy Bear.

Warm-up. Greet on so–mi. Pat a quiet ta under the last phrase.

Ask the class. “What was one favourite gift or winter fun?” One or two answers only.

Lucky Me! (book pp. 48–49). Teach from the book (title and page only). Echo melody by two-measure phrases. Pat a simple chord-bordun feel, then march while singing (sticks rest on shoulders). Alternate song and the word chain (same/different). Homeroom: one carried-in barred instrument for the teacher bordun if available; Music room next year: full BX/BM.

Labeling SM (book p. 49). After months of singing so–mi songs, name So and Mi and show Curwen hand signs (book sequence: high/low taps, then names and signs). Combine so and mi in four-beat patterns with both hands; so near mouth level, mi below the chin. Ask: “What songs have we sung on so and mi?”

Activity. Change the ostinato; class follows your lead for one more poem pass.

Close. Whisper ta ta ta ta to the door.

Week 17 · So–Mi Patterns and Shape Moves

We echo phrase-length body patterns and so–mi hand-sign patterns, build ta/ti-ti on the board, move with same/different shapes, play so–so–mi on a barred instrument for Valentine, and notate so–mi on a three-line staff.

Warm-up. Greet on so–mi. Short phrase-echo clap (book p. 38 idea).

Rhythmic Training (book p. 50). Review four-measure phrase echoes in 2/4 with one, two, or three body-percussion levels (stamp with alternating feet). Homeroom: stay beside desks.

Valentine / SSM on bars (book pp. 51–52). Review Teddy Bear with so–so–mi signs. Substitute “Valentine” for every so–so–mi. Demonstrate S–S–M (G–G–E) on a vertical glockenspiel (only G and E if you can). One child plays; then half play SSM on the barred instrument while half do Teddy actions. Homeroom kit: one carried-in glock or xylo; rotate short turns.

What Shall We Do When We All Go Out? (book p. 51) + 10-second checks. Teach from the book (title and page only). Echo by phrase. After the song, one outdoor or winter idea; class pantomimes while you keep a pulse on drum or piano. Homeroom: scattered beside desks. Rotating check, group A (about 7 children): while the game runs, give each child a 10-second solo: place so and mi on a three-line staff, then freeze. Mark Y or N on the record sheet.

Activity. Draw four tas; speak, speak-and-clap, clap. Change one ta to ti-ti and repeat. Prepares the paper assessment later.

Close. So–mi tip-toe to the door.

Week 18 · Label Notes and Winter Games

Stick rhythms get note heads and names, Old Gray Cats match rest and jog pulses, Baker’s Hat builds a so–mi melody, and we add winter singing games with kind loud/quiet and a bounce game that prepares la.

Warm-up. Greet on so–mi. One short ta/ti-ti board review.

Labeling Quarter Note and Eighth Note (book p. 53). Draw ta/ti-ti/rest in stick notation; students identify. Add note heads and name quarter and eighth notes — still called ta and ti-ti when speaking. Point to mixes while the class speaks. Homeroom: board or document camera.

The Old Gray Cats Are Sleeping (book pp. 53–54). Teach from the book (title and page only). Verses alternate sleepy cats (rest feel) and creeping/running mice (jogging pulse). Split class into cats and mice; pantomime safely (jog, do not catch). Match movement to the pulse you support.

Baker’s Hat (book pp. 54–55). Teach from the book (title and page only). Speak and clap the baker rhythm; build a so–mi melody with hat visuals so each “Baker’s hat” motive repeats. Pat the pulse; transfer to one barred instrument if available. Pass the hat; holder says what they would bake; class chants the reply four times as a contrasting section.

Activity. Use the book guide. Sticks become ta and ti-ti.

Close. Clap ta ti-ti ta rest to the door.

Week 19 · Rhythm Cards, Line Notes and Kangaroo

We read four-beat rhythm cards with note heads, learn line notes vs space notes with body shapes, review so–mi echoes, and play The Kangaroo with so–mi answers and gentle barred jumps.

Warm-up. Greet on so–mi.

Rhythm Cards (book p. 56). Show four-beat cards with note heads (title and page only — draw your own rows). Class speaks with ta and ti-ti and touches shoulders on each rest. Point along the beats at first. Homeroom: cards under the document camera or on the board.

Labeling Line and Space Notes (book pp. 56–57). Two-line staff: a line note sits on a line; a space note sits between lines. Body: fingertips to temples (line note) or one hand on head and one under chin (space note). Children draw line or space notes on the board in short turns.

The Kangaroo (book pp. 57–58). Teach from the book (title and page only). Class finds the repeated word “jump.” So–mi echoes in the third phrase. Game: one kangaroo jumps on each “jump,” points to four children for so–mi food answers; class echoes. Then kangaroo and soloists play any two C-pentatonic notes on each “jump” on the barred instrument. Homeroom: one instrument; short turns; soft jumps in place.

Activity. Use the book guide. Careful playing.

Close. Speak one card once, then walk.
