Introduction & Phrase

Phrase echo and holiday pulse, toy rhythms and marionettes, and high/low phrases with seasonal songs.

Week 13 · Phrase Echo and Holiday Pulse

We feel how long a musical phrase is by echoing four-measure patterns, sing a so–mi weekend chat, and keep a steady pulse in a December holiday song with a short introduction before the dance.

Warm-up. Greet on so–mi. Pat a quiet ta under the last phrase.

Weekend Fun (book pp. 38–39). Teach from the book (title and page only). Echo the melody by two-measure phrases. Sing while patting ta. Then a short so–mi chat: you ask who had weekend fun; a few children answer on so–mi. Keep chats short and kind.

Add colour (homeroom kit). Map patting to a gentle two-note pulse on one carried-in barred instrument (teacher or two careful players). Map the fist pulse to triangles or finger cymbals. Rotate short turns. Music room next year: BX/BM lines and more metals.

Dance for Hanukkah (book p. 40). Teach Part One body levels: stamp, pat, shoulders, head — eight each. Part Two: partner clap / own clap, then elbow swing. After an eight-beat introduction, the dance repeats. Call that wait the introduction (song doorway). Homeroom: partners beside desks; use a recording if you have Rhythmically Moving 6, or keep a steady drum pulse and count the sets.

Activity. “What is one fun thing you did?” One or two so–mi answers only.

Close. Whisper ta ta ta ta to the door.

Week 14 · Toys, Marionettes and Pulse

A toy theme carries the week: we move like marionettes to a famous ballet pulse, speak toy word-chains on unpitched instruments, play Jack-in-the-Box, and prepare so–mi with Teddy Bear.

Warm-up. Greet on so–mi. Clap a short phrase-echo warm-up (review book p. 38 idea).

Marionette Game (book p. 41). Talk about marionettes vs other puppets. Move one “hinge” at a time (wrist, elbow, knee) on a quarter-note pulse while you drum or play Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy. First theme: bend big hinges. Accents: slump and recover. Soft ending: tiny finger/wrist moves. Homeroom: stay in self-space beside desks; Music room next year: more scatter space.

Read-aloud · Clap Your Hands. Use the book guide. Moves on the pulse.

Jack-in-the-Box (book p. 43). Speak the poem from the book (title and page only) while tapping shoulders on the pulse. Press a “Jack,” mime winding, then a drum pop. That child becomes the next leader. Add ratchet for wind and drum for pop. Extension: 1, then 2, then 4 Jacks.

Activity. Move on ta; freeze when the drum stops.

Close. Tiny marionette steps to the door.

Week 15 · High-Low Phrases and Holiday Songs

Teddy Bear becomes a high-low chart, we learn La Piñata with body percussion, build song rhythms with sticks, and read holiday patterns while we sing seasonal songs.

Warm-up. Greet on so–mi. One short phrase-echo clap pattern (book p. 38 idea).

Teddy Bear with chart (book p. 44). Review Teddy Bear (on this site). Show a simple high/low chart for “Teddy bear” (two highs, one low). Sing “high, high, low” in place of those words. Class invents new body pairs (shoulders/knees, waist/toes). Perform sing / think / sing.

Popsicle Stick Game (book p. 45; see also p. 36). Pairs build rhythms from familiar titles: Teddy bear; Dale dale dale; Dashing through the snow; Up on the housetop. Use one stick = ta, two short sounds on one beat (beam), and a rest. Speak and clap. Homeroom: sticks on desks.

Oh What Fun! (book p. 46). Show four holiday rhythm patterns from the book chart (title and page only; draw simple ta / two-short / rest rows yourself). Speak with rhythm syllables you already use; review the repeat sign. Point to numbers while the class claps quietly (two-finger clap). Optional: clap along with an instrumental Jingle Bells while you change patterns.

Activity. Sing, think with actions, sing again.

Close. High-high-low tip-toe to the door.
