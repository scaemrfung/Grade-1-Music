Unit 1 · Print pack

Voice & Echo

Speaking voice, singing voice, echo games, and so–mi.

Week 1 · Hello, Music Class

Learn how music class works: we sing, we listen, we move, and we stop when the hand goes up.

Warm-up. Teacher sings “Hello, _____” on so–mi. The class echoes. Go around as many names as five minutes allow. If time is short, greet the whole class: “Hello, Grade One.”

How we do music. In this room we sing, we play, we move, and we listen. We take turns with our voices. We keep our bodies in our own space.

The quiet hand. When the teacher’s hand goes up, voices and instruments rest. Practice three times: talk, hand up, freeze. Smile when they freeze. Do not shout over them.

A hello song. Teach “Hello Everybody” (or “Hello, Hello”) by echo. Phrase one, class copies. Phrase two, class copies. Then the whole thing. Keep it high enough for children’s voices — not an adult low voice.

Sit like a singer. Feet on the floor, tall backs, hands in laps. “If your chair is a drum, we are not playing it yet.”

Activity. Teacher claps a 4-beat pattern. Class claps it back. Then teacher sings a 4-beat so–mi snippet on “loo.” Class sings it back. End with the hello song once more, standing.

Close. Ask: “What does the quiet hand mean?” Stamp the lesson when most of the class can freeze without a reminder.

Week 2 · Talking Voice, Singing Voice

Feel the difference between a speaking voice and a singing voice, and find a light singing tone.

Warm-up. Whisper “good morning,” speak it, call it (playground voice), then sing it on so–mi. Students copy each one. Name them: whisper, talk, call, sing.

Talking sits, singing floats. Say “hello” in a talking voice. Now sing “hello” on so–mi. Put a hand on your head for sing, on your chest for talk, so students have a body cue.

We do not shout songs. A shout hurts ears and does not stay in tune. If the class gets loud, drop to a whisper-sing and they will follow.

Match my pitch. Sing so on “loo.” Class matches. Then mi. Then so–mi. Smile and nod for close matches. Do not single out a child who is low — sing beside them later.

Song: Rain, Rain. “Rain, rain, go away” is so–mi. Echo by phrase. Add a gentle finger rain. Keep it light.

Activity. Teacher says or sings a line. Students show talk (hand on chest) or sing (hand on head). Mix them. End by singing Rain, Rain all the way through.

Close. Ask two students to demonstrate talk, then sing, on the word “hello.” Stamp when most hands sort correctly.

Week 3 · Echo Me

Copy short claps and short sung patterns. Echo is how Grade 1 learns almost every song.

Warm-up. Four-beat claps, class echoes. Start easy (ta ta ta ta). Add one rest. Keep a smile. If they rush, slow your model, do not say “you’re rushing.”

Echo means copy. I go. You go. Same speed, same number, same tune. We do not add extra claps.

Voice echo. Sing so–mi on “yoo.” Class echoes. Change the last note sometimes. They have to listen, not guess.

Engine, Engine. Chant “Engine, engine, number nine” in a speaking voice with a steady beat on laps. Then sing a so–mi version of the first line. Echo by two bars.

Alone and together. Once the class is strong, let one brave child be the leader for a clap echo. Sitting is fine. No one has to stand in front.

Activity. Circle. Teacher leads three echoes. Then three students each lead one clap pattern. Class copies. Finish with Engine, Engine all together.

Close. Ask: “Does an echo add extra sounds?” No. Stamp when echoes stay the same length as the model.

Week 4 · So and Mi

Two pitches: so (higher) and mi (lower). Hand signs and a tiny song.

Warm-up. Hands low: hum a low mi. Hands high: hum a higher so. Ride the elevator three times. No words yet.

So is higher, mi is lower. Show the Kodály signs. Mi: flat hand held level, palm down, at chest height. So: flat hand, palm facing your body, fingers pointing sideways, thumb on top, higher at chin or shoulder height. Students copy the signs without sound, then with “so” and “mi.”

I sing, you sign. Teacher sings so or mi. Students only show the hand sign. Then reverse: teacher signs, students sing.

See-Saw. “See-saw, up and down” sits on so–mi. Bounce a gentle knee bend on the beat. Echo, then all.

Do not stretch so too high. If so is a strain, drop the whole song a bit. Grade 1 should not look tight in the neck.

Activity. Sing See-Saw. Students show so and mi while they sing. Then a few students take turns being the “hand teacher” while the class sings.

Close. Silent hand signs: class sings what they see. Two patterns. Stamp when most signs match the sound.

Week 5 · Singing Games

A song can be a game. We sing, we move, we take turns, we keep the tune.

Warm-up. Seated bounce on the beat, then standing. Words: “Bounce high, bounce low, bounce the ball to Shiloh.” Swap in a class name. So–mi–la sneaks in; do not lecture it yet.

A singing game has a job. We sing the whole time. The game is not a recess shout. If the tune falls apart, freeze, sing once sitting, then play again.

Lucy Locket. Circle. One child walks the beat on the outside with a “pocket” (beanbag). At the end of the verse they drop it behind a friend. That friend walks next. Keep the singing going.

Kind turns. Everyone gets a turn over the year, not always today. We do not grab. We do not say “you missed.”

Still a singing voice. Excitement makes shouts. Remind: light voice, strong beat in the feet.

Activity. Run Lucy Locket or Bounce High for the middle of the block. Sit after two or three turns to reset the tune, then play again.

Close. Sit. Sing the song once with no game. That is how we remember it. Stamp if the tune survived the game.

Week 6 · We Can Sing Together

Unison: one tune, many voices. This is hand-in project 1 — our class song.

Warm-up. Breathe together. Hand in: sing “loo” on so. Hand out: rest. Four tries. This is conducting for six-year-olds.

Unison means one tune. Not faster, not louder than your neighbour. Match. If you cannot hear the person beside you, you are too loud.

Polish the class song. Words, then tune, then both. Fix one muddy phrase by echo. Do not restart the whole song ten times — fix the sticky bar.

Standing tall. Feet quiet, faces toward the conductor. Hands still unless the song has actions.

One sharing take. Sing it through as if someone is listening. Celebrate the stop as much as the start.

Activity. Two practice runs, one “sharing” run. Optional: record on a classroom device to send home or play later. No solos required.

Close. Name one thing the class did well (starts, soft voices, still feet). Stamp project 1 when the sharing run holds together.
