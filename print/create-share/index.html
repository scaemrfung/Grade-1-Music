SML Check, Ostinato & Singing Games

All About Success and SML assessment with Mexican Counting Song and Allison’s Camel, Bell Horses and Punchinella, Gallant Ship timbres and Jump Jim Joe, then Otto speech phrases, I Lost My Glove, Willum improv, and year-end game review. Week 36 still holds the unreworked June placeholder.

Week 32 · All About Success, SML Check and Mexican Counting Song

We read rhythm cards speaking ti-ti while thinking ta and rests, sing All About Success with so–mi–la echoes, prepare Which Melody Do You Hear?, take Written Assessment #4 on so–mi–la, and learn the Mexican Counting Song with Allison’s Camel.

Warm-up. Greet on so–mi–la.

Rhythm cards · All About Success · Which Melody (book p. 95). Rhythm cards (review idea from p. 56): speak “ti-ti” on eighths; internalize quarter notes and rests; point beat by beat left to right. All About Success by title and page only — substitute your school name or mascot; echo contour on the solfege ladder; add text one measure at a time; class sings, then teacher (then volunteers) improvise short so–mi–la fragments with hand signs for the class to echo. Which Melody Do You Hear?: teacher sings so–mi–la fragments with hand signs; class echoes and identifies the matching pattern on the chart — prepare for Written Assessment #4. Homeroom: ladder card and rhythm cards on the board.

Assessing So–Mi–La · Mexican Counting Song (book pp. 96–97). Written Assessment #4 (Appendix D idea) if you are assessing: students echo a so–mi–la fragment with hand signs, then circle the matching notation. Mexican Counting Song by title and page only — count to ten in Spanish with pronunciation support; speak A rhythmically then add melody and B (“La, la, la…”); partners show fingers on A and elbow-swing on B. Homeroom: partners standing beside desks; no need to copy book notation onto the board.

Allison’s Camel · share + 10-second checks (book p. 97). Allison’s Camel (traditional): seated circle copy-cat game — pat, push hands away, sway; repeat while decreasing humps from ten to one; coda “So Allison has a horse, of course!” Rotating check, group D (about 7 children): while the camel game runs, give each child a 10-second solo — echo one so–mi–la hand-sign pattern from All About Success. Mark Y or N.

Activity. Use the book guide. Brave sway.

Close. Sign so–mi–la once, then walk.

This week in 3 classes (devotional song 5 min + music 20 min each).

Week 33 · Bell Horses, Counting Ostinato and Punchinella

We build a body-percussion ostinato under the Mexican Counting Song, notate recent song rhythms with popsicle sticks, orchestrate Bell Horses with so on the middle line, and play Punchinella.

Warm-up. Greet on so–mi–la.

Mexican Counting ostinato · Popsicle Stick Game (book p. 98). Review Mexican Counting Song (p. 96). Class creates an ostinato on a body-percussion staff — start with two levels, then three; four levels are sophisticated for Grade 1. Popsicle Stick Game (review idea from p. 36): notate rhythms from recent songs by feel and title (Lucy Locket, Bounce High, One Two Three, Rain on the Green Grass, Sugar Sugar) — speak ti-ti and ta patterns; do not copy book notation onto the board. Homeroom: sticks on desks; ostinato levels shown with four board circles.

Bell Horses (book pp. 98–99). Bell Horses by title and page only — discover horses on the middle line as so; sing contour with solfege and hand signs; echo text with melody while pointing to horses; partners one behind the other, hands joined. A: bounce knees in place while singing; B: internalize the song while moving through space. Orchestration from movement: bounce = barred / sleigh bells; clap on “Bell, One, two”; optional coconut shells or wood blocks on the B pulse. Homeroom: one bordun player; partners in an aisle.

Punchinella · share + 10-second checks (book p. 100). Punchinella (traditional): circle with one student in the centre. Four verses — monkey eyes; pulse-point while Punchinella improvises; class copies; march and choose a new Punchinella. Rotating check, group A (about 7 children): 10-second solo — speak or clap one popsicle-stick rhythm from Day 1. Mark Y or N.

Activity. Use the book guide. Quiet practise.

Close. Bounce four soft knees, then walk.

Week 34 · The Gallant Ship, Rhythm Ostinatos and Jump Jim Joe

We clap quarters and pat eighths on rhythm cards, play an Amos Moses ostinato listening game, orchestrate The Gallant Ship with pitched and unpitched timbres in AB, notate so–mi–la, and play Jump Jim Joe.

Warm-up. Greet on so–mi–la.

Rhythm cards · Amos Moses game (book p. 101). Rhythm cards: clap quarters, pat (alternating hands) eighths; speak ta and ti-ti first, then body percussion only (think the syllables). Choose two cards; each becomes an ostinato when the teacher points — not both at once. Add Amos Moses (Rhythmically Moving 8) if you have the recording. Homeroom: cards on the board; seated pats and claps.

The Gallant Ship (book pp. 101–102). The Gallant Ship by title and page only — echo by phrase; prepare orchestration with body percussion (pat = barred, clap = hand drum, shake fists = maracas); perform with teacher bass xylophone if available; transfer to instruments and talk about different timbres. Introduce B as a teacher/student conversation (“She sank?” / “She sank!”). Form: AB repeated; students rotate to the next instrument during each B. Homeroom: one barred instrument plus maracas and a hand drum in a short rotation line.

Notating SML · Jump Jim Joe · 10-second checks (book p. 103). Notating so–mi–la (review idea from p. 90): echo short fragments (Lucy Locket or Bell Horses), then place disks on three-line staffs. Jump Jim Joe (traditional favourite): partners with hands joined — jump; shake/nod/tap; turn; choose a new partner. Demonstrate with one student; grow the group as partners multiply. Rotating check, group B (about 7 children): 10-second solo — notate or point one so–mi–la fragment. Mark Y or N.

Activity. Use the book guide. Different timbres.

Close. Clap ta, pat ti-ti once, then walk.

Week 35 · Otto Goes to the Beach, I Lost My Glove and Year-End Games

We speak a rhythm-card ostinato, build Poor Otto / Lucky Otto speech phrases from Todd Parr’s book, play I Lost My Glove, improvise on Willum with unpitched instruments, and choose favourite singing games before summer.

Warm-up. Greet on so–mi–la.

Rhythm-card ostinato · Otto Goes to the Beach (book pp. 104–105). Rhythm cards: one student chooses a card as an ostinato for half the class while the others read remaining cards. Otto Goes to the Beach (Todd Parr): read and discuss patience/kindness; discover Poor = ta, Otto = ti-ti; eight students fill board boxes with ta or ti-ti; class speaks “Poor / Otto” phrases; reread with spoken inserts; last time swap “Lucky” for “Poor.” Speak expressively. Homeroom: board boxes; book on the doc cam if available.

I Lost My Glove (book p. 105). I Lost My Glove (Trinidad) by title and page only — seated circle; find three phrases (1 and 2 the same); echo by phrase; chase game with a glove dropped on the final “drop!”; tagged runner to the mush pot (centre). Homeroom: tight circle beside desks; one glove.

Willum improv · game review · 10-second checks (book p. 106). Willum (review from April p. 87) by title and page only — class identifies the song; seven students improvise eight measures each on unpitched instruments after each verse while you accompany. Then class chooses favourite singing games to review before summer (book list includes Walk and Stop, Jim Along Josie, Old Grey Cats, Go All Around the Village, Punchinella, Jump Jim Joe — titles only). Book ends with The Finish Line reflection for the teacher. Rotating check, group C (about 7 children): 10-second solo — speak one Poor/Otto phrase or improvise four beats on an unpitched instrument. Mark Y or N.

Activity. Use the book guide. Poor / Lucky phrases.

Close. Speak “Lucky Otto” once, then walk.

Week 36 · Requests and Listening

We choose favourite songs and games, and listen with respect to music of First Nations, Métis and Inuit peoples.

Warm-up. Four voices.

Requests. Children vote for favourite songs and games from the year.

Read-aloud · We Belong to the Drum. Use the book guide.

Student choice. Children lead games.

Activity. Play a favourite game (Bee Bee, Bow Wow Wow, Punchinella).

Close. Line up.
