Songs to Share

Same and different, call and response, audience manners, and a class sharing.

Week 31 · Same and Different

Form we can feel: this part is the same, that part is different. Echo and verse live here.

Warm-up. Teacher claps a pattern. Second time is either identical or one beat different. Thumbs.

Phrases are musical sentences. Sing Rain, Rain phrase 1, phrase 2. Are they the same? Mostly yes with a small tail. Keep it honest.

A song with a chorus. If you have a simple verse/chorus (even a hello song with a repeated line), hold SAME on the repeat.

Call and response is a form. Teacher call, class response. That is not just a trick — it is a shape. Name it.

Map it with colour. Blue card each time the same phrase returns. Yellow for a new one.

Activity. Two students hold SAME and DIFFERENT. Class sings. Cards pop up. Switch helpers.

Close. Ask for a song that feels like echo (same) vs a song that tells two different ideas. Stamp if cards match the music.

Week 32 · Call and Response

A leader sings, the group answers. We practise being both.

Warm-up. Teacher: “Charlie over the ocean.” Class: echo. Then a child’s name in the call if the song allows. Keep it short.

Jobs. Leader waits after the call. Group does not steal the call. If you are not the leader, you are the response — that is a real job.

Teach the song. Charlie Over the Ocean (or Kye Kye Kule). Echo by phrase, then call-response in two groups, then with a student leader.

Matching pitch still matters. The response should sound like the call’s cousin, not a yell.

Kind leaders. The leader faces the group, uses a singing voice, and thanks the group after.

Activity. As many student calls as time allows. Sitting leaders welcome.

Close. Group response without a reminder. Stamp if waiting is clean.

Week 33 · Choose What We Will Share

Pick two songs and one played piece for the sharing concert. Rehearse the order.

Warm-up. Hum four known songs. Students raise hands for the ones they feel ready to share.

What makes a good share song. We know the words. We can start and stop. We still like it. Not the newest, hardest thing.

Vote with kindness. We may not pick everyone’s first choice. We pick what this class can do well together.

The played piece. Body percussion ostinato, a found-sound pattern, or a student four-beat echoed by all. One only.

Write the order. Song, played piece, song. Practise walking to standing spots between them. That transition is the concert.

Activity. Stand, piece 1, sit or stay, piece 2, piece 3, bow (a small nod). Time it. Aim under five minutes.

Close. Write the program on the board and leave it up. Stamp if the order survived one full run.

Week 34 · How to Be an Audience

When we are not the ones singing, we still have a job: still, kind, clap at the end.

Warm-up. Teacher models wiggling and talking. Students freeze it. Then model still and watching. Name the difference. Keep it funny, not shaming.

Audience rules. Voices off. Bodies on the chair. Eyes on the players. Clap when the piece is done, not in the rest in the middle. No phones — say it if families will attend.

Performer rules. Wait for the start. If you mess up, keep going. Smile at the end.

Rehearse both jobs. Half class performs piece 1, half is audience. Swap.

The clap. Two or three seconds of clapping, then rest. Not a stomp-clap chant unless you choose that as a class.

Activity. Full order. Real start and stop. Teacher only conducts. No extra teaching talk if you can help it.

Close. One compliment from audience to performers, one from performers to audience. Stamp if both jobs appeared.

Week 35 · Sharing Concert

Hand-in project 4. We share two songs and one played piece with our audience.

Warm-up. Shake out. Tall. One so–mi on loo. Quiet hand. That is the whole warmup — do not tire them.

This is the share, not a new lesson. No new songs. Remind order. Remind still feet. Remind smile.

If the audience is late. Sing the warmup song sitting. Do not lose the room.

You run it. Announcer (teacher or a child) names piece 1. Conduct. Nod. Piece 2. Piece 3. Thank the audience.

After. Sit. Two comments from you that are specific (“Your rest in the rhythm piece was real silence”).

Activity. Do the program. If no visitors, invite another class or perform for the principal on a walk-by. Stuffed animal audience is a real backup and still counts.

Close. Thank the musicians. Stamp project 4. Take a still photo if allowed.

Week 36 · What I Know About Music Now

Remember the year: beat, ta and ti-ti, so–mi–la, instruments, listening with respect. Celebrate.

Warm-up. Thirty seconds each of four favourite songs. No teaching. Just the joy.

Name the big ideas. Beat. Long and short. High and low. Kind listening. Unison. Let students finish your sentences.

Show what you know. Stations if you can: clap a four-beat card, sign so–mi, play a beat on a shaker, sit as audience for a 20-second listen.

A letter to next year’s class. One sentence: “In Grade 1 music you will…” Teacher can scribe for those who need it.

Thank you. Thank their voices. Thank their waiting. Thank their families if that is your culture.

Activity. Worksheet plus one last song standing. Optional: they teach a so–mi hello to a stuffed guest or a Grade 1 buddy from another class.

Close. Sing the very first hello song from week 1. Notice how it feels now. Stamp the year.
