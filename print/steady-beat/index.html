Steady Beat

Feel, walk, and play a steady beat. Strong beats and freeze games.

Week 7 · Feel the Beat

The beat is the steady heart of the song — even when the words are long or short.

Warm-up. Hand on chest. Feel your own heart if you can. Teacher plays a slow steady drum. Students pat that same speed on their legs. “The drum is the heart of the music.”

Beat does not stop for big words. Say “cat, cat, cat, cat” on the beat. Then “elephant, cat, elephant, cat.” The lap pats stay even. The words change. That even pat is the beat.

Show it three ways. Pat. Step in place. Quiet two-finger clap. Switch on your cue so they do not get stuck in one motion.

Song on top. Sing Rain, Rain or Hello while they pat. If pats follow the words instead of the beat, go back to drum only.

The beat can be slow or faster. Two tempos only today. Still even. Not “as fast as you can.”

Activity. Teacher drum leads. Students match. Fade the drum and see if their pats stay even. Bring the drum back if they rush.

Close. Ask a student to play four even beats on the drum. Class pats with them. Stamp if most pats stay even without the words pulling them.

Week 8 · Walk the Beat

Take the steady beat into the feet. Walk, freeze, walk again.

Warm-up. Eight pats sitting. Stand. Eight steps in place. Sit. Repeat. Check shoes and space.

Walk the drum. Teacher plays even beats. Students walk the room on those beats. Not a race. Soft knees. Own space.

Freeze is part of music. Drum stops, feet stop. Practice five times. Celebrate still statues.

The song walks too. Sing a known song while walking the beat. If they skip to the words, go back to drum only.

Directions. Walk forward. Walk in a circle. Walk back to your spot by the last phrase. Give the “home” cue early.

Activity. Three songs or drum grooves. Freeze on silence. End by walking home to spots and sitting on the last beat.

Close. Sitting: “Did your feet follow the drum or the words?” Stamp if freezes are clean.

Week 9 · Hands, Feet, and Freeze

Switch the beat between body parts. Control is the skill.

Warm-up. “Beat in your hands.” “Beat in your feet.” “Beat on your shoulders.” Change every eight beats. Freeze on “rest.”

The beat can move around the body. Same even heart, new place. This is body percussion without fancy names yet.

Shoulders and heads are gentle. No hitting. Two fingers. Model the volume you want.

Sing on top. See-Saw or Engine, Engine. Teacher points to hands or feet. Class keeps the song going while the beat location changes.

Freeze is a rest for the body. Connect to next unit’s silence, but do not name ta yet.

Activity. A student helper holds HANDS or FEET. Teacher still controls freeze. Rotate the helper.

Close. Name a child who changed on the first try. Stamp if the song survived the switches.

Week 10 · Strong Beats and Quiet Beats

Some beats feel heavier. We can show a strong beat without getting loud and wild.

Warm-up. Left is stronger, right is lighter. “STRONG, quiet, STRONG, quiet.” Keep it small so knees stay safe.

Strong is not shout. A strong beat is a little heavier, not angry. Model a tiny knee bend on 1.

Groups of 2. See-Saw. Bend on “See,” light on “saw.” Pat-clap. Pat is strong.

Groups of 4. Engine, Engine. Strong on “En-gine” downbeats you choose — keep it simple: beat 1 is stronger, 2 3 4 stay light.

Conducting. Teacher’s hand is bigger on the strong beat. Students’ bodies match.

Activity. Walk the beat. On each strong beat, a tiny freeze-frame, then keep going. Silly but controlled. Then sit and pat-clap a known song.

Close. Ask: “Is the strong beat the yelling beat?” No. Stamp if strong/weak is visible in their bodies.

Week 11 · Beat vs the Words

The beat stays even. The rhythm is the pattern of the words. They are not the same.

Warm-up. Half the class pats even beats. Half speaks “Engine, engine, number nine.” Swap.

Give them names. Beat = even heart. Rhythm = the way the words go. We will clap the words next week as ta and ti-ti. Today we only feel the difference.

Draw it. Four even dots on the board. Under them, write Engine engine number nine and swoop the syllables. Dots stay even. Syllables bunch.

Hands vs laps. Laps keep the beat. Hands tap the words. This is hard. Do it slowly. Many will mix them — that is the lesson, not a failure.

Song check. Sing Rain, Rain. Beat on laps. Then tap the words on shoulders. Ask which one stayed even.

Activity. Line A: beat. Line B: words. Conduct them in and out. Swap. End all together on beat only, to land on something they can succeed at.

Close. “Which one is even?” Beat. Stamp if they can point to it.

Week 12 · Beat Parade

Hand-in project 2: walk or play a steady beat, freeze on silence, stay safe.

Warm-up. Walk the beat with no talking. Drum only. Freeze. Sit.

What “done” looks like. Even feet or even instrument. Freeze on stop. Instruments stay quiet until the start hand.

If you have a shaker or sticks. Play the beat, not the words. Two-finger volume. Sticks are not swords.

The song is the street. Teacher sings. Parade moves. At the fermata or drum stop, statues.

Audience spots. Half parade, half watch. Watchers check: even? safe? freeze? Swap.

Activity. Two rehearsal parades, one sharing parade. Watchers give one kind comment: “I saw even feet.”

Close. Collect instruments carefully — that is still music class. Stamp project 2.
