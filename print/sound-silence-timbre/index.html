Sound, Silence & Timbre

Sound and silence with the Muffin Man, walking and jogging beats, whisper and shout voices, a steady two-note accompaniment on bars, and musical sentences felt in movement.

Week 5 · Sound and Silence

Music has sounds and silences. In The Muffin Man every muffin is a sound and an empty plate is a silent beat: we keep the pulse going inside. We meet spider songs, puppets, and our first gentle mallet turns.

Warm-up. This year: walk and pat the pulse to the drum, freeze when it stops, and sing the Hello Song on so–mi. Use this review each time the class has missed earlier weeks.

The Muffin Man (book p. 13). Sing the song in your own voice. Pat the pulse while you sing. A page on this site has the words.

Eency Weency Spider / Itsy Bitsy Spider (book p. 15). Finger play on the pulse: climb, wash down, sun, climb. Between verses, tap a body part. A page on this site has the words.

Moving like a spider (book p. 16). Spiders creep in place at their desks, or around the carpet, on the drum. Stop the drum and everyone freezes. When it starts again, they creep on, a little faster or slower.

Activity. Draw two rows of four plates. Put one muffin on each. Point to the plates one by one on the pulse and the class says “yum” for each. Use these eight yums between verses.

Close. Silent beats at the door: walk to the drum and freeze when it stops.

Week 6 · Silent Beats and Walking and Jogging Beats

We keep the pulse through silence with the Muffin Man, feel walking beats and jogging beats in Big Black Cats, and find up and down in Eency Weency Spider.

Warm-up. Greet children on so–mi.

Big Black Cats (book p. 17). Say the cat poem from the book. The class finds which lines match and which differ. Echo it phrase by phrase with a clap on the pulse.

Four woodblocks in the interlude (book p. 18). Four children play the pulse on woodblocks between verses. Pass the woodblocks around during the interlude so every child gets a turn over the three days.

Read-aloud · The Very Busy Spider (book p. 19). Use the book guide. Pat the pulse and say the spider’s repeating working line on the beat. When the animals interrupt, hold still for one beat.

Activity. Take one step on each beat for a walk, and two quick steps on a beat for a jog. Follow the poem’s words: some beats walk and some jog.

Close. Walk and jog to the door on the drum.

Week 7 · Loud and Quiet Voices, Beats and Bars

We use whisper and strong voices in Stirring My Brew, step walking and jogging beats on paper sheets, play Big Black Cats on woodblocks and glockenspiel, and hold a steady two-note accompaniment under Candy Store.

Warm-up. Greet children on so–mi.

Paper beats: stand on the walk and jog (book p. 20). Tape four sheets to the carpet or floor at the front, one for each beat. Children stand one to a sheet for a walking beat, and two to a sheet for a jogging beat. Use the rhythm of Big Black Cats.

Big Black Cats on instruments (book p. 21). Woodblocks play on lines 1, 2 and 4 of the poem and the glockenspiel (set to a pentatonic scale) plays on line 3. Pass the instruments around the circle during the interlude. Other children say the poem and clap and pat.

Candy Store: steady two-note accompaniment (book p. 22). Teach the song from the book. Two children play two bars on a pulse underneath while the class sings. Add a word-chain of candy words between verses and swap players.

Activity. Say the cat poem. Clap on walking beats and pat on jogging beats. Keep the silent beat if the poem has one.

Close. Walk and jog to the door.

Week 8 · Musical Sentences and the Mountain King

Songs and poems are made of musical sentences. We feel them in In the Hall of the Mountain King, count them in Little Jack Pumpkin Face, and play a steady two-note accompaniment under What Will You Be?

Warm-up. Greet children on so–mi.

In the Hall of the Mountain King (book p. 23). Play Grieg’s recording. Follow the leader: copy pulse actions that change at the end of each musical sentence. Start with both hands together, then alternate. Let the music speed up and the actions speed up with it.

What Will You Be? (book p. 24). Teach the song from the book. Two children play a steady two-note accompaniment on bars under the singing. A child whispers what they will be for Halloween, goes to the bars, and plays while the class sings. Rotate players each verse.

Pumpkin Face musical-sentence game + 10-second checks + 10-second checks. Each child leads one sentence of Little Jack Pumpkin Face with an action while the class copies. Give each child a short solo. Rotating check, group D (about 7 children): while the game runs, give each child a 10-second solo: lead one sentence of Little Jack Pumpkin Face with an action on the pulse. Mark Y or N on the record sheet.

Activity. Echo the Halloween rhyme with four actions, one for each line: tap the floor, pat knees, clap getting bigger, snap. Ask how many musical sentences the rhyme has. Four.

Close. Walk and freeze at the door.
