Instruments & Colour

Body percussion, classroom instruments, found sounds, loud and quiet, fast and slow.

Week 25 · Body Percussion

Clap, pat, stamp, snap — four sounds we already own. Transfer the beat and simple patterns onto the body.

Warm-up. Clap clap pat pat. Class copies. Snap if they can; many Grade 1s cannot snap — two-finger tap on the palm is a snap substitute.

Name the four. Clap, pat (laps), stamp (gentle), snap or tap. Volume rule: we should still hear the song.

Beat on the body. Sing Hello or Rain, Rain. Beat in pats. Then beat in quiet stamps.

Pattern on the body. ti-ti ta rest on clap-clap pat open. This links unit 3 to instruments next week.

Safety. Stamps are not jumps. Snaps are not at someone’s ear.

Activity. A small group repeats clap-pat-clap-rest while the class sings. Swap groups.

Close. Freeze in a body-percussion statue. Stamp if volume stayed kind.

Week 26 · Classroom Instruments with Care

Rhythm sticks, shakers, bells, drums. How we hold them, how we rest them, how we play a beat.

Warm-up. Instruments on the floor or on shoulders, not making sound. Practice pick up, play four beats, rest. That loop is the whole classroom management system.

One job today: the beat. Not the words. Even sounds with the drum or with me.

How each one speaks. Sticks tap. Shakers shake once per beat (not a sandstorm). Bells ding and wait. Drums are open palm, not fists, unless it is a proper hand drum technique you teach.

Quiet is a skill. The best players can play so the song is still the star.

Passing out and in. You pass out. They do not help themselves. Collect the same way. Build this ritual now.

Activity. If you have four types, four stations, two minutes each, beat only, then rest. A song underneath the whole time.

Close. Collect. Count. Thank the instruments. Stamp if rest position worked.

Week 27 · Name, Sound, Picture

Match an instrument’s name, its sound, and what it looks like. Alberta Grade 1 loves this game.

Warm-up. Hide one instrument. Play it unseen. Students guess the name. Reveal.

Three cards for each. Word, picture, sound. We connect all three. Start with drum, shaker, bell, sticks.

How it makes the sound. Hit, shake, scrape. Let them name the action.

High or low colour. Bell vs drum. Link to week 19 without a lecture.

Use the studio. The website can play drum, shaker, bell, stick if you have no kit today.

Activity. Stations: hear, then walk to the picture card. Or a paper match on the worksheet. Then the site quiz in pairs if devices exist — otherwise teacher-led on the projector.

Close. Four sounds, four names, fast. Stamp if most matches are right.

Week 28 · Found Sounds and New Ways

Music can come from pencils, paper, chairs (gently), and invented instruments. We explore with rules.

Warm-up. Each child has one paper. Explore: tap, wave, rip a tiny corner if allowed. Freeze. Which sound was your favourite?

Found sound has rules. We do not wreck school things. We do not use friends as drums. We try, we share, we put it back.

Same object, new way. A pencil can tap, scrape, or roll. That is “new ways” — Alberta language, kid-sized.

Build a tiny piece. Four beats of found sound as an ostinato under a known chant.

Invented instrument. Two objects together (bottle + pencil). Name it. Play a beat. Bow.

Activity. Pairs pick two found sounds, write a four-beat idea (icons), perform once.

Close. Reset the room better than we found it. That is still musicianship. Stamp if exploration stayed inside the rules.

Week 29 · Fast and Slow, Loud and Quiet

Tempo and dynamics. We change the music on purpose and still stay together.

Warm-up. Walk slow, walk faster, freeze. Then clap piano (quiet) and forte (loud) — use the kid words plus the music words once.

Two pairs. Fast/slow is speed. Loud/quiet is volume. Do not mix the cards at first.

The song stays the same. Rain, Rain slow. Rain, Rain faster. Same tune. Then quiet vs strong. Same tune.

Conductor tools. Small hand = quiet. Big hand = stronger. Slow scoop vs quick tick.

Piano and forte. Say the words once. They do not need to spell them. Quiet and loud remain on the cards.

Activity. A student helper shows a card. Class sings the known song that way. Rotate helpers.

Close. One child conducts quiet to loud on a long loo. Stamp if the class actually changes.

Week 30 · Listen with Respect

We listen to music of First Nations, Métis, and Inuit peoples, and music from many places, with care. We do not copy what is not ours to copy.

Warm-up. Still bodies, looking at the speaker or the player. One listen with eyes. Name what we will listen for: drum, voice, fiddle, or dance feet.

Respect first. Some songs are sacred or family-owned. In this class we listen. We do not pretend, costume, or make up “Indigenous-sounding” chants. If a local Nation or Métis artist has shared a song for schools, we credit them by name.

One focus listen. Play 45–90 seconds. Ask: drum or fiddle? Fast or slow? For dancing or for sitting? Accept kid words.

Métis fiddle and jig is one good door. If you use a jig, students may pat a beat or try a simple step only if you are teaching a school-appropriate dance, not a mockery. Movement is optional. Listening is the job.

Many cultures. If time, a second short piece from another place the class has a real link to (a classmate’s family, a book you read). Same respectful frame.

Activity. A listening map: dots for drum, line for voice or fiddle. Share one noticing with a partner. No performing of the recording.

Close. Credit the artists again. “Thank you for the listen.” Stamp if the room stayed in listening mode.
