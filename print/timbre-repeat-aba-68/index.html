Unit 8 · Print pack

Ostinato, ABA & Pitched Percussion

Hammer ABA and rain ostinato with Village, Sugar Sugar wood/metal and Tres Pajaritos, Caboose trains with Drip Drop, then Buttercup instruments, Higglety ostinato, and the p.94 She’ll be Comin’ / Caboose finish.

Week 28 · There’s Music in a Hammer, Rain and the Village

We warm up with phrase body percussion and so–mi–la, build There’s Music in a Hammer as ABA with mallets, skip and gallop to Handel’s Country Dance, create a Rain on the Green Grass ostinato, and play Go All Around the Village.

Warm-up. Greet on so–mi–la.

Rhythmic + melodic warm-ups · There’s Music in a Hammer (book pp. 83–84). Phrase body percussion (review idea from p. 38): patterns that fill four measures in 2/4, building from one body level toward four. Echo so–mi–la with hand signs and a solfege ladder that shows only S, M, and L. There’s Music in a Hammer by title and page only — echo with the ladder; build a four-beat hammer/nail speech pattern as a B; perform ABA with a chord bordun if you have one; optional meows as a coda. Homeroom: one barred instrument; waiting students pat the bordun on laps.

Country Dance · Rain on the Green Grass (book pp. 84–85). Skip vs gallop: demonstrate, name the difference (gallop leads with the same foot; skip alternates), practise with a hand drum. Listen to Handel’s Country Dance (Water Music); discover ABA; assign skip/gallop/skip; two groups move on A or B. Rain on the Green Grass (traditional): speak the rhyme; arrange four body-percussion circles as an ostinato (one circle per beat; start on one or two levels); student composers rearrange. Homeroom: skip/gallop in a desk aisle; ostinato circles on the board.

Go All Around the Village (book p. 85). Traditional three-verse singing game: circle with hands joined, one leader outside. Vs. 1 skip around; vs. 2 windows (joined hands up) while the leader weaves; vs. 3 class claps the pulse while the leader marches and stops behind a new leader. Cumulative — add a leader each repeat. Homeroom: smaller circle beside desks if space is tight.

Activity. Use the book guide. Quiet rain fingers.

Close. Pat four ostinato beats, then walk.

Week 29 · Sugar Sugar, Willum and Tres Pajaritos

We read rhythm cards with rests on the shoulders, rotate through Sugar Sugar on barred instruments (wood vs metal), invent Willum verses with leaders, sing Tres Pajaritos with contour and call/response, and review Go All Around the Village.

Warm-up. Greet on so–mi–la.

Rhythm cards · Sugar Sugar (book pp. 86–87). Warm-up: speak each rhythm card; touch shoulders on rests. Sugar Sugar by title and page only — clap/pat the companion rhythms; speak then think the poem; transfer to barred instruments in C pentatonic (hands together on clap measures, alternating on the pat measure). Rotate on a drum cue: speak, then play. Challenge: clap parts on metal, pat parts on wood. Homeroom: one or two barred instruments; waiting line pats on laps.

Willum (book p. 87). Traditional song by rote. Class invents seven chore verses (chopping wood, taking out trash, jumping…); list on the board; seven leaders pantomime while the class copies and sings. Homeroom: leaders at the front of the carpet.

Tres Pajaritos · Village review (book p. 88). Tres Pajaritos by title and page only — echo text; discuss meaning; trace contour while singing; call/response by measure; prepare a chord bordun and transfer if you have instruments; try boy/girl or solo/class turns. Optional one-mallet glockenspiel challenge on the bird calls. Review Go All Around the Village with new leaders. Homeroom: contour with a finger in the air; one bordun player.

Activity. Use the book guide. Quiet bird contour.

Close. Sign so–mi–la once, then walk.

Week 30 · Little Red Caboose and Drip, Drop

We form train lines with Little Red Caboose and a speech B, notate so–mi–la on a three-line staff, build Drip, Drop as ABA with flower word chains and mallets, and begin All Around the Buttercup.

Warm-up. Greet on so–mi–la.

Little Red Caboose (book pp. 89–90). Little Red Caboose by title and page only — echo while clapping the cowbell rhythm; two train lines follow engineers (trains may not cross); last student in each line plays a cowbell; class invents a four-beat speech ostinato (“All aboard!”) repeated four times as B; engineers rotate to the caboose. Homeroom: one line in the aisle; one cowbell.

Notating SML · Drip, Drop (book pp. 90–91). Review three-line staff so–mi–la (idea from p. 74). Echo short fragments (Acka Backa or Hammer) then notate. Prepare “la–so–mi–so” and extend into Drip, Drop by title and page only — flower word-chain B spoken twice; ABA with chord bordun and one-hand glockenspiel if available; rain stick free during A. Homeroom: paper staffs on clipboards; one barred instrument.

All Around the Buttercup (book p. 91). All Around the Buttercup by title and page only — echo melody; discuss buttercup and clever; circle game: leader travels inside on A; on B the leader points on the pulse and the last child becomes the new leader with a new locomotor move (cumulative followers). Homeroom: smaller circle; tip-toe and march only if space is tight.

Activity. Use the book guide. Train pulse.

Close. Chug four beats, then walk.

Week 31 · All Around the Buttercup and Higglety Pop

We read rhythm cards speaking only ta while thinking ti-ti and rests, warm up so–mi–la, orchestrate All Around the Buttercup with bordun, glockenspiel, and unpitched hits, and layer Higglety, Pigglety Pop with a “Please stop eating the mop!” ostinato, then finish April with She’ll be Comin’ instruments and a Caboose body-percussion ostinato (p. 94).

Warm-up. Greet on so–mi–la.

Rhythm cards · SML warm-up · Buttercup instruments (book pp. 92–93). Rhythm-card challenge: speak “ta” on quarters only; internalize eighths and rests; track left to right. Echo so–mi–la on the ladder. All Around the Buttercup by title and page only — review song; prepare BX/BM/CBB = pat and AG/SG = snap, then transfer; clap (then unpitched) on “one, two, three” and “just choose me!”; add the circle game and repeat AB. Homeroom: one bordun, one glockenspiel, two unpitched.

Higglety, Pigglety Pop (book p. 93). Higglety, Pigglety Pop by title and page only — echo text on a dotted-quarter feel while patting; discuss funny vocabulary; class speaks poem alone; learn the ostinato “Please stop eating the mop!”; poem over ostinato in two groups; invent a short intro/coda (animal sounds or poem fragments); perform. Homeroom: seated two groups facing each other.

She’ll be Comin’ · Caboose ostinato · 10-second checks (book p. 94). p.94 April finish (from the May–June scan): She’ll be Comin’ Round the Mountain (see p. 67) — review song and actions; seven students play unpitched instruments on the sound effects (Toot=cowbell, Whoa=hand drum, Hi babe=tambourine, Chop=wood block, Yum=maracas, Whistle=slide whistle, Snore=ratchet). Little Red Caboose (see p. 89) — class creates a body-percussion ostinato on a staff (start with two levels, then three). Rotating check, group C (about 7 children): 10-second solo — speak the mop ostinato four times or play one Caboose ostinato pattern. Mark Y or N.

Activity. Use the book guide. Seed to flower.

Close. Speak the mop ostinato once, then walk.
