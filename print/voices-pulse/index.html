Unit 1 · Print pack

Voices & Pulse

Music-room routines and hello songs, pulse in the body and as a group, high/low and up/down, and first mallet manners.

Week 1 · Welcome to the Music Room

We learn how our Music room works: where we sit on the floor, our three rules, the quiet hand, and how to move to a steady pulse. We meet the hello song, our first instruments, and a freeze game.

Warm-up. Greet the class on so and mi at the door of the Music room. Sing the Hello Song for Grade One from the book’s master sheet, then ask “what’s your name?” on so–mi for the class to echo.

Floor spots and seating chart. In the Music room, children sit on the floor in three or four rows with the shortest at the front. Put a marker at each spot and fill in the seating chart as they settle. Rehearse walking in, sitting on a spot, and walking out.

Welcome Back to School (book p. 2). Teach the song from the book, patting the pulse on your knees. Then everyone pats and counts to eight together. (The song words are in the book and are not copied here.)

Move and Freeze + 10-second checks + 10-second checks. Everyone moves around the room to the drum: tiptoe, march, crawl low. Stop the drum to freeze. Then ask whether the drum was the same as before or different (faster, slower, higher, lower). Rotating check, group A (about 7 children): while the game runs, give each child a 10-second solo: tiptoe four steps to the drum and freeze when it stops. Mark Y or N on the record sheet.

Activity. Sit tall and copy your simple moves to the drum: pat knees, clap, tap shoulders. Start with both hands together, eight beats for each move, and stop the drum to freeze.

Close. Drum a slow pulse. One row at a time walks to the door on the drum and freezes.

This week in 3 classes (devotional song 5 min + music 20 min each).

Week 2 · Pulse in the Body

Music has a steady pulse, like a heartbeat. We feel it by patting, walking and leading it, we change the speed, and we notice what stays the same and what is different.

Warm-up. Greet children on so–mi with a favourite ice-cream flavour for the name echo.

Clap, Clap, Clap Your Hands (book p. 4). Teach the song by ear. Verse 1 pats knees, verse 2 taps the head, verse 3 touches the floor. Ask the class to invent the move for verse 4.

Hickory, Dickory, Dock (book p. 5). Say the rhyme. Fingers climb up on “up” and slide down on “down.” One child keeps the pulse on the woodblock while the others tap it on their knees.

Monkey See and Monkey Do (book p. 6). Teach the song from the book. One “monkey” sits on a chair and leads a pulse action; the class copies. Change leaders every eight beats.

Activity. Walk to the drum and freeze when it stops. Play a faster drum, then a slower one, and ask for a new path each time: circle, zigzag, straight.

Close. Walk to the door on the drum and freeze.

Week 3 · Pulse as a Group

We keep one pulse together as a class: drawing it as hearts, playing it on woodblocks under a rhyme, and following a leader. We also move high and low and sing our names on so–mi.

Warm-up. Greet children on so–mi.

I Think Music’s Neat: draw the beat (book p. 7). Sing the song while everyone pats the pulse. As they pat, draw one heart for each beat on the board, eight to a line. Count the hearts together. The hearts are the steady beat; they have no other name yet.

Hickory, Dickory, Dock: tick-tock pulse (book p. 9). Split the class. Half whisper “tick-tock” on the pulse while the other half says the rhyme. Then swap. Swing arms like a pendulum.

Look at Me (book p. 8). Teach the so–mi singing game from the book. Freeze with a smile when the song ends.

Activity. Pass the bean bag around the circle. The holder sings “my name is ___” on so–mi and the class sings it back.

Close. Walk on the pulse and freeze at the door.

Week 4 · High, Low, Up and Down

Sounds can be high or low, and a melody can go up or down. We move up and down with our bodies and voices, then play it with the mallet on a xylophone or glockenspiel.

Warm-up. Greet children on so–mi. Offer a favourite colour for the name echo.

Playing up and down: mallet manners (book p. 10). Show a gentle mallet hold and a relaxed bounce. Two or three children at a time play up and down the bars while the class sings a slide. Everyone gets a turn over Days 1–3.

Hickory with instruments (book p. 11). One child plays the pulse on a woodblock. A pair plays the tune on so and mi bars on the xylophone. The glockenspiel slides up on “up the clock” and down on “ran down.” Tiptoe around the circle while players swap.

Saw, Saw, Saw Away + 10-second checks (book p. 12) + 10-second checks. Say the saw rhyme from the book with a sawing motion on each beat. Children invent a new verse. Give each child a short solo. Rotating check, group D (about 7 children): while the game runs, give each child a 10-second solo: keep eight even pats on their knees while the drum fades. Mark Y or N on the record sheet.

Activity. Pass the bean bag around the circle. The holder sings their name and a favourite colour on so–mi and the class echoes.

Close. Drum a slow pulse and freeze at the door.
