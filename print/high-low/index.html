High & Low

Melody goes up and down. So–mi–la hand signs and tiny tunes.

Week 19 · High and Low

Pitch can be high or low. We hear it, we show it with our bodies, and we sing it.

Warm-up. Teacher slides a siren up. Students stretch tall. Siren down. Students crouch. No words. Do it four times.

High is not loud. A mouse can be high and quiet. A drum can be low and soft. Separate pitch from volume right away.

Two notes only. Play a high G and a low C (or so and do). Students point up or down. Then they sing “high” and “low” on those pitches.

Xylophone or site piano. If you have a glockenspiel, let two children play high and low while the class shows with bodies. If not, use the studio on this website.

Song: Star Light. Star Light, Star Bright sits in a small range. Listen for the drop. Trace it in the air.

Activity. Teacher plays or sings high/low in mixed order. Students stand for high, sit for low. Then pairs take the site piano or a bell and quiz their partner.

Close. Ask: “If I sing softly, can it still be high?” Yes. Stamp if bodies match pitch, not volume.

Week 20 · Melody Goes Up and Down

A melody is a line. It can go up, down, or stay. We draw the line as we sing.

Warm-up. Teacher sings a slow so–mi–so. Students draw it in the air with one finger. Then a line that only goes up. Then only down.

Contour means the shape. We do not need note names to see the hill. Draw three maps on the board: hill, slide down, flat road.

Map a known song. See-Saw is a little up-down. Draw it together. Rain, Rain too.

Same or different shape. Sing two patterns. Thumbs up if the maps would look the same.

Stay means repeats. Ta ta on the same pitch is a flat line. That is still a melody.

Activity. Students listen three times to a short pattern and draw one contour line. Share with a neighbour: did your hills match?

Close. Hold up two student maps. Class sings them as best they can. Stamp if drawings show direction.

Week 21 · So–Mi Songs

Put high and low into real songs. So–mi is the Grade 1 singing home.

Warm-up. Silent signs, class sings. Then sung pattern, class signs. Keep it in so–mi only.

Three songs, one pair of pitches. Rain Rain, See-Saw, Star Light. They are family. Hum one and ask which song it might be.

Fix the sticky phrase. Do not run whole songs if phrase two is out of tune. Echo that phrase three times, then go back.

Alone, then group. A volunteer may sing a so–mi hello. The group answers. Sitting solos are allowed.

Add a beat. Laps on the beat while we sing. Two jobs again — easier now than week 11.

Activity. Sing all three songs with signs. Pick one to perform standing as if it were concert posture.

Close. Which song felt easiest? That is their home song for later weeks. Stamp if signs and sound agree.

Week 22 · Add La

La sits above so. Three pitches: so, mi, and la. Bounce High uses them.

Warm-up. Review so–mi. Then teacher sings so–la–so–mi. Students echo on “loo.”

La is a step above so. Show the sign higher than so. Students copy. Sing la lightly — it should not punch.

Bounce High. “Bounce high, bounce low, bounce the ball to Shiloh.” Teach by echo. Put a class name in. Keep the ball bounce on the beat if you use one.

Sign the new note only. First pass, only sign la when it happens. Then sign the whole song.

Do not pile on do yet. Three pitches is plenty. Hot Cross Buns can wait until they own la.

Activity. Bounce High with a soft ball or beanbag. The named child sings the last “Shiloh” as their own name on the same tune if they want. Optional.

Close. Silent signs: so–la–so–mi. Class sings. Stamp if la is in the neighbourhood of in-tune.

Week 23 · Hand Signs We Can Read

The teacher’s hands are a score. We read so–mi–la without words.

Warm-up. Silent sign strings of four. Students copy. Then add voice.

Hands are the music. You are not guessing. You are reading. Eyes on hands, then sound.

Four-beat reading. so so mi so. so la so mi. For a rest, hands stay still and open (a closed fist is the sign for do). Keep it slow.

Student conductor. A child signs a pattern they know. Class sings. Help them stand where everyone can see.

From signs to a song. Sign the first phrase of Rain, Rain. They should recognize it. Magic moment — name it.

Activity. Three student conductors. Class sings each pattern twice. Teacher sneaks in a known song phrase to be discovered.

Close. All eyes closed. Teacher sings so–mi–la–so. Students sign what they heard. Stamp if signs match.

Week 24 · A Little Melody

Make a tiny so–mi–la tune, four beats long, and sing it for a friend.

Warm-up. Class sings so, mi, la as you point. Then they tap those three on the studio piano.

Recipe. Four beats. Use so, mi, and maybe la. Start and end on so or mi so it feels finished.

Voice first. If they write letters they cannot sing, it is not done. Hum it. Then write so/mi/la under four beats.

Add rhythm they know. Each beat is ta (one pitch) or ti-ti (two pitches). Rest is allowed.

Share with a partner, not always the whole class. Grade 1 composition is quiet and small. A few volunteers can share out.

Activity. Compose. Try on piano. Teach your partner. Two volunteers share.

Close. Collect tunes. We may reuse one in the concert unit. Stamp when papers have four beats of pitches.
