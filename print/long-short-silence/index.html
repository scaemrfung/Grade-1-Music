Unit 3 · Print pack

Long, Short, Silence

Ta, ti-ti, and rest. Four-beat patterns a Grade 1 can clap and write with icons.

Week 13 · Long and Short

Sounds can be long or short. We hear it, we draw it, we clap it.

Warm-up. Slide a long “oooo” with arms opening. Then two short “ti ti” finger taps. Alternate.

Long takes more time. Hold “luuuu.” Short is “ti-ti.” Both can sit on a beat: one long fills a beat, two shorts share a beat. Say it in kid words: one long sound for one step, two quick sounds for one step.

Draw icons. A long macaroni. Two little dots. No stems required. Grade 1 can write these.

Names come next week. Today: long and short. Tomorrow: ta and ti-ti. Let the ears go first.

Song hunt. Sing Rain, Rain. Which words feel longer? “Rain” can be long. “Go a-way” has shorter bits. Do not over-analyse.

Activity. Pairs get four beats of space on the floor or paper. They lay one long or two shorts in each beat. Clap it. Switch with a neighbour.

Close. Class claps one pair’s pattern. Stamp if they can tell long from short by ear.

Week 14 · Ta

The long sound that fills one beat has a name: ta. We say it, clap it, and see it.

Warm-up. Say “ta ta ta ta” while stepping. Clap it. Whisper it. That is the whole warmup.

Ta is one sound on one beat. Point to four tas on the board. Touch each as you say it. Students write four tas in the air.

Icon they can draw. A simple stick, a heart, or a stemless note. Pick one and stay with it all year.

Find ta in a chant. “One, two, tie my shoe” — many tas. Pat and say ta instead of words, then put words back.

Read a line. Board: ta ta ta ta. Class reads. Change beat 4 later in the block when rest arrives in week 16 — not yet.

Activity. On the worksheet or mini whiteboards, four tas. Walk and read a friend’s. Play them on laps.

Close. One child points, class says ta. Stamp if the pointing matches the speaking.

Week 15 · Ti-ti

Two short sounds that share one beat: ti-ti.

Warm-up. Step ta. Jog in place ti-ti on the same beat speed — two little steps in the time of one. Hold a chair if jogging is too much; two finger taps instead.

Ti-ti shares the beat. One ta = one sandwich. Ti-ti = two halves. They take the same plate (the beat).

Draw two little notes together. Two sticks with a beam, or two dots with a line. Stay consistent with week 14’s icon style.

Pease Porridge Hot. Clap the words. Find the quick pairs. Say them as ti-ti. Keep the beat in the feet.

Read mixed lines. ta ta ti-ti ta. Point. If they say ti-ti too slowly, it becomes two tas — fix it kindly with a model.

Activity. Four beat boxes on paper. Fill each with ta or ti-ti. Clap. Trade papers.

Close. Ear quiz: teacher claps ta or ti-ti. Students hold one finger or two. Stamp if most are right.

Week 16 · Sh — the Rest

Silence is music too. A rest is one beat of no sound, with a still body.

Warm-up. Clap, open hands (silence), clap, open hands. The open hands are the rest. Faces stay ready — rest is not slump.

Rest is a beat with no sound. We still feel it. We do not skip it. We do not say “sh” loudly if that becomes a sound — a finger on lips or open palms works.

Write it. A squiggle, a Z, or the traditional rest. Pick one.

Read ta ta rest ta. The tricky part is staying still for the full beat. Count it in your head.

Put a rest in a known chant. Make beat 4 silent in Engine, Engine. Giggle once, then make the silence real.

Activity. Teacher claps a four-beat pattern with one rest. Students point to which beat was silent (1, 2, 3, or 4). Then they write their own with one rest and perform.

Close. Class statue for one full rest. Stamp if rests keep their full beat.

Week 17 · Four-Beat Patterns

Put ta, ti-ti, and rest together. Read, clap, play.

Warm-up. Three cards: all tas; ta ti-ti ta ta; ta ta sh ta. See, wait, clap. No talking between.

Eyes, then hands. We read first in our heads. Then we clap. This stops blurting.

Play it on sticks. Same pattern, new timbre. Quiet sticks.

Error hunt. Teacher claps a written pattern with one mistake. Students catch it. Kind voices: “Beat 3 was a rest on the paper.”

Link to a song. Use a pattern as an ostinato under Rain, Rain — teacher sings, a small group plays the pattern twice.

Activity. Each child writes four beats using at least two kinds (ta / ti-ti / sh). Trade with a partner. Play your partner’s.

Close. Perform two student patterns as a class. Stamp if reading is becoming independent.

Week 18 · My Four-Beat Pattern

Hand-in project 3: one four-beat pattern the class can echo.

Warm-up. Teacher claps four, class echoes. Student volunteer claps four, class echoes. Set the project mechanic.

The recipe. Four beats. At least two of: ta, ti-ti, rest. Must be echoable — not so wild the class cannot copy.

Practice alone. Write. Clap. Fix. If you cannot clap it, change it.

How we share. Child claps. Class echoes once. Kind applause (two claps). Next child. You will not get through 24 solos in 12 minutes — do a row today, another row if you have extra time, or split with week 19’s warmup.

If they freeze. They may clap all tas. That still counts. Pride matters more than complexity.

Activity. As many students as time allows. Teacher notes who still needs a turn next class. Collect papers.

Close. Name the musical bravery, not the fanciest pattern. Stamp project 3 as you go, or stamp the week when papers are in.
